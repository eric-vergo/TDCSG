# Towards finiteness of $GG_5(r)$ below $\sqrt{3+\varphi}$: the straight-cut limit at the critical points, rigidity away from the tight points, and a period-two obstruction there

Authors: Eric Vergo, Claude

**Status.** Let $r_c=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$. **(B), finiteness of $GG_5(r)$ for every $r<r_c$, is still open; this note does not prove it.** It carries out the referee's route of `referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` §5 (limit straight-cut system, its finiteness, matching from a level $k_0$, the bulk, assembly) as far as it goes and locates where it breaks. The note has been independently refereed; the report is [`referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-limit-system.md`](referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-limit-system.md), and §7 records its verdicts. In summary:

1. **Proved: the straight-cut limit of the fixed-word system is trivial** (§3).
   - In zoomed coordinates at the critical points, the induced system generated by the fixed finite word set of a certified window converges to a pseudogroup $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$ of rotations $X\mapsto\zeta^jX$ on half-planes and whole discs.
   - It is explicit (Tables 3.1, 4.1) except for finitely many linear germs at the tight points, and its orbits have at most $190$ points.
   - Every *non-linear* return to the level-$k$ core is a concatenation of at least $(\varphi^{3k}/2C-6)/T_W$ first returns to the window (Proposition 3.3).
   - So step (I) of the route, as formulated, produces a limit system whose finiteness is immediate but which does not see the dynamics that (R-core) is about. The object to study is the limit of the first-return maps, whose words have unbounded length.
2. **Proved modulo an exact finite computation, and certified closures: everything happens at the four tight points $\pm E,\pm\bar E$** (§4).
   - For the nine non-tight classes of critical points, every first return to the core is a linear germ from level $k=4$ on (core radius $C=3/10$). This holds for *all* points, not only lattice points (Theorem 4.2, resting on the exact computation of Table 4.1).
   - Hence (R-core) is equivalent, up to a factor $190$, to its restriction to the tight cores (Corollary 4.3).
   - The relative certificate with windows **only at the four tight points** exists for every window size tried (nine sizes; Table 4.2). The two largest are certified: they are VALID under the author's four-window copy of the earlier referee's interval-arithmetic checker, and the referee re-ran that checker on both (VALID). The other seven rows are computed, not certified.
   - So **for every $r<r_c$, $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if its orbits meet four small windows at $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ in uniformly boundedly many points** (Corollary 4.4). And (B) follows from two statements about the tight class alone, (R-bulk-t) and (R-core-t) (Theorem 6.1).
3. **Refuted (exact): literal $\varphi^{-3}$ matching at the tight points, at every level pair tested, including $3\to4$ and $4\to5$** (§5.2).
   - On the fixed sample $S_E$ (811 tight core points), the first-return sets agree between consecutive levels for 0, 14 and 14 points at $1\to2$, $2\to3$, $3\to4$. On the 84 tight points of $S_{21}$ the counts are 0, 0 and 0.
   - This holds with or without lune points.
4. **Conjecture with exact evidence: the renormalization at the tight points has period two, with unit $\varphi^{-6}$** (§5.3).
   - On the fixed sample $S_E$, agreement two levels apart is 185/811 ($1\to3$) and 518/811 ($2\to4$), against at most 14/811 one level apart.
   - Level 5 could be computed only for a selected family of 117 points of $S_E$. On that family agreement is already 116/117 at $1\to3$ (53/117 at $2\to4$, 117/117 at $3\to5$). The level-5 rows therefore show that this family stays fixed with period two; they do **not** show a rise of agreement with $k$.
   - On the tight points whose level-$k$ excursion contains no lune point at zoomed distance $\le3$, the first-return set at level $k+2$ equals that at level $k$ in all 273 cases tested, with none failing: 20 + 124 at $k=2$, 12 + 117 at $k=3$; no tight point is lune-free at $k=1$. With period one the corresponding test fails: 0 of 32 lune-free tight points of $S_{21}$ agree at $3\to4$.
   - The orbit of $E$ at $r_c$ is mapped into itself by $y\mapsto E+\varphi^{-6}(y-E)$ on all $3\,661$ tested points within distance 1 of $E$ (memberships found, hence exact). It is **not** mapped into itself by $y\mapsto E+\varphi^{-3}(y-E)$: about 22% of the images are exact non-members (referee: 200 of 200 sampled failures at $\rho=1$ have finite orbits not containing $E$).
   - The referee identified the mechanism of the parity (an exact identity, attributed and not yet independently checked; §5.3). On it the referee based the prediction "period one exactly when the level unit is totally positive", and confirmed period one for $n=12$ (§5.5).
5. **Evidence against a finite straight-cut first-return system** (§5.4).
   - The domains of the excursion chains from the tight cores are cut by lines that do not recur between levels. Most of these lines come from circles that pass near the excursion at macroscopic distance from every critical point, and their number grows with $k$.
   - Any limit system therefore has to be defined at the level of the return relation, not of chain domains. Nothing proved here reduces its finiteness to a line-arrangement closure.

So the route breaks at step (III) for the tight class, in the strong sense that the level-$k$ and level-$(k+1)$ systems do not match. Whether they match with period two from some $k_0$ is the open question. If they do, the data are consistent with $k_0\le3$ for lune-free lattice points. That is beyond the certified base (level $k\approx1.18$), and an induction would also need the return relation on the whole tight core, not only on lune-free lattice points.

Terms used below: *core*, *zoomed coordinate*, *first-return set*, *linearly related* (§2); *skeleton* (§4.1); *lune point* (§2.2).

## 1. Setting

As in `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` §1:
- $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$, $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ with basis $1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3$, $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and $\sigma\colon\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2$;
- $D_1,D_2$ are the closed disks of radius $r$ about $\mp1$; $a,b$ rotate by $\zeta^{-1}$ about $\mp1$ on their disks and are the identity elsewhere;
- $\varepsilon=\varphi^{-3}=2\varphi-3$, with $\sigma(\varepsilon)=-\varphi^3$ and $N(\varepsilon)=-1$;
- the levels are $r_k^2=3+\varphi-\varepsilon^k/10$, so $10r_k^2=30+10\varphi-\varepsilon^k$. They are non-norm radii (`sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` Lemma 2.2), so no lattice point lies on a circle of radius $r_k$ about a lattice point.

The 38 *critical points* are the lattice points of $\partial D_1\cup\partial D_2$ at $r_c$, in 10 classes under $\{\pm z,\pm\bar z\}$ (`sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Lemma 2.1, Table 2.1). The *tight class* is $\{\pm E,\pm\bar E\}$, $E=\zeta-\zeta^2=(0,1,-1,0)$: $\pm E$ are the ends of the critical chord of Theorem A and $\pm\bar E$ their mirror images, all four tight points of the critical words (`critical-radius-n5.md` §3). For a critical point $Q$ on $\partial D_p$ write $w=Q-p$; then $|w|^2=3+\varphi$, $w$ is one of the 20 elements $\pm\zeta^j\pi,\pm\zeta^j\bar\pi$, and
$$|\sigma w|^2=\sigma(3+\varphi)=4-\varphi,\qquad |\sigma w|=1.5434\quad\text{for every critical point.}\tag{1.1}$$
The arguments of the 20 elements $w$ are $\pm9.7323^\circ+36^\circ j$.

Refereed facts used: orbit and group monotonicity (`boundary-contact.md` Theorems 3.1, 3.4); bounded orbits give a finite group (`theorem1-converse.md` Proposition 4.2); the relative-certificate theorem and its corollaries (`sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3, Propositions 4.4, 4.5). Their proofs never use which critical points carry windows. In particular they never use where tangencies occur: an external tangency with $\partial D_S$ is a one-point, hence degenerate, component that needs no image wherever it lies, and the checkers skip such circles by an exact test independent of the windows. The referee re-read the proof line by line and confirmed this; no extra argument is needed for windows at a subset of the critical points.

## 2. Zoomed coordinates, cores and lunes

### 2.1 Definitions

Fix $C>0$ (below always $C=3/10$). The *level-$k$ core* is $B_k=\bigcup_Q\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$ over the 38 critical points; the *tight core* $B_k^{\rm t}$ is the union over the four tight points. A point $x\in\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$ has *zoomed coordinate* $X=\varepsilon^{-k}(x-Q)$. Since $\varepsilon$ is a unit, $x\in\Lambda$ if and only if $X\in\Lambda$; the zoomed internal coordinate is $\sigma X=\sigma(\varepsilon)^{-k}(\sigma x-\sigma Q)$.

The *first-return set* of a core point $x$ at level $k$ is $R_{B_k}(x)\cap B_k$, where $R_{B_k}(x)$ is the set of endpoints of chains at radius $r_k$ from $x$ whose intermediate points avoid $B_k$ (Definition 3.2 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). The orbit $O_{r_k}(x)\cap B_k$ is the connected component of $x$ in the graph of first returns, because the generating set is symmetric.

Two core points $x\in B(Q,\cdot)$, $y\in B(Q',\cdot)$ are *linearly related* if $y-Q'=\zeta^j(x-Q)$ for some $j$. This is an equivalence relation; a class has at most $38\cdot5=190$ points. A germ $g(z)=\zeta^jz+t$ with $g(Q)=Q'$ is *linear*; in zoomed coordinates it is $X\mapsto\zeta^jX$ (`sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Lemma 6.1).

### 2.2 The tight tests at every critical point (proved)

For a critical point $Q\in\partial D_p$ and a lattice point $y=Q+\varepsilon^kY$ put
$$\lambda_Q(Y)=10\cdot2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(Y\bar w\bigr)+1\in\mathbb Z[\varphi],\qquad w=Q-p .$$
Then exactly
$$10\bigl(|y-p|^2-r_k^2\bigr)=\varepsilon^k\lambda_Q(Y)+10\,\varepsilon^{2k}|Y|^2 .\tag{2.1}$$
This is (6.1) of `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md`, written in zoomed coordinates and valid at all 38 critical points. The *tangent half-plane test* is $\lambda_Q(Y)<0$; in the limit $k\to\infty$ the disk test becomes the half-plane
$$H_{Q,p}=\bigl\{X:\ 2\operatorname{Re}(X\bar w)<-\tfrac1{10}\bigr\},$$
whose boundary line lies at distance $1/(20r_c)=0.02327$ from $0$, on the side of $p$. A *lune point* is a lattice point where the disk test and the tangent half-plane test disagree.

**Lemma 2.1 (monotone lunes).** Let $Q\in\partial D_p$ be critical and $Y\in\Lambda$.
1. $Q+\varepsilon^kY$ is a lune point at level $k$ if and only if $-10\varepsilon^k|Y|^2<\lambda_Q(Y)<0$. The number $\lambda_Q(Y)$ does not depend on $k$. Hence the set of zoomed lune points decreases with $k$, and each $Y$ is a lune point for only finitely many $k$.
2. (Lune lemma in zoomed form.) If $10\,\varepsilon^k|Y|^2\bigl(20|\sigma w|\,|\sigma Y|+1\bigr)<1$, then $Q+\varepsilon^kY$ is not a lune point. With (1.1) the constant is $20|\sigma w|=30.87$ at every critical point.
3. Consequently, on $\{Y:|Y|\le R,\ |\sigma Y|\le M\}$ there is no lune point once $10\,\varepsilon^kR^2(30.87M+1)<1$.

*Proof.* (1) By (2.1), with $\varepsilon>0$. Equality cases do not occur: $\lambda\equiv1\pmod{10}$ is non-zero, and the left side of (2.1) is non-zero at a non-norm radius. (2) $\lambda\equiv1\pmod{10}$ gives $N(\lambda)\equiv1\pmod{10}$, so $|\lambda|\ge1/|\sigma\lambda|$, and $|\sigma\lambda|\le20|\sigma Y||\sigma w|+1$. If $|\lambda|>10\varepsilon^k|Y|^2$, item (1) excludes the lune. (3) follows. $\square$

Item 2 is `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` Lemma 6.1(2), extended from the tight pairs to all 38 critical points. It is the answer to the route's question whether "the same Diophantine argument is available for the other circles": **for every circle through a critical point, yes, with one uniform constant.** For circles that pass close to an excursion away from all critical points, no argument of this kind is available. Such *non-critical near misses* do occur (§5.4).

## 3. The straight-cut limit of the fixed-word system (proved)

Let $W$ be a window set for which a restricted closure exists. Let $\mathcal W$ be its finite word set, $G_W$ the finite set of germs, and $T_W=\max\{|\sigma t|\}$ over $G_W$ (Proposition 4.4 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). Proposition 3.1 uses windows at all 38 critical points (Table 5.1 there); Proposition 3.3 holds for any such $W$, including the four-window sets of Table 4.2.

**Proposition 3.1 (zoom dichotomy).** There is $k_1$ such that for $k\ge k_1$ and every germ $g\in G_W$ whose level-$k$ domain meets the core $\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$, exactly one of the following holds.
1. $g(Q)=Q'$ is critical. In zoomed coordinates $g$ is $X\mapsto\zeta^jX$. Its domain in the zoomed core is cut by:
   - the tests at $Q$ itself, those of the disk tight at $Q$, which tend to $H_{Q,p}$ or its complement;
   - for every prefix image $h(Q)$ that lies on $\partial W$, and for $g(Q)$ if it lies on $\partial W$, the window-avoidance (or endpoint) condition, which tends to a half-plane bounded by a line through $0$, the tangent of $\partial W$ there.

   All other tests are constant on the core.
2. $g(Q)$ is not critical, and $g(\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k))\cap B_k=\emptyset$.

Here "tends to" means the following. For $k\ge k_1$ the zoomed domain is exactly the set cut out by the listed tests, and their curved boundaries converge locally uniformly to the stated lines. The limit lives on 38 discs of radius $C$; regarding it as a system on whole planes is a convention.

*Proof.* Finitely many germs and prefixes. An intermediate prefix image $h(Q)$ of a first return to $W$ is not critical. Otherwise the points $h(x)$, $x$ in the core, would lie in the window centred at $h(Q)$ for large $k$, while they must avoid $W$. A non-critical lattice point satisfies $|h(Q)-p|\ne r_c$, so it is at a fixed positive distance from $\partial D_p(r_c)$ and from the critical set, while $r_c-r_k\to0$ and the core radius tends to $0$.
- If $h(Q)\notin\partial W$, it is also at a fixed positive distance from $\partial W$, and the avoidance condition is constant on the core.
- If $h(Q)\in\partial W$, which is possible because $\partial W$ consists of lattice-centred arcs of radius $r_c$ (Lemma 2.1(5) of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`), the condition cuts the core along a curve through $0$ with the stated tangent.

At $Q$ itself, (2.1) applies. In case 2, $g(Q)$ is at a fixed positive distance from the critical set. $\square$

The extra cuts through $0$ do not affect the linearity of the germs, so Proposition 3.2 is unaffected. Whether any $h(Q)$ actually lies on $\partial W$ is a finite check over the prefixes of $\mathcal W$; it was not carried out.

**The limit system $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$ (explicit data).** It acts on 38 copies of the plane, one per critical point, and is generated by the limits of the linear germs of case 1. These are of two kinds.
- *Single tight letters.* A letter tight at $Q\in\partial D_p$ acts as $X\mapsto\zeta^{\mp1}X$ on $H_{Q,p}$, from copy $Q$ to copy $R_p^{\pm1}(Q)$. Table 3.1 lists them at the 10 class representatives; the others follow by symmetry.
- *Linear germs through non-critical points.* These start with a letter whose disk contains $Q$ in its interior (in all ten cases it maps $Q$ to a non-critical point), act on the whole copy, and reach a critical point after non-critical intermediate points. For the nine non-tight classes they are the paths of the finite skeleta of Table 4.1. For the tight class they are the finitely many linear germs of $G_W$ starting at $\pm E,\pm\bar E$, which were not enumerated.

**Table 3.1.** Letters at the critical class representatives (on $\partial D_1$, $\operatorname{Im}\ge0$).

| representative | value | $a^{\pm1}$ | $b^{\pm1}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(0,1,-1,0)=E$ (tight) | $1.1180+0.3633i$ | tight, critical image | interior; images $F=\varphi^{-1}E$ and $3-\varphi$ (not critical) |
| $(1,2,1,1)$ lens corner | $0+1.9021i$ | tight, critical image | tight, critical image |

The rotation about $-1$ permutes the 20 critical points of $\partial D_1$ in four 5-cycles. Two of them contain the tight class and the lens corner; the lens corners join the cycles of $\partial D_1$ to those of $\partial D_2$.

**Proposition 3.2.** Every orbit of $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$ has at most $190$ points.

*Proof.* Linear germs preserve $|X|$ and permute the 38 copies (Lemma 6.1 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). $\square$

So the limit straight-cut system built from the fixed finite word set, as in step (I) of the route, is well defined, explicit and has bounded orbits. The bound is trivial, and the system misses the non-linear returns:

**Proposition 3.3 (non-linear returns are long).** Let $x\in\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$ and $y\in\overline B(Q',C\varepsilon^k)$ be core points (lattice points or not) in one orbit at level $k$, not linearly related. Then every chain from $x$ to $y$ is a concatenation of at least
$$m_k=\Bigl\lceil\bigl(\varphi^{3k}/(2C)-2s\bigr)/T_W\Bigr\rceil$$
first returns to $W$, so it visits $W$ at least $m_k-1$ times strictly between $x$ and $y$. Here $s=\max_Q|\sigma Q|=2.5263$ (so $2s<6$).

*Proof.* Cut the chain at $x=v_0$, its visits $v_1,\dots,v_{m-1}$ to $W$, and $v_m=y$. Each $v_{i+1}\in R_W(v_i)$ is $g_i(v_i)$ for a germ $g_i\in G_W$ (Proposition 4.4 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). The composite $G=g_m\circ\cdots\circ g_1$, $G(z)=\zeta^Jz+t$, has $|\sigma t|\le mT_W$. Since $y=G(x)$, we have $G(Q)-Q'=(y-Q')-\zeta^J(x-Q)$, so $|G(Q)-Q'|\le2C\varepsilon^k$. This lattice element is non-zero, because $x,y$ are not linearly related. Its absolute norm is $|G(Q)-Q'|^2|\sigma(G(Q)-Q')|^2\ge1$. Hence $|\sigma(G(Q)-Q')|\ge\varphi^{3k}/(2C)$, while $|\sigma(G(Q)-Q')|\le|\sigma t|+|\sigma Q|+|\sigma Q'|\le mT_W+2s$. $\square$

**Consequence for the route.** The orbit of a core point is a union of linear classes (at most 190 points each). By Proposition 3.3, consecutive classes in an orbit are joined only through at least $m_k\asymp\varphi^{3k}$ visits to $W$. This is a statement about which object to study, not a theorem about all conceivable limits. The fixed-word system $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$ sees only the linear classes; the object that carries the core dynamics is the limit, if it exists, of the first-return maps $\Theta_k$ to $B_k$, whose words have unbounded length (a renormalization limit). §5 tests whether such a limit exists. The constants $T_W$ and $\beta_W$ were not computed, so $m_k$ is not explicit.

## 4. Rigidity away from the tight points

### 4.1 Skeleta (exact finite computation)

The *skeleton* $S_P$ of a critical point $P$ is the set of lattice points reached from $P$ by breadth-first search at $r_c$, with closed disks, in which critical points other than $P$ are recorded but not expanded. At a non-critical lattice point the closed and open tests agree, since lattice points on the boundary circles are critical. Every test made is an exact sign in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ (`skelrc.py`, `rigid.py`).

**Table 4.1.** Skeleta at $r_c$. "Binding" is the smallest of the ratios below; $k_0$ is the least $k$ with $\varepsilon^k$ below it.

| class (representative) | $|S_P|$ | critical endpoints | binding constraint | bound on $\varepsilon^k$ | $k_0$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tight $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ | infinite (capped at $10^7$) | | | | none |
| $(1,1,1,0)$, $0.5+1.539i$ | 559 | 3 | test margin | 0.011682 | 4 |
| $(-1,1,-1,-1)$, $0.927+0.951i$ | 42 | 2 | test margin | 0.07966 | 2 |
| lens corner $(1,2,1,1)$ | 5 | 4 | none | | 0 |
| the six outside classes | 3 | 2 | margin at $P$ | $\ge1.75$ | 0 |

The constraints, for every non-critical $q\in S_P$ and $p=\pm1$, and for the non-tight disk at $P$, are:
- (a) $\bigl||q-p|-r_c\bigr|\ge(C+0.0236)\,\varepsilon^k$, where $0.0236\,\varepsilon^k\ge r_c-r_k$ for all $k\ge0$;
- (b) $|q-Q|>2C\varepsilon^k$ for every critical $Q$.

They were evaluated with 50-digit decimal arithmetic. The binding one at $k_0=4$ holds with a factor $3.6$ to spare ($\varepsilon^4=0.00325$ against $0.01168$).

### 4.2 The theorem

**Theorem 4.2 (non-tight cores are rigid; proved modulo the computation of Table 4.1).** Let $C=3/10$, $k\ge4$, and let $P$ be a critical point outside the tight class. For **every** $x\in\overline B(P,C\varepsilon^k)$, lattice point or not:
1. $R_{B_k}(x)\subseteq\{h(x):h\text{ a germ with }h(P)\in S_P\}$, so $|R_{B_k}(x)|\le|S_P|\le559$;
2. every point of $R_{B_k}(x)\cap B_k$ is linearly related to $x$.

*Proof.* Induction along a chain $x=z_0,z_1,\dots$ at radius $r_k$ whose intermediate points avoid $B_k$, with prefix germs $h_i$ ($z_i=h_i(x)$). Claim: $h_i(P)\in S_P$, and if $i\ge1$ is not the last index, then $h_i(P)$ is not critical.
- At $z_0=x$: the tests of the disk tight at $P$ split the core (the inside part is rotated about $p$, onto the core of the critical point $R_p^{\pm1}(P)$, and the chain must stop there). The test of the other disk is decided by constraint (a) at $P$, the same for all $x$, and gives the letters of $S_P$ at $P$.
- At $z_i$ with $q=h_i(P)$ non-critical: $|z_i-q|=|x-P|\le C\varepsilon^k$. By (a) the test of $D_p(r_k)$ at $z_i$ has the outcome of the test of $D_p(r_c)$ at $q$. So the next point is $h_{i+1}(x)$ with $h_{i+1}(P)$ a successor of $q$ in $S_P$. By (b), $z_i\notin B_k$.
- If $h_i(P)=Q'$ is critical, then $|z_i-Q'|=|x-P|\le C\varepsilon^k$, so $z_i\in B_k$ and the chain ends there; $z_i=Q'+\zeta^j(x-P)$ is linearly related to $x$. $\square$

Lattice data agree with the theorem from level $2$ on: on the 714 non-tight sample points of §5, the first-return sets at levels $2,3,4,5$ are identical, and the excursion sizes are exactly the skeleton sizes ($3,5,42,559$).

**Corollary 4.3 ((R-core) reduces to the tight class).** For $k\ge4$ and every orbit $O$ at radius $r_k$,
$$|O\cap B_k|\le190\cdot\max\bigl(1,|O\cap B_k^{\rm t}|\bigr).$$

*Proof.* $O\cap B_k$ is connected by first returns. By Theorem 4.2, a first return that is not linear has both endpoints in the tight core: the reverse of a chain is a chain, so neither end can be non-tight. If $O\cap B_k$ has two or more linear classes, every class meets a non-linear return, so every class contains a tight core point and there are at most $|O\cap B_k^{\rm t}|$ classes. Otherwise there is one class. Each class has at most 190 points. $\square$

The four-window closures are smaller than the 38-window ones, by a factor of $1.2$ to $3.9$ in circles. Fewer windows mean fewer window-boundary arcs to propagate. That no window is needed at the non-tight critical points is consistent with Theorem 4.2, which shows that nothing unbounded happens near them; it is not derived from it.

**Corollary 4.4 (proved modulo the certified row).** Let $W^{\rm t}$ be the four windows $\pm E+W_0$, $\pm\bar E+W_0$ with $w=(0,2,-1,0)$. For every $r<r_c$ and every $z$,
$$|O_r(z)|\le\beta\cdot\max\bigl(1,\sup_x|O_r(x)\cap W^{\rm t}|\bigr)$$
with one constant $\beta<\infty$. Hence $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if its orbits meet $W^{\rm t}$ in uniformly boundedly many points. And (B) holds if and only if this is so at every level $r_k$.

*Proof.* `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Corollary 4.3, whose proof uses only that a restricted closure exists for the window set. $\square$

## 5. The tight class: exact first returns at levels 1–5

### 5.1 Method and validation

`exc.c` and `exc2.c` run, for each start $x=Q+\varepsilon^k\eta$, a breadth-first search over $\Lambda$ at $r_k$ with exact closed-disk tests. Core points other than $x$ are recorded and not expanded, so the program computes $R_{B_k}(x)$ and the first-return set exactly. The core test is $100|y-Q|^2\le9\varepsilon^{2k}$, exact in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$. `exc2.c` also records the rotation index of the chain, and counts lune points (§2.2) by zoomed distance; the lune test is exact.
- **Validation.** Run in closure mode (newly found core points become starts), the program reproduces Table 6.1 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` exactly at levels 1, 2 and 3: 387, 414, 414 orbits with at most 1 578, 1 352, 1 546 core visits. Those numbers were reproduced independently by the referee at levels 1–2.
- **Samples.**
  - $S_{21}$: the 798 points with $|\eta|\le0.3$, $|\sigma\eta|\le8$ (21 per critical point).
  - $S_E$: the 811 points at $E$ with $|\eta|\le0.3$, $|\sigma\eta|\le50$.
  - $S_{\rm cl}$: the closure samples (all core points in the orbits of $S_{21}$; 12 588, 9 146, 10 538 points at levels 1, 2, 3).

### 5.3 Period two

**Conjecture 5.1 (period two at the tight points).** There is $k_0$ such that for $k\ge k_0$ the first-return relation of the tight cores at level $k+2$, in zoomed coordinates, equals that at level $k$, on every zoomed lattice point whose level-$k$ excursion is lune-free within zoomed distance 3.

Evidence (exact; tight points only; the non-tight points carry no evidence, since there the agreement follows from Theorem 4.2, and at levels 1–3 it even holds with period one):
- **Lune criterion.** Tight points whose level-$k$ excursion has no lune point within zoomed distance $3$, and of those, how many have equal first-return sets at $k$ and $k+2$:
  - $k=1$: none of the tight points is lune-free;
  - $k=2$: 20 of 20 ($S_{21}$) and 124 of 124 ($S_E$);
  - $k=3$: 12 of 12 ($S_{21}$, the points computed at level 5) and 117 of 117 ($S_E$, the selected family).

  That is 273 tight cases without a failure, of which the 129 at $k=3$ come from the selected family of Table 5.2. With period one the criterion fails: 0 of the 32 tight points of $S_{21}$ that are lune-free at level 3 agree at $3\to4$, and of the 244 points of $S_E$ lune-free at level 3 only 14 agree at $3\to4$. Conversely, every point of $S_E$ whose return set changes between levels 2 and 4 has a lune point at level 2.
- **Self-similarity of the critical orbit.** Let $O(E)$ be the orbit of $E$ at $r_c$ with closed disks; it is infinite and was computed to $4\cdot10^7$ points. For $y\in O(E)$ with $|y-E|\le\rho$, and with internal coordinate small enough that the image lies within the explored region, it was tested whether $E+\varepsilon^m(y-E)\in O(E)$ (`psi.c`).

  | $\rho$ | $m=1$ ($\varphi^{-3}$): found | $m=2$ ($\varphi^{-6}$): found |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.05 | 84/101 | 6/6 |
  | 0.2 | 856/1 028 | 83/83 |
  | 1.0 | 35 642/45 522 (78%) | **3 661/3 661** |
  | 1.2 | | 4 586/4 640 |
  | 2.0 | | 10 277/12 589 |

  A point found in the explored part of $O(E)$ is an exact member. For $m=1$ the misses are **exact non-memberships**. The referee computed the orbit at $r_c$ of failing images $z$ (`psi5.c`): at $\rho=1$, 200 of the first 200 failures have a finite orbit not containing $E$, and at $\rho=0.2$, 65 of 67 do. Since $z\in O(E)$ would force $E\in O(z)$, these are not members. The referee's own run (orbit to $2\cdot10^7$ points, a stricter internal-radius filter) gives 49 428/63 013 found for $m=1$ and 4 711/4 711 for $m=2$ at $\rho=1$.

  So near $E$ the critical orbit is not invariant under the homothety of ratio $\varphi^{-3}$ about $E$ (proved, by the exact non-memberships). It is consistent with invariance under ratio $\varphi^{-6}$ within distance 1. That invariance is local: it starts to fail between distance 1 and 1.2. Whether it holds exactly is open.
- **Growth.** The skeleton of $E$ grows like (internal radius)$^{1.75\text{–}1.81}$ (sizes $10^5,10^6,10^7$ at internal radii $924$, $3\,289$, $12\,233$). The level-$k$ orbits grow by about $14=(\varphi^3)^{1.83}$ per level. This is consistent with level-$k$ orbits being truncations of the fractal $r_c$ orbit at internal radius $\propto\varphi^{3k}$.

**The mechanism of the parity (referee's analysis, §3 of the report; not yet independently checked).**
- The golden rotation on the chord is exactly self-similar under $\varphi^{-3}$: near $E$ the two-sided chord orbit of $E$ is $E+2\mathbb Z[\varphi]E$, an $\varepsilon$-invariant set. So the one-dimensional part does not explain the parity.
- In the two-dimensional internal space $\sigma(\mathbb C)$, multiplication by $\sigma\varepsilon=-\varphi^3$ is a rotation by $\pi$ composed with an expansion. It preserves orientation and the Hermitian quantities $|\sigma u|^2$, so it does not by itself flip any test.
- All disk tests are signs of real numbers of $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$, whose internal space is the one-dimensional line $\mathbb R$. On that line $\sigma\varepsilon=-\varphi^3$ reverses orientation. Two facts make this matter:
  - total positivity of Hermitian norms, $\sigma(|u|^2)=|\sigma u|^2\ge0$;
  - the alternating level defect $\sigma(r_c^2-r_k^2)=(-\varphi^3)^k/10$.
- **Referee's identity (exact).** For a test at a non-critical point, with $u=h(Q)-p$, write the zoomed cut offset as $\tilde\delta=\varepsilon^{-k}\cdot10(|u|^2-r_k^2)\in1+10\mathbb Z[\varphi]$. Then
$$\sigma\tilde\delta-1=(-\varphi^{3})^{-k}\bigl(10|\sigma u|^2-10(4-\varphi)\bigr),\qquad \operatorname{sign}(\sigma\tilde\delta-1)=(-1)^k\operatorname{sign}\bigl(|\sigma u|-|\sigma w|\bigr).$$
  Only finitely many totally positive values $|u|^2$ near $3+\varphi$ have $\sigma|u|^2<4-\varphi$. So for large $k$ every non-critical near miss has $\sigma\tilde\delta-1$ of sign $(-1)^k$: **the cut offsets of non-critical near misses lie on opposite sides with the parity of $k$**. No non-critical cut offset then occurs exactly at two consecutive levels; the only common offset is $\tilde\delta=1$, the tangent half-plane of the critical tests. The condition is invariant under the totally positive unit $\varepsilon^2$.
- What this does and does not show. It is a necessary-condition argument about exact cut offsets. It does not prove that period-one matching of return sets fails, since cuts need not recur for the combinatorics to recur. It does not prove period two either: §5.4 finds that lines do not recur at period two, which the argument permits but does not force.
- **Prediction.** The level unit gives period one exactly when it is totally positive, and period two otherwise:
  - $n=5$ ($\varphi^{-3}$, norm $-1$): period two;
  - $n=8$ ($\sqrt2-1$, norm $-1$): period two, consistent with `sharp-lower-bound-n8.md` §6;
  - $n=10$ ($\varphi^{-3}$, norm $-1$): period two;
  - $n=12$ ($2-\sqrt3$, totally positive): period one.

**Relation to `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` §7.** That note reports "the observed period is one level" for $n=5$. The two statements measure different things and do not conflict.
- §7 there concerns the orbit of the point $0$, the midpoint of the chord and not a critical point. It records the one-sided inclusion $C_k(c_0)\subseteq C_{k+1}(c_0)$ of the $\varepsilon$-rescaled part of $O_{r_k}(0)$ near $0$, and the fact that level-$(k+1)$ orbits near $0$ are unions of rescaled level-$k$ orbits.
- This note concerns *equality* of first-return relations at the tight points $\pm E,\pm\bar E$, which is a much stronger property.

An inclusion that recurs with period one is compatible with equality failing with period one. The combined note's refutation of level-independent orbit partitions (`sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` §6.3) already showed that period-one inclusion near $0$ does not extend to equality. Nothing in this note re-tests the inclusion at $0$. For the question that matters for (R-core), the return relation at the tight cores, Table 5.2 decides: it is not $\varphi^{-3}$-periodic.

### 5.4 The chain domains do not form a finite line arrangement (evidence)

For each excursion step at a visited point $y=h(x)$ within $2C\varepsilon^k$ of a boundary circle $\partial D_p(r_k)$, `exc5.c` computes the line in the start's zoomed coordinate along which that test cuts the zoomed core. The line has offset $\varepsilon^{-k}(|h(Q)-p|-r_k)$ and normal $\zeta^{-J}(h(Q)-p)$, where $h(Q)=y-\zeta^J(x-Q)$ is the exact image of the critical point. Over $S_{21}$:
- the number of distinct such lines meeting the zoomed core balls is $951$, $1\,742$, $4\,876$ at levels 1, 2, 3;
- of these, $747$, $1\,150$, $3\,000$ come from tests at physical distance $\ge0.1$ from every critical point: *non-critical near misses*, which Lemma 2.1 does not control;
- between levels (and between levels two apart) essentially no line recurs within $10^{-4}$ in offset and angle (at most $0.5\%$, against more than $80\%$ within $10^{-2}$, which is what the density of the line sets alone produces).

Yet two levels apart the first-return sets agree on a large fraction of points (Table 5.2). So most of these cuts must be inessential for the return relation, in the sense that another chain with the same endpoint takes over across the cut. **Consequently a limit system has to be defined on the level of the return relation (germs and their maximal domains), not as a closure of chain-domain lines.** Nothing proved here reduces its finiteness to a straight-line arrangement closure. This is the precise point at which step (II) of the route, "a certificate-style closure for a straight-line arrangement", is not available as stated.

## 6. Assembly

**(R-bulk-t)** For a sequence of window sets $W^{(j)}$ at the four tight points only, with $\rho_{W^{(j)}}\to0$ (windows bounded by arcs of any radius allowed), $\beta^*(W^{(j)})<\infty$.

**(R-core-t)** There are $N$ and $k_0$ such that $\sup_x|O_{r_k}(x)\cap B_k^{\rm t}|\le N$ for every $k\ge k_0$.

**Theorem 6.1 (proved).** (R-bulk-t) and (R-core-t) imply (B).

*Proof.* As `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Proposition 6.3. For $k\ge k_0$ choose $j$ with $W^{(j)}\subseteq B_k^{\rm t}$. Then $|O_{r_k}(z)|\le\beta^*(W^{(j)})\max(1,N)$ by Corollary 4.3 there, which needs only a window set with finite $\beta^*$. Monotonicity finishes. $\square$

## Appendix A. Data and reproduction

All programs are in the session scratch directory `sharp3/`. They were written for this note, except `rcut_sel.c`, `chk_tight.py` and `refchk_tight.py`: these are copies of `sharp2/rcut.c`, `sharp2/chk.py` and `refcomb/refchk.py` restricted to the four tight windows.

- `exc.c` (`cc -O2 -o exc exc.c -lm`): `exc k Cn Cd cap closure < starts`. Starts are lines `Qindex e0 e1 e2 e3` (the index into the list of 38 in `exc.c`, $\eta$ in the power basis). Output: `E Q f0..f3` for each first-return core point, then `S Q e0..e3 size maxsigma lune0..lune4 capped`. `exc 2 3 10 50000000 1 < st_03_8.txt` reproduces the level-2 row of Table 6.1 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`.
- `exc2.c`: as `exc.c`, with the chain rotation index on `E` lines and the near-miss statistics. `exc3.c` and `exc5.c` add the near-miss histograms and the zoomed cut lines of §5.4.
- `gen.py C0 R0 [indices]`: start lists ($S_{21}$ = `gen.py 0.3 8`; $S_E$ = `gen.py 0.3 50 19`).
- `skelrc.py`, `rigid.py`: Table 4.1. `psi.c` (`psi Pindex cap rho limf`): the $\Psi_E^m$ tests of §5.3. `skelc.c`: skeleton growth.
- `rcut_sel.c`: `WSEL=7,18,19,30 rcut_sel 0 0 1 w0 w1 w2 w3 maxpieces out.txt` (Table 4.2). `chk_tight.py` and `refchk_tight.py LIBDIR arcfile w0 w1 w2 w3` check four-window certificates.
- Comparison scripts: `comp.py` (orbit blocks from closure runs), `ana.py`, `dcmp.py` (Tables 5.1, 5.2), `nmcmp.py`, `lhist.py` (§5.4).

Indices of the tight points in the list of 38: $7=(-1,-1,-1,-2)$, $18=(0,-1,1,0)$, $19=(0,1,-1,0)=E$, $30=(1,1,1,2)$.

**Excursion sizes at the tight points** (two families of $S_{21}$; levels 1–5):
- $156,\ 60\,861,\ 21\,066,\ 16\,128\,171,\ 3\,473\,926$ (first-return sets of $6,36,6,46,6$ points, counting the start);
- $155,\ 29\,299,\ 21\,025,\ 5\,249\,989,\ 3\,473\,937$ (sets of $2,14,2,10,2$ points).

In both, the odd-level sets are equal and the even-level ones differ.

Checker logs: `chk_tight.log`, `refchk_tight.log` (row $(0,2,-1,0)$), `chk_tight_w1.log`, `refchk_tight_w1.log` (row $(5,-1,4,0)$). Closure logs: `rct_tight.log`, `rct_t_*.log`.
