# Finiteness of $GG_5(r)$ below $\sqrt{3+\varphi}$: the straight-cut limit at the critical points, rigidity away from the tight points, and a period-two obstruction there

Authors: Eric Vergo, Claude

**Status.** Let $r_c=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$. **(B), finiteness of $GG_5(r)$ for every $r<r_c$, is still open; this note does not prove it.** It carries out the referee's route of `referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` §5 (limit straight-cut system, its finiteness, matching from a level $k_0$, the bulk, assembly) as far as it goes and locates where it breaks. In summary:

1. **Proved: the straight-cut limit of the fixed-word system is trivial** (§3). In zoomed coordinates at the critical points, the induced system generated by the fixed finite word set of a certified window converges to a pseudogroup $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$ of rotations $X\mapsto\zeta^jX$ on half-planes and whole copies. It is explicit (Tables 3.1, 4.1) except for finitely many linear germs at the tight points, and its orbits have at most $190$ points. Every *non-linear* return to the level-$k$ core is a concatenation of at least $(\varphi^{3k}/2C-6)/T_W$ first returns to the window (Proposition 3.3). So step (I) of the route, as formulated, produces a limit system whose finiteness is immediate but which does not see the dynamics that (R-core) is about; a useful limit must be a limit of first-return maps with words of unbounded length.
2. **Proved modulo an exact finite computation: everything happens at the four tight points $\pm E,\pm\bar E$** (§4).
   - For the nine non-tight classes of critical points, every first return to the core is a linear germ from level $k=4$ on (with core radius $C=3/10$), for *all* points, not only lattice points (Theorem 4.2).
   - Hence (R-core) is equivalent, up to a factor $190$, to its restriction to the tight cores (Corollary 4.3).
   - The relative certificate with windows **only at the four tight points** exists for every window size tried (nine sizes; Table 4.2). The two largest are VALID under the referee's interval-arithmetic checker and under `chk.py`, both adapted to the four windows. So **for every $r<r_c$, $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if its orbits meet four small windows at $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ in uniformly boundedly many points** (Corollary 4.4), and (B) follows from two statements about the tight class alone, (R-bulk-t) and (R-core-t) (Theorem 6.1).
3. **Refuted (exact): literal $\varphi^{-3}$ matching at the tight points, at every level tested, including $3\to4$ and $4\to5$** (§5). The first-return sets of zoomed core lattice points of the tight class almost never agree between consecutive levels (at most $14$ of $811$, and $0$ of $2\,436$, $2\,564$, $84$, $12$ in the other samples), lune-free or not. This is not a lune effect: in the data, lune points account for every disagreement between levels $k$ and $k+2$, but not for those between $k$ and $k+1$.
4. **Conjecture with exact evidence: the renormalization at the tight points has period two, with unit $\varphi^{-6}$** (§5.3).
   - The two-level comparison $k\to k+2$ agrees on a fraction rising with $k$: $9.5\%$ and $23\%$ ($1\to3$), $62\%$ and $64\%$ ($2\to4$), $100\%$ ($3\to5$, on all $12+117$ tight points that fit in memory at level 5).
   - On $1\,784$ sampled core points whose level-$k$ excursion contains no lattice point in a lune at zoomed distance $\le3$, the first-return set at level $k+2$ equals that at level $k$, without exception ($k=1,2,3$).
   - The orbit of $E$ at $r_c$ is mapped into itself by $y\mapsto E+\varphi^{-6}(y-E)$ on all $3\,661$ tested points within distance $1$ of $E$, but not by $y\mapsto E+\varphi^{-3}(y-E)$ ($22\%$ failures).
5. **Evidence against a finite straight-cut first-return system** (§5.4). The domains of the excursion chains from the tight cores are cut by lines that do not recur between levels, most of them coming from circles that pass near the excursion at macroscopic distance from every critical point. Their number grows with $k$. Any limit system therefore has to be defined at the level of the return relation, not of chain domains, and its finiteness is not reduced to a line-arrangement closure by anything proved here.

So the route breaks at step (III) for the tight class, in the strong sense that the level-$k$ and level-$(k+1)$ systems do not match. Whether they match with period two from some $k_0$ is the open question; if they do, the data point to $k_0\le3$ for lune-free points, which is beyond the certified base (level $\approx1.3$).

Terms used below: *core*, *zoomed coordinate*, *first-return set*, *linearly related* (§2); *skeleton* (§4.1); *lune point* (§2.2).

## 1. Setting

As in `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` §1:
- $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$, $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ with basis $1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3$, $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and $\sigma\colon\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2$;
- $D_1,D_2$ are the closed disks of radius $r$ about $\mp1$; $a,b$ rotate by $\zeta^{-1}$ about $\mp1$ on their disks and are the identity elsewhere;
- $\varepsilon=\varphi^{-3}=2\varphi-3$, with $\sigma(\varepsilon)=-\varphi^3$ and $N(\varepsilon)=-1$;
- the levels are $r_k^2=3+\varphi-\varepsilon^k/10$, so $10r_k^2=30+10\varphi-\varepsilon^k$. They are non-norm radii (`sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` Lemma 2.2), so no lattice point lies on a circle of radius $r_k$ about a lattice point.

The 38 *critical points* are the lattice points of $\partial D_1\cup\partial D_2$ at $r_c$, in 10 classes under $\{\pm z,\pm\bar z\}$ (`sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Lemma 2.1, Table 2.1). The *tight class* is $\{\pm E,\pm\bar E\}$, $E=\zeta-\zeta^2=(0,1,-1,0)$: $\pm E$ are the ends of the critical chord of Theorem A and $\pm\bar E$ their mirror images, all four tight points of the critical words (`critical-radius-n5.md` §3). For a critical point $Q$ on $\partial D_p$ write $w=Q-p$; then $|w|^2=3+\varphi$, $w$ is one of the 20 elements $\pm\zeta^j\pi,\pm\zeta^j\bar\pi$, and
$$|\sigma w|^2=\sigma(3+\varphi)=4-\varphi,\qquad |\sigma w|=1.5434\quad\text{for every critical point.}\tag{1.1}$$
The arguments of the 20 elements $w$ are $\pm9.7323^\circ+36^\circ j$.

Refereed facts used: orbit and group monotonicity (`boundary-contact.md` Theorems 3.1, 3.4); bounded orbits give a finite group (`theorem1-converse.md` Proposition 4.2); the relative-certificate theorem and its corollaries (`sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3, Propositions 4.4, 4.5), whose proofs never use which critical points carry windows.

## 2. Zoomed coordinates, cores and lunes

### 2.1 Definitions

Fix $C>0$ (below always $C=3/10$). The *level-$k$ core* is $B_k=\bigcup_Q\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$ over the 38 critical points; the *tight core* $B_k^{\rm t}$ is the union over the four tight points. A point $x\in\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$ has *zoomed coordinate* $X=\varepsilon^{-k}(x-Q)$. Since $\varepsilon$ is a unit, $x\in\Lambda$ if and only if $X\in\Lambda$; the zoomed internal coordinate is $\sigma X=\sigma(\varepsilon)^{-k}(\sigma x-\sigma Q)$.

The *first-return set* of a core point $x$ at level $k$ is $R_{B_k}(x)\cap B_k$, where $R_{B_k}(x)$ is the set of endpoints of chains at radius $r_k$ from $x$ whose intermediate points avoid $B_k$ (Definition 3.2 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). The orbit $O_{r_k}(x)\cap B_k$ is the connected component of $x$ in the graph of first returns, because the generating set is symmetric.

Two core points $x\in B(Q,\cdot)$, $y\in B(Q',\cdot)$ are *linearly related* if $y-Q'=\zeta^j(x-Q)$ for some $j$. This is an equivalence relation; a class has at most $38\cdot5=190$ points. A germ $g(z)=\zeta^jz+t$ with $g(Q)=Q'$ is *linear*; in zoomed coordinates it is $X\mapsto\zeta^jX$ (`sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Lemma 6.1).

### 2.2 The tight tests at every critical point (proved)

For a critical point $Q\in\partial D_p$ and a lattice point $y=Q+\varepsilon^kY$ put
$$\lambda_Q(Y)=10\cdot2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(Y\bar w\bigr)+1\in\mathbb Z[\varphi],\qquad w=Q-p .$$
Then exactly
$$10\bigl(|y-p|^2-r_k^2\bigr)=\varepsilon^k\lambda_Q(Y)+10\,\varepsilon^{2k}|Y|^2 .\tag{2.1}$$
This is (6.1) of `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md`, written in zoomed coordinates and valid at all 38 critical points. The *tangent half-plane test* is $\lambda_Q(Y)<0$; in the limit $k\to\infty$ the disk test becomes the half-plane
$$H_{Q,p}=\bigl\{X:\ 2\operatorname{Re}(X\bar w)<-\tfrac1{10}\bigr\},$$
whose boundary line lies at distance $1/(20r_c)=0.02327$ from $0$, on the side of $p$. A *lune point* is a lattice point where the disk test and the tangent half-plane test disagree.

**Lemma 2.1 (monotone lunes).** Let $Q\in\partial D_p$ be critical and $Y\in\Lambda$.
1. $Q+\varepsilon^kY$ is a lune point at level $k$ if and only if $-10\varepsilon^k|Y|^2<\lambda_Q(Y)<0$. The number $\lambda_Q(Y)$ does not depend on $k$. Hence the set of zoomed lune points decreases with $k$, and each $Y$ is a lune point for only finitely many $k$.
2. (Lune lemma in zoomed form.) If $10\,\varepsilon^k|Y|^2\bigl(20|\sigma w|\,|\sigma Y|+1\bigr)<1$, then $Q+\varepsilon^kY$ is not a lune point. With (1.1) the constant is $20|\sigma w|=30.87$ at every critical point.
3. Consequently, on $\{Y:|Y|\le R,\ |\sigma Y|\le M\}$ there is no lune point once $10\,\varepsilon^kR^2(30.87M+1)<1$.

*Proof.* (1) By (2.1), with $\varepsilon>0$. Equality cases do not occur: $\lambda\equiv1\pmod{10}$ is non-zero, and the left side of (2.1) is non-zero at a non-norm radius. (2) $\lambda\equiv1\pmod{10}$ gives $N(\lambda)\equiv1\pmod{10}$, so $|\lambda|\ge1/|\sigma\lambda|$, and $|\sigma\lambda|\le20|\sigma Y||\sigma w|+1$. If $|\lambda|>10\varepsilon^k|Y|^2$, item (1) excludes the lune. (3) follows. $\square$

Item 2 is `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` Lemma 6.1(2), extended from the tight pairs to all 38 critical points. It is the answer to the route's question whether "the same Diophantine argument is available for the other circles": **for every circle through a critical point, yes, with one uniform constant.** For circles that pass close to an excursion away from all critical points, no argument of this kind is available. Such *non-critical near misses* do occur (§5.4).

## 3. The straight-cut limit of the fixed-word system (proved)

Let $W$ be a window set for which a restricted closure exists. Let $\mathcal W$ be its finite word set, $G_W$ the finite set of germs, and $T_W=\max\{|\sigma t|\}$ over $G_W$ (Proposition 4.4 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). Proposition 3.1 uses windows at all 38 critical points (Table 5.1 there); Proposition 3.3 holds for any such $W$, including the four-window sets of Table 4.2.

**Proposition 3.1 (zoom dichotomy).** There is $k_1$ such that for $k\ge k_1$ and every germ $g\in G_W$ whose level-$k$ domain meets the core $\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$, exactly one of the following holds.
1. $g(Q)=Q'$ is critical. In zoomed coordinates $g$ is $X\mapsto\zeta^jX$. Its domain in the zoomed core is cut only by the tests at $Q$ itself: those of the disk tight at $Q$ converge to $H_{Q,p}$ or its complement, and all other tests are constant on the core.
2. $g(Q)$ is not critical, and $g(\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k))\cap B_k=\emptyset$.

*Proof.* Finitely many germs and prefixes. The intermediate prefix images $h(Q)$ of a first return to $W$ lie outside $W$, so they are not critical (critical points are window centres). A lattice point that is not critical satisfies $|h(Q)-p|\ne r_c$, so it is at a fixed positive distance from $\partial D_p(r_c)$, from $\partial W$ and from the critical set, while $r_c-r_k\to0$ and the core radius tends to $0$. At $Q$ itself, (2.1) applies. In case 2, $g(Q)$ is at a fixed positive distance from the critical set. $\square$

**The limit system $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$ (explicit data).** It acts on 38 copies of the plane, one per critical point, and is generated by the limits of the linear germs of case 1. These are of two kinds.
- *Single tight letters.* A letter tight at $Q\in\partial D_p$ acts as $X\mapsto\zeta^{\mp1}X$ on $H_{Q,p}$, from copy $Q$ to copy $R_p^{\pm1}(Q)$. Table 3.1 lists them at the 10 class representatives; the others follow by symmetry.
- *Linear germs through non-critical points.* These start with a letter whose disk contains $Q$ in its interior (in all ten cases it maps $Q$ to a non-critical point), act on the whole copy, and reach a critical point after non-critical intermediate points. For the nine non-tight classes they are the paths of the finite skeleta of Table 4.1. For the tight class they are the finitely many linear germs of $G_W$ starting at $\pm E,\pm\bar E$, which were not enumerated.

**Table 3.1.** Letters at the critical class representatives (on $\partial D_1$, $\operatorname{Im}\ge0$).

| representative | value | $a^{\pm1}$ | $b^{\pm1}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(0,1,-1,0)=E$ (tight) | $1.1180+0.3633i$ | tight, critical image | interior; images $F=\varphi^{-1}E$ and $3-\varphi$ (not critical) |
| $(1,2,1,1)$ lens corner | $0+1.9021i$ | tight, critical image | tight, critical image |
| $(1,1,1,0)$ | $0.5+1.5388i$ | tight, critical image | interior, not critical |

The rotation about $-1$ permutes the 20 critical points of $\partial D_1$ in four 5-cycles. Two of them contain the tight class and the lens corner; the lens corners join the cycles of $\partial D_1$ to those of $\partial D_2$.

**Proposition 3.2.** Every orbit of $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$ has at most $190$ points.

*Proof.* Linear germs preserve $|X|$ and permute the 38 copies (Lemma 6.1 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). $\square$

So the limit straight-cut system built from the fixed finite word set, as in step (I) of the route, is well defined, explicit and has bounded orbits. The bound is trivial, and the system misses the non-linear returns:

**Proposition 3.3 (non-linear returns are long).** Let $x\in\overline B(Q,C\varepsilon^k)$ and $y\in\overline B(Q',C\varepsilon^k)$ be core points (lattice points or not) in one orbit at level $k$, not linearly related. Then every chain from $x$ to $y$ is a concatenation of at least
$$m_k=\Bigl\lceil\bigl(\varphi^{3k}/(2C)-2s\bigr)/T_W\Bigr\rceil$$
first returns to $W$, so it visits $W$ at least $m_k-1$ times strictly between $x$ and $y$. Here $s=\max_Q|\sigma Q|=2.5263$ (so $2s<6$).

*Proof.* Cut the chain at $x=v_0$, its visits $v_1,\dots,v_{m-1}$ to $W$, and $v_m=y$. Each $v_{i+1}\in R_W(v_i)$ is $g_i(v_i)$ for a germ $g_i\in G_W$ (Proposition 4.4 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`). The composite $G=g_m\circ\cdots\circ g_1$, $G(z)=\zeta^Jz+t$, has $|\sigma t|\le mT_W$. Since $y=G(x)$, we have $G(Q)-Q'=(y-Q')-\zeta^J(x-Q)$, so $|G(Q)-Q'|\le2C\varepsilon^k$. This lattice element is non-zero, because $x,y$ are not linearly related. Its absolute norm is $|G(Q)-Q'|^2|\sigma(G(Q)-Q')|^2\ge1$. Hence $|\sigma(G(Q)-Q')|\ge\varphi^{3k}/(2C)$, while $|\sigma(G(Q)-Q')|\le|\sigma t|+|\sigma Q|+|\sigma Q'|\le mT_W+2s$. $\square$

**Consequence for the route.** The orbit of a core point is a union of linear classes (at most 190 points each). By Proposition 3.3, consecutive classes in an orbit are joined only through at least $m_k\asymp\varphi^{3k}$ visits to $W$. A limit system that captures the core dynamics must therefore be a limit of first-return maps $\Theta_k$ to $B_k$, whose words have unbounded length (a renormalization limit), not the fixed-word system $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$. §5 tests whether such a limit exists.

## 4. Rigidity away from the tight points

### 4.1 Skeleta (exact finite computation)

The *skeleton* $S_P$ of a critical point $P$ is the set of lattice points reached from $P$ by breadth-first search at $r_c$, with closed disks, in which critical points other than $P$ are recorded but not expanded. At a non-critical lattice point the closed and open tests agree, since lattice points on the boundary circles are critical. Every test made is an exact sign in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ (`skelrc.py`, `rigid.py`).

**Table 4.1.** Skeleta at $r_c$. "Binding" is the smallest of the ratios below; $k_0$ is the least $k$ with $\varepsilon^k$ below it.

| class (representative) | $|S_P|$ | critical endpoints | binding constraint | bound on $\varepsilon^k$ | $k_0$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tight $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ | infinite (capped at $10^7$) | | | | none |
| $(1,1,1,0)$, $0.5+1.539i$ | 559 | 3 | test margin | 0.011682 | 4 |
| $(-1,1,-1,-1)$, $0.927+0.951i$ | 42 | 2 | test margin | 0.07966 | 2 |
| lens corner $(1,2,1,1)$ | 5 | 4 | none | | 0 |
| the six outside classes | 3 | 2 | margin at $P$ | $\ge1.75$ | 0 |

The constraints, for every non-critical $q\in S_P$ and $p=\pm1$, and for the non-tight disk at $P$, are:
- (a) $\bigl||q-p|-r_c\bigr|\ge(C+0.0236)\,\varepsilon^k$, where $0.0236\,\varepsilon^k\ge r_c-r_k$ for all $k\ge0$;
- (b) $|q-Q|>2C\varepsilon^k$ for every critical $Q$.

They were evaluated with 50-digit decimal arithmetic. The binding one at $k_0=4$ holds with a factor $3.6$ to spare ($\varepsilon^4=0.00325$ against $0.01168$).

### 4.2 The theorem

**Theorem 4.2 (non-tight cores are rigid; proved modulo the computation of Table 4.1).** Let $C=3/10$, $k\ge4$, and let $P$ be a critical point outside the tight class. For **every** $x\in\overline B(P,C\varepsilon^k)$, lattice point or not:
1. $R_{B_k}(x)\subseteq\{h(x):h\text{ a germ with }h(P)\in S_P\}$, so $|R_{B_k}(x)|\le|S_P|\le559$;
2. every point of $R_{B_k}(x)\cap B_k$ is linearly related to $x$.

*Proof.* Induction along a chain $x=z_0,z_1,\dots$ at radius $r_k$ whose intermediate points avoid $B_k$, with prefix germs $h_i$ ($z_i=h_i(x)$). Claim: $h_i(P)\in S_P$, and if $i\ge1$ is not the last index, then $h_i(P)$ is not critical.
- At $z_0=x$: the tests of the disk tight at $P$ split the core (the inside part is rotated about $p$, onto the core of the critical point $R_p^{\pm1}(P)$, and the chain must stop there). The test of the other disk is decided by constraint (a) at $P$, the same for all $x$, and gives the letters of $S_P$ at $P$.
- At $z_i$ with $q=h_i(P)$ non-critical: $|z_i-q|=|x-P|\le C\varepsilon^k$. By (a) the test of $D_p(r_k)$ at $z_i$ has the outcome of the test of $D_p(r_c)$ at $q$. So the next point is $h_{i+1}(x)$ with $h_{i+1}(P)$ a successor of $q$ in $S_P$. By (b), $z_i\notin B_k$.
- If $h_i(P)=Q'$ is critical, then $|z_i-Q'|=|x-P|\le C\varepsilon^k$, so $z_i\in B_k$ and the chain ends there; $z_i=Q'+\zeta^j(x-P)$ is linearly related to $x$. $\square$

Lattice data agree with the theorem from level $2$ on: on the 714 non-tight sample points of §5, the first-return sets at levels $2,3,4,5$ are identical, and the excursion sizes are exactly the skeleton sizes ($3,5,42,559$).

**Corollary 4.3 ((R-core) reduces to the tight class).** For $k\ge4$ and every orbit $O$ at radius $r_k$,
$$|O\cap B_k|\le190\cdot\max\bigl(1,|O\cap B_k^{\rm t}|\bigr).$$

*Proof.* $O\cap B_k$ is connected by first returns. By Theorem 4.2, a first return that is not linear has both endpoints in the tight core: the reverse of a chain is a chain, so neither end can be non-tight. Every linear class of $O\cap B_k$ other than the one of a fixed base point is entered through such a return, so it contains a tight core point. Each class has at most 190 points. $\square$

**Corollary 4.4 (proved modulo the certified row).** Let $W^{\rm t}$ be the four windows $\pm E+W_0$, $\pm\bar E+W_0$ with $w=(0,2,-1,0)$. For every $r<r_c$ and every $z$,
$$|O_r(z)|\le\beta\cdot\max\bigl(1,\sup_x|O_r(x)\cap W^{\rm t}|\bigr)$$
with one constant $\beta<\infty$. Hence $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if its orbits meet $W^{\rm t}$ in uniformly boundedly many points. And (B) holds if and only if this is so at every level $r_k$.

*Proof.* `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Corollary 4.3, whose proof uses only that a restricted closure exists for the window set. $\square$

## 5. The tight class: exact first returns at levels 1–5

### 5.1 Method and validation

`exc.c` and `exc2.c` run, for each start $x=Q+\varepsilon^k\eta$, a breadth-first search over $\Lambda$ at $r_k$ with exact closed-disk tests. Core points other than $x$ are recorded and not expanded, so the program computes $R_{B_k}(x)$ and the first-return set exactly. The core test is $100|y-Q|^2\le9\varepsilon^{2k}$, exact in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$. `exc2.c` also records the rotation index of the chain, and counts lune points (§2.2) by zoomed distance; the lune test is exact.
- **Validation.** Run in closure mode (newly found core points become starts), the program reproduces Table 6.1 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` exactly at levels 1, 2 and 3: 387, 414, 414 orbits with at most 1 578, 1 352, 1 546 core visits. Those numbers were reproduced independently by the referee at levels 1–2.
- **Samples.**
  - $S_{21}$: the 798 points with $|\eta|\le0.3$, $|\sigma\eta|\le8$ (21 per critical point).
  - $S_E$: the 811 points at $E$ with $|\eta|\le0.3$, $|\sigma\eta|\le50$.
  - $S_{\rm cl}$: the closure samples (all core points in the orbits of $S_{21}$; 12 588, 9 146, 10 538 points at levels 1, 2, 3).
- **Level 4:** all of $S_{21}$ and $S_E$, no cap reached (largest excursion $1.81\cdot10^7$ points).
- **Level 5:** cap $3\cdot10^7$ points per excursion. It was reached for 72 points of $S_{21}$, all in the tight class (18 at each tight point). The level-5 data on the tight class are therefore the 12 uncapped points of $S_{21}$ and 117 points of $S_E$, whose excursions have about $3.47\cdot10^6$ points.

For example, $(P,\eta)=(E,(-3,-1,-1,-3))$, with $|\eta|=0.236$, $|\sigma\eta|=4.24$, has excursions of $21\,066$, $16\,128\,171$ and $3\,473\,926$ points at levels 3, 4, 5. Its first-return sets have $6$, $46$ and $6$ elements, counting $x$ itself; those at levels 3 and 5 are equal, and the one at level 4 is different. Its level-3 excursion contains no lune point within zoomed distance 3. At levels 1–3 this point was re-checked by an independent program (`xcheck.py`: the referee's arithmetic module `ref_zeta.py` and a separate search). It gives the same excursion sizes $156$, $60\,861$, $21\,066$, equal first-return sets at levels 1 and 3, and a different one at level 2.

### 5.3 Period two

**Conjecture 5.1 (period two at the tight points).** There is $k_0$ such that for $k\ge k_0$ the first-return relation of the tight cores at level $k+2$, in zoomed coordinates, equals that at level $k$, on every zoomed lattice point whose level-$k$ excursion is lune-free within zoomed distance 3.

Evidence (exact):
- **Lune criterion with period two.** Points whose level-$k$ excursion has no lune point within zoomed distance $3$, and of those, how many have equal first-return sets at $k$ and $k+2$:
  - $k=1$: 216 of 216 ($S_{21}$);
  - $k=2$: 718 of 718 ($S_{21}$) and 124 of 124 ($S_E$);
  - $k=3$: 726 of 726 ($S_{21}$).

  That is $1\,784$ cases without a failure. With period one the same criterion fails: all 726 points of $S_{21}$ are lune-free at level 3, yet 12 of them change between levels 3 and 4. Conversely, every point of $S_E$ whose return set changes between levels 2 and 4 has a lune point at level 2.
- **Self-similarity of the critical orbit.** Let $O(E)$ be the orbit of $E$ at $r_c$ with closed disks; it is infinite and was computed to $4\cdot10^7$ points. For $y\in O(E)$ with $|y-E|\le\rho$ and with internal coordinate small enough that the image is within the explored region, it was tested whether $E+\varepsilon^m(y-E)\in O(E)$ (`psi.c`).

  | $\rho$ | $m=1$ ($\varphi^{-3}$) | $m=2$ ($\varphi^{-6}$) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.05 | 84/101 | 6/6 |
  | 0.2 | 856/1 028 | 83/83 |
  | 1.0 | 35 642/45 522 (78%) | **3 661/3 661** |
  | 1.2 | | 4 586/4 640 |
  | 2.0 | | 10 277/12 589 |

  So near $E$ the critical orbit appears to be invariant under the homothety of ratio $\varphi^{-6}$ about $E$, and not under ratio $\varphi^{-3}$. The invariance is local: it starts to fail between distance 1 and 1.2.
- **Growth.** The skeleton of $E$ grows like (internal radius)$^{1.75\text{–}1.81}$ (sizes $10^5,10^6,10^7$ at internal radii $924$, $3\,289$, $12\,233$). The level-$k$ orbits grow by about $14=(\varphi^3)^{1.83}$ per level. This is consistent with level-$k$ orbits being truncations of the fractal $r_c$ orbit at internal radius $\propto\varphi^{3k}$.

**Why period two is plausible, and what it is not.**
- The golden rotation on the chord is exactly self-similar under $\varphi^{-3}$: the two-sided chord orbit of $E$ is $E+2\mathbb Z[\varphi]E$ near $E$, a $\varepsilon$-invariant set. So the 1-D part does not explain the parity.
- Multiplication by $\varepsilon$ reverses the orientation of internal space ($\sigma\varepsilon<0$), while $\varepsilon^2$ does not. The level radii see this: $\sigma(r_k^2)=4-\varphi-\sigma(\varepsilon)^k/10$ alternates between large positive and large negative values. The positive unit $\varepsilon^2$ is the natural candidate for an exact renormalization of a cut-and-project set whose internal window is not centrally symmetric.
- This is offered as an explanation, not proved. The parity agrees with the period two found for $n=8$ (`sharp-lower-bound-n8.md` §6).

### 5.4 The chain domains do not form a finite line arrangement (evidence)

For each excursion step at a visited point $y=h(x)$ within $2C\varepsilon^k$ of a boundary circle $\partial D_p(r_k)$, `exc5.c` computes the line in the start's zoomed coordinate along which that test cuts the zoomed core. The line has offset $\varepsilon^{-k}(|h(Q)-p|-r_k)$ and normal $\zeta^{-J}(h(Q)-p)$, where $h(Q)=y-\zeta^J(x-Q)$ is the exact image of the critical point. Over $S_{21}$:
- the number of distinct such lines meeting the zoomed core balls is $951$, $1\,742$, $4\,876$ at levels 1, 2, 3;
- of these, $747$, $1\,150$, $3\,000$ come from tests at physical distance $\ge0.1$ from every critical point: *non-critical near misses*, which Lemma 2.1 does not control;
- between levels (and between levels two apart) essentially no line recurs within $10^{-4}$ in offset and angle (at most $0.5\%$, against more than $80\%$ within $10^{-2}$, which is what the density of the line sets alone produces).

Yet the first-return sets are stable at period two (Table 5.2). So most of these cuts must be inessential for the return relation, in the sense that another chain with the same endpoint takes over across the cut. **Consequently a limit system has to be defined on the level of the return relation (germs and their maximal domains), not as a closure of chain-domain lines.** Nothing proved here reduces its finiteness to a straight-line arrangement closure. This is the precise point at which step (II) of the route, "a certificate-style closure for a straight-line arrangement", is not available as stated.

## 6. Assembly

**(R-bulk-t)** For a sequence of window sets $W^{(j)}$ at the four tight points only, with $\rho_{W^{(j)}}\to0$ (windows bounded by arcs of any radius allowed), $\beta^*(W^{(j)})<\infty$.

**(R-core-t)** There are $N$ and $k_0$ such that $\sup_x|O_{r_k}(x)\cap B_k^{\rm t}|\le N$ for every $k\ge k_0$.

**Theorem 6.1 (proved).** (R-bulk-t) and (R-core-t) imply (B).

*Proof.* As `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` Proposition 6.3. For $k\ge k_0$ choose $j$ with $W^{(j)}\subseteq B_k^{\rm t}$. Then $|O_{r_k}(z)|\le\beta^*(W^{(j)})\max(1,N)$ by Corollary 4.3 there, which needs only a window set with finite $\beta^*$. Monotonicity finishes. $\square$

With Corollary 4.3 above, (R-core-t) is equivalent to (R-core) for $k\ge4$. With Corollary 4.4, (B) is equivalent to the per-level finiteness of $\sup_x|O_{r_k}(x)\cap W^{\rm t}|$. The route's steps now stand as follows.

| step | status |
|---|---|
| (I) limit system | Fixed-word limit $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$: **proved**, trivial (Propositions 3.1–3.3). Renormalization limit of first returns: not constructed. It is not $\varphi^{-3}$-self-similar (**refuted**, §5.2) and is conjecturally $\varphi^{-6}$-self-similar (Conjecture 5.1) |
| (II) finiteness of the limit | $\mathcal S_{\rm lin}$: **proved** (190). Renormalization limit: open; no reduction to a line-arrangement closure (§5.4) |
| (III) matching | Non-tight classes: **proved** from $k=4$, all points (Theorem 4.2). Tight class: **refuted** with period one at $3\to4$ and $4\to5$; with period two, consistent at $1\to3$, $2\to4$, $3\to5$ on lune-free points (1 784 cases), not proved |
| (IV) bulk | Reduced to the tight class (Table 4.2: nine sizes closed, the two largest certified). (R-bulk-t) for all scales: open; the annulus stabilization was not attempted |
| (V) assembly | Theorem 6.1. Base levels: certified to $\delta=1/55$ (level $\approx1.3$). If Conjecture 5.1 held from $k_0=3$, both phases would need a base: levels 3 and 4. A full closure at level 3 needs about 150 GB, so the base is **out of reach of ordinary certificates** |

**The hybrid certificate that the base would need.** At a fixed level $k$, combine
- a fixed relative certificate with windows $W^{\rm t}$ (Table 4.2), which bounds everything outside the four tight windows uniformly in $r$, and
- an exact closure of the induced system on $W^{\rm t}$ at $r_k$: the first-return pseudogroup on four small sets, generated by the finite word set of Proposition 4.4 of `sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`.

Its finiteness at level $k$ is equivalent to that of $GG_5(r_k)$ (Corollary 4.4). Its size is governed by the tight-core excursions, which at level 4 already have up to $1.8\cdot10^7$ lattice points.

## 8. Next steps

1. **Test period two at the deepest affordable level.** Compute level 6 for the 129 tight points that fit at level 5 (expected excursions $\sim5\cdot10^8$ points; needs a disk-backed or compressed hash), and compare with level 4. A single disagreement between levels 4 and 6 on a lune-free point would refute Conjecture 5.1 in its present form.
2. **Construct the period-two return relation directly at $r_c$.** By Conjecture 5.1 and the $\varphi^{-6}$-invariance of $O(E)$ near $E$, the natural object is the first-return relation of the $r_c$ orbit structure at $E$ under the homothety $\Psi_E^2$. One should try to prove $\Psi_E^2(O(E)\cap B(E,1))\subseteq O(E)$ exactly, by finding a finite set of words that conjugates the dynamics near $E$ at two consecutive even scales. This is the 2-D analogue of the golden induction on the chord, with step $\varphi^{-6}$.
3. **Tight-window annuli.** With windows only at the tight points, the referee's annulus scheme (`referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` §5, item 3) concerns one class and four points. Test structural stabilization of the restricted closures between window scales $\rho$ and $\varphi^{-6}\rho$ (period two), not $\varphi^{-3}\rho$.
4. **Hybrid certificate at level 3** (§6): the fixed tight-window certificate together with an exact closure of the induced system on $W^{\rm t}$ at $r_3$. This is the only visible way to extend the certified base beyond level $\approx1.3$ to the levels where Conjecture 5.1 could start.
5. **Certify Table 4.2.** Run the adapted interval checker on the remaining rows; the Lean port of relative certificates (in progress elsewhere) needs only a window-selection option.
6. **$n=8$.** The same rigidity computation (Table 4.1) should identify which of the 32 critical points of $n=8$ carry the infinite skeleton. The period two observed there fits §5.3.
