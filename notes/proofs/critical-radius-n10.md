# The critical radius of $GG_{10}$

Authors: Eric Vergo, Claude

**Statement.** Let $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/10}$, $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ and $r_c=\sqrt{4-\varphi}\approx1.5433619$, the paper's conjectured critical radius. The target theorem is:

> (A) $GG_{10}(r)$ is infinite for every $r\ge r_c$; and (B) $GG_{10}(r)$ is finite for every $r<r_c$.

**Status.**
- **(A) is proved.** Every point, word and disk test is checked in exact arithmetic in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ (§3). The mechanism is the one of the $n=5$ note, not the one of $n=8,12$. The line $\ell$ through $0$ in the direction $u=\zeta^4=e^{4\pi i/5}$ meets $D_1\cup D_2$, at $r=r_c$, in the segment $J=\{su:|s|\le\sqrt5\}$, whose endpoints $\pm\sqrt5\,u$ are lattice points on the boundary circles. Three words, $\mathtt{bbAB}$, $\mathtt{abAA}$ and $\mathtt{aaaBBB}$, act on three closed sub-segments of $J$ as translations and together induce the rotation $s\mapsto s+2\varphi^{-1}$ of the circle $\mathbb R/2\sqrt5\,\mathbb Z$. Its rotation number $1/(\varphi+2)$ is irrational, so the orbit of $0$ (indeed of every point of $J$) is infinite for every $r\ge r_c$ (Theorem A).
- **(B) is open.** It is proved only for $r\le r_\ast$, with $r_\ast^2=4-\varphi-\tfrac1{16}=\tfrac{63}{16}-\varphi$, i.e. $r_\ast\approx1.522979$ and $r_c-r_\ast\approx0.0204$, by an exact finite computation (§4, a cut-set certificate with 33 364 circles and 260 176 maximal arcs) combined with the refereed group monotonicity. What is proved on this range is the full group statement: $GG_{10}(r)$ is a finite group. The window $r_\ast<r<r_c$ is not proved.
- **Is $r_c$ the exact threshold?** What is proved is $r_\ast\le\inf\{r:GG_{10}(r)\text{ infinite}\}\le r_c$. The closed form is confirmed as an *upper bound* for the critical radius, and as the exact radius at which this mechanism switches on: each of the three words uses a lattice point that lies on a boundary circle exactly when $r=r_c$, and fails for every $r<r_c$. Whether some other mechanism already gives an infinite group at a radius in $(r_\ast,r_c)$ is not excluded; the evidence (§6) says no.
- **Relation to $n=5$** (§5). $GG_5(r)=\langle a^2,b^2\rangle\subseteq GG_{10}(r)$, and $GG_{10}(r)$ is generated by $GG_5(r)$ and the two half-turns $a^5,b^5$. At $r_c$ the subgroup $GG_5(r_c)$ is finite (an exact certificate is given, independent of the $n=5$ note), so the infinite orbit of Part A genuinely needs the odd powers. The Galois conjugacy $4-\varphi\leftrightarrow3+\varphi$ is explained at the level of the formula: both critical radii are the radius at which the lens corners $\pm i\sqrt{r^2-1}$ become the lattice points $\pm(\zeta_n-\zeta_n^{-1})$, i.e. $r^2=3-2\cos(4\pi/n)$, and $\zeta_5\mapsto\zeta_5^2$ carries $\cos(2\pi/5)$ to $\cos(4\pi/5)$. No map between the two *systems* was found, and a direct Galois transport of the $n=5$ construction fails. That the lens-corner radius is critical for both $n=5$ and $n=10$ is, on present knowledge, a coincidence of two separately verified mechanisms of the same shape.

Throughout, "verified exactly" means that every equality, and the sign of every real number used, is decided by integer and rational arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ or in towers $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)(\sqrt{H_1},\sqrt{H_2})$ with $H_i\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$. No floating-point decision enters any proof step. Floats are used only to search for the data that are then checked.

## 1. Setting and notation

- $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/10}=e^{i\pi/5}$, with minimal polynomial $\Phi_{10}(x)=x^4-x^3+x^2-x+1$. $\mathbb Z[\zeta]=\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$, since $\zeta=-\zeta_5^3$.
- $\varphi=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=2\cos(\pi/5)=1+\zeta^2-\zeta^3$, and $\sqrt5=2\varphi-1$.
- $D_1,D_2$ are the closed disks of radius $r$ centred at $-1$ and $+1$.
- $A(z)=-1+\zeta^{-1}(z+1)$ and $B(z)=1+\zeta^{-1}(z-1)$ are the global clockwise rotations by $2\pi/10$.
- $a=A$ on $D_1$ and $a=\mathrm{id}$ off $D_1$; $b=B$ on $D_2$ and $b=\mathrm{id}$ off $D_2$. $GG_{10}(r)=\langle a,b\rangle$.
- Inverses are written as capitals, $\mathtt A=a^{-1}$, $\mathtt B=b^{-1}$. **Words are read left to right**: $\mathtt{bbAB}$ applies $b$, $b$, $a^{-1}$, $b^{-1}$ in that order.

**Arithmetic.**
- Elements of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ are integer vectors $(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)$ meaning $\sum x_j\zeta^j$, reduced by $\zeta^4=\zeta^3-\zeta^2+\zeta-1$. Multiplication by $\zeta$ is $(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)\mapsto(-x_3,\,x_0+x_3,\,x_1-x_3,\,x_2+x_3)$, and $\zeta^{-1}=(1,-1,1,-1)$.
- A real element of $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ has the form $(\alpha+\beta,0,\beta,-\beta)=\alpha+\beta\varphi$. So $|w|^2=w\bar w$ is computed exactly as a pair $(\alpha,\beta)$.
- The sign of $\alpha+\beta\varphi=\tfrac12\big((2\alpha+\beta)+\beta\sqrt5\big)$ is decided by one comparison of $(2\alpha+\beta)^2$ with $5\beta^2$ when the two terms have opposite signs.
- Hence "$|z-c|\le r$" is decided exactly for $z,c\in\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ and $r^2\in\mathbb Q(\varphi)$. Here $r_c^2=4-\varphi$ is the pair $(4,-1)$.

**Lemma 1.1 (convexity).** Let $[x,y]$ be a segment, $g\in\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}\}$ with disk $D$, and suppose $x,y\in D$. Then $[x,y]\subseteq D$, and $g$ acts on $[x,y]$ as the global rotation. *Proof.* Disks are convex. $\square$

A word $g_1\cdots g_\ell$ is *valid* on a segment $\sigma$ if, for each $j$, the image of $\sigma$ under the global rotations of $g_1,\dots,g_{j-1}$ lies in the disk of $g_j$. By Lemma 1.1 it suffices to check this at the two endpoints, and then the word acts on $\sigma$ as the composite isometry $x\mapsto\zeta^kx+t$. A word valid on $\sigma$ at radius $r_0$ is valid on $\sigma$, with the same action, at every $r\ge r_0$, because the disks only grow.

## 2. Two facts used in Part B (already proved and refereed)

From `boundary-contact.md` (Theorems 3.1 and 3.4, Corollary 3.6) and `theorem1-converse.md` (Proposition 4.2), whose proofs do not depend on $n$:
- **Orbit monotonicity.** If $r'\le r$, then $O_{r'}(z)\subseteq O_r(z)$ for every $z$.
- **Group monotonicity.** If $GG_{10}(r)$ is finite, then $GG_{10}(r')$ is finite for every $r'\le r$.

So $\{r:GG_{10}(r)\text{ finite}\}$ is a down-set, and it suffices to certify finiteness at single radii. What is proved in Part B is the full group statement $|GG_{10}(r)|<\infty$.

## 3. Part A: $GG_{10}(r)$ is infinite for every $r\ge r_c$

### 3.1 The critical chord

Put $u=\zeta^4=e^{4\pi i/5}$ and let $\ell=\mathbb Ru$, the line through $0$ at angle $144^\circ$. Points of $\ell$ are written $su$, $s\in\mathbb R$; lattice points of $\ell$ have $s\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, and $s\in\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ gives a point of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$.

| point | $s$ | vector | numerical value | on which circle at $r_c$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $W'=-\sqrt5\,u=\sqrt5\,\zeta^{-1}$ | $-\sqrt5$ | $(1,1,-1,-1)$ | $1.80902-1.31433i$ | $\partial D_2$ |
| $P'=-P$ | $-\varphi^{-1}$ | $(0,1,-1,0)$ | $0.5-0.36327i$ | $\partial D_1$ |
| $0$ | $0$ | $(0,0,0,0)$ | $0$ | |
| $P=\varphi^{-1}u=\zeta^2-\zeta$ | $\varphi^{-1}$ | $(0,-1,1,0)$ | $-0.5+0.36327i$ | $\partial D_2$ |
| $U=u=\zeta^4$ | $1$ | $(-1,1,-1,1)$ | $-0.80902+0.58779i$ | (interior of $D_1$) |
| $W=\sqrt5\,u$ | $\sqrt5$ | $(-1,-1,1,1)$ | $-1.80902+1.31433i$ | $\partial D_1$ |
| $L=\zeta-\zeta^{-1}=2i\sin36^\circ$ (lens corner) | — | $(-1,2,-1,1)$ | $1.17557i$ | $\partial D_1$ and $\partial D_2$ |

Checked exactly (`t4_10/partA10.py`):
- $|W+1|^2=|W'-1|^2=4-\varphi$ and $|W-1|^2=|W'+1|^2=8+\varphi$;
- $|P-1|^2=|P'+1|^2=4-\varphi$ and $|P+1|^2=|P'-1|^2=2-\varphi=\varphi^{-2}$;
- $|L\pm1|^2=4-\varphi$, so $L$ and $-L$ are the lens corners $\pm i\sqrt{r^2-1}$ exactly at $r=r_c$.

**Lemma 3.1.** At $r=r_c$: $\ell\cap D_1=[P',W]$ ($s\in[-\varphi^{-1},\sqrt5]$), $\ell\cap D_2=[W',P]$ ($s\in[-\sqrt5,\varphi^{-1}]$), so $\ell\cap(D_1\cup D_2)=J:=[W',W]$ and $\ell\cap D_1\cap D_2=[P',P]$.

*Proof.* $|su+1|^2=s^2+2s\operatorname{Re}u+1=s^2-\varphi s+1$, so $su\in D_1$ iff $s^2-\varphi s+\varphi-3\le0$. The roots are $\sqrt5$ and $-\varphi^{-1}$: their sum is $\sqrt5-\varphi^{-1}=\varphi$ and their product is $-\sqrt5\varphi^{-1}=\varphi-3$. The statement for $D_2$ follows by the symmetry $z\mapsto-z$. $\square$

So $J$ is a chord of $D_1\cup D_2$ through $0$ with both endpoints lattice points on the boundary, exactly as the chord $E'E$ of the $n=5$ note; the difference is that $J$ is not contained in the lens.

**Lattice points on the boundary.** At $r=r_c$ there are exactly 20 lattice points on $\partial D_1$: $-1+\zeta^k(W+1)$ and $-1+\zeta^k\overline{(W+1)}$, $k\bmod10$. *Proof.* $w\bar w=4-\varphi$ has norm $11$ from $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$; $11\equiv1\pmod5$ splits completely in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$, which is a principal ideal domain, so $(w)$ is one of the two primes $\mathfrak P,\bar{\mathfrak P}$ above $(4-\varphi)$; two generators differ by a unit $v$ with $v\bar v=1$ at both places, which is a root of unity by Kronecker's theorem, i.e. one of $\zeta^k$. A brute-force search over $|x_j|\le4$ confirms the count. $\square$ The lens corner and $P'$ are among them: $L+1=\zeta^{-2}(W+1)$ (so $L=a^2(W)$ for $r\ge r_c$) and $P'+1=\zeta^3\,\overline{(W+1)}$. So "the lens corners are lattice points", "$W\in\partial D_1$" and "$P'\in\partial D_1$" are one and the same condition on $r$.

**Relation to the paper's Figure 5b.** The figure (inspected for this note, rendered from the PDF) shows the regular decagons of circumradius $\varphi^{-1}$ about $\pm1$ and dotted lines through $0$ joining the vertex $-1+\varphi^{-1}e^{\pm i\pi/5}$ of the left decagon to the opposite vertex of the right one; the right circle passes through the left decagon's vertices at $\pm36^\circ$. The upper vertex is exactly $-1+\varphi^{-1}\zeta=\zeta^2-\zeta=P$, and $|P-1|^2=4-4\varphi^{-1}\cos36^\circ+\varphi^{-2}=4-2+(2-\varphi)=4-\varphi$. So the figure's dotted line through $0$ is the lens part $[P',P]$ of the chord $J$, and the paper's "similar analysis" is the condition $P\in\partial D_2$. (A pixel measurement on the rendering gives a radius within about 1% of $r_c$; this is a consistency check only.)

### 3.2 The three words

**Lemma 3.2.** At $r=r_c$:

| word | segment of $J$ | action on $s$ | image segment |
|---|---|---|---|
| $w_1=\mathtt{bbAB}$ | $[W',P']$, $s\in[-\sqrt5,-\varphi^{-1}]$ | $s\mapsto s+2\varphi^{-1}$ | $s\in[-1,\varphi^{-1}]$ |
| $w_2=\mathtt{abAA}$ | $[P',U]$, $s\in[-\varphi^{-1},1]$ | $s\mapsto s+2\varphi^{-1}$ | $s\in[\varphi^{-1},\sqrt5]$ |
| $w_3=\mathtt{aaaBBB}$ | $[U,W]$, $s\in[1,\sqrt5]$ | $s\mapsto s-2\varphi$ | $s\in[-\sqrt5,-1]$ |

Each word is valid on its segment, its composite rotation is $\zeta^0$, and it acts as the stated translation along $\ell$ (by $2\varphi^{-1}u=2P$, respectively by $-2\varphi u=-(2+2\varphi)P$).

*Proof (exact computation, `t4_10/partA10.py`).*
1. Apply the letters left to right to both endpoints. Before each letter, check exactly that both current endpoints lie in the closed disk of that letter. By Lemma 1.1 the letter then acts on the whole current segment as the global rotation.
2. The exponents of $\zeta$ sum to $0$ in each word, so each composite is a translation.
3. The final endpoints equal the target endpoints exactly. The image endpoints are lattice points of $\ell$: $-\sqrt5+2\varphi^{-1}=-1$, $1+2\varphi^{-1}=\sqrt5$, $1-2\varphi=-\sqrt5$, $\sqrt5-2\varphi=-1$. $\square$

The trajectories are listed in Appendix A.1.

**All three words are tight at $r_c$.** In each word the largest squared distance from a current endpoint to the centre of the letter being applied is exactly $4-\varphi$. The boundary contacts (letter number, letter, point) are:
- $\mathtt{bbAB}$: $1\,b$ at $W'$; $2\,b$ at $(2,-1,-1,0)$; $3\,\mathtt A$ at $-L$ and at $(1,-1,1,0)$; $4\,\mathtt B$ at $L$;
- $\mathtt{abAA}$: $1\,a$ at $P'$; $2\,b$ at $-L$ and at $P$; $3\,\mathtt A$ at $L$; $4\,\mathtt A$ at $(-2,1,1,0)$;
- $\mathtt{aaaBBB}$: $1\,a$ at $W$; $2\,a$ at $(-2,1,1,0)$; $3\,a$ at $L$; $4\,\mathtt B$ at $(-1,1,-1,0)$; $5\,\mathtt B$ at $-L$; $6\,\mathtt B$ at $(2,-1,-1,0)$.

In particular every word passes through a lens corner, as for $n=5$. For every $r<r_c$ the first letter of each word is applied at a point at squared distance $4-\varphi>r^2$ from its centre, so each word fails on its segment (checked exactly at $r^2=4-\varphi-10^{-6}$; the general statement is immediate). Unlike $n=5$, the intermediate segments are not all inside the lens: each letter only needs its own disk.

### 3.3 The circle rotation and the infinite orbit

Define $T:[-\sqrt5,\sqrt5)\to[-\sqrt5,\sqrt5)$ by
$$T(s)=\begin{cases}s+2\varphi^{-1}, & -\sqrt5\le s<1,\\ s-2\varphi, & 1\le s<\sqrt5.\end{cases}$$
It is realized by $w_1$ on $[-\sqrt5,-\varphi^{-1}]$, by $w_2$ on $[-\varphi^{-1},1)$ and by $w_3$ on $[1,\sqrt5)$; at the common endpoint $-\varphi^{-1}$ both $w_1$ and $w_2$ give the same value. Since $2\varphi^{-1}+2\varphi=2\sqrt5$, $T$ is the rotation $s\mapsto s+2\varphi^{-1}\pmod{2\sqrt5}$ of the circle $\mathbb R/2\sqrt5\,\mathbb Z$, written on the fundamental domain $[-\sqrt5,\sqrt5)$. Its rotation number is $2\varphi^{-1}/2\sqrt5=1/(\varphi+2)=(5-\sqrt5)/10\approx0.27639$, with continued fraction $[0;3,1,1,1,\dots]$.

**Theorem A.** For every $r\ge r_c=\sqrt{4-\varphi}$, the orbit of $0$ under $GG_{10}(r)$ contains the pairwise distinct points $s_ku$, $s_k\equiv2k\varphi^{-1}\pmod{2\sqrt5}$, $s_k\in[-\sqrt5,\sqrt5)$, $k=0,1,2,\dots$ Hence the orbit of $0$ is infinite and $GG_{10}(r)$ is infinite.

*Proof.*
1. For $r\ge r_c$ every disk test of Lemma 3.2 still holds, because the disks only grow. So the words act on the three closed sub-segments exactly as at $r_c$.
2. Put $s_0=0$ and $s_{k+1}=T(s_k)$. Each $s_ku$ is the image of $0$ under the product of the $k$ words used.
3. $s_k=2k\varphi^{-1}-2m_k\sqrt5$ for integers $m_k$. If $s_k=s_l$ then $(k-l)\varphi^{-1}=(m_k-m_l)\sqrt5$; comparing rational parts in $\varphi^{-1}=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ gives $k=l$.
4. An infinite orbit forces an infinite group. $\square$

**Remarks.**
1. The whole chord $J$ is invariant under the three words and $T$ is minimal, so every point of $J$ has an infinite (dense in $J$) orbit at every $r\ge r_c$.
2. Only the closed-disk convention at boundary lattice points ($\pm W$, $\pm P$, $\pm L$ and the other contact points above) is used. All of them lie on a boundary circle exactly at $r=r_c$.
3. *Redundant check.* The program also follows 200 steps of $T$ from $0$ with the actual gated action of the group (not the global rotations) at $r^2=4-\varphi$, $r^2=13/3-\varphi$ and $r^2=9$, and obtains exactly $s_ku$ each time.
4. *Comparison with $n=5$.* There the chord $E'E$ has $E=\zeta_5-\zeta_5^2=\zeta_5(1-\zeta_5)$, and the induced map is the rotation by $2\varphi^{-1}$ on a circle of length $2$ (in units of $|E|$), rotation number $\varphi^{-1}$. Here the chord has direction $P=\zeta^2-\zeta=-\zeta(1-\zeta)$, the same expression $\pm\zeta_n(1-\zeta_n)$, i.e. the chord is parallel to a side of the regular $n$-gon in both cases; the translation is again $2\varphi^{-1}$ (in absolute units), on a circle of length $2\sqrt5$. Both rotation numbers have continued fractions ending in $1,1,1,\dots$, so both maps have the golden (period-one) Rauzy renormalization with scale $\varphi$.

## 4. Part B: finiteness for $r\le r_\ast$

### 4.1 The cut-set certificate

The certificate is Theorem 4.2 of `critical-radius-n5.md` (restated as Theorem 3.1 of `critical-radius-n8.md`). Its proof does not depend on $n$; it was re-read for this note and is restated here in the form used.

**Theorem 4.1.** Fix $r>1$. Let $K\subset\mathbb C$ be a finite union of non-degenerate closed arcs of circles of radius $r$ such that:
- **(C1)** $\partial D_1\cup\partial D_2\subseteq K$;
- **(C2)** $A^{\pm1}(K\cap D_1)\subseteq K$ and $B^{\pm1}(K\cap D_2)\subseteq K$;
- **(C3)** no circle carrying an arc of $K$, other than $\partial D_p$ itself, is tangent to $\partial D_p$ ($p=\pm1$);
- **(C4)** every endpoint of a maximal arc of $K$ (a component of $K\cap C$ for a circle $C$) that lies on $\partial D_p$ is an endpoint of a maximal arc entering $\operatorname{int}D_p$ there.

Then $GG_{10}(r)$ is finite.

*Proof (summary; details as in the $n=5$ and $n=8$ notes).*
1. (C2) for $A$ and $A^{-1}$ gives $a(K)=K$; likewise $b(K)=K$.
2. The set $V$ of non-regular points of $K$ is finite and $a(V)=V$, $b(V)=V$. At a point $x\in\partial D_1\cap V$ some arc of $K$ other than $\partial D_1$ meets $x$; by (C3) it is transversal, and by (C4) (if it ends at $x$) some arc of $K$ enters $\operatorname{int}D_1$ at $x$. Its image under $A$ is an arc of $K$ meeting $\partial D_1$ at $A(x)$ from inside, so $A(x)\in V$.
3. The points of $V$, the components of $K\setminus V$ and the components of $\mathbb C\setminus K$ are finitely many cells partitioning $\mathbb C$. By (C1) each face lies in $\operatorname{int}D_p$ or outside $D_p$, and an edge not on $\partial D_p$ does not meet $\partial D_p$. So each generator acts on each cell by one isometry, mapping it onto a cell.
4. Every $g\in GG_{10}(r)$ therefore acts on each cell $X$ by one map $x\mapsto\zeta^jx+t$ onto a cell. By (C1) every face lies inside or outside each disk, and a face outside both disks (in particular the unbounded one) is fixed pointwise by every generator; this does not need $K\subseteq D_1\cup D_2$, which holds by construction but is not part of the check. For a bounded cell inside $D_1\cup D_2$ and a target cell there are at most $10$ such maps (one $t$ per $j$, since a non-trivial translation preserves no bounded set). So there are finitely many $g$. $\square$

### 4.2 The computation

**Programs.** `t4_10/cut/cut.py` (closure) and `t4_10/cut/verify.py` (certificate check), with the exact field module `t4_10/cut/field.py`. They are an adaptation to $n=10$ of the $n=8,12$ programs of `critical-radius-n8.md` §3.3 (`t4_8/cut/`), which were copied, not modified. Input: a rational $\delta$, with $r^2=\rho=4-\varphi-\delta$.

**What had to change for $n=10$.** For $n=8,12$ the real and imaginary parts of cyclotomic numbers lie in the real quadratic field. For $n=10$ the imaginary part of every element of $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ is $\sin36^\circ$ times an element of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$: $\operatorname{Im}\zeta=\sin36^\circ$ and $\operatorname{Im}\zeta^2=\operatorname{Im}\zeta^3=\varphi\sin36^\circ$. The programs therefore work in the plane with coordinates $(x,\,y/\sin36^\circ)$.
- This vertical scaling is linear with positive determinant, so it preserves the half-plane of a vector and the sign of a cross product, hence the cyclic (angular) order of points on each circle. That order is all the closure and the verification use.
- The arc endpoint symbol $(e,s)$ on $\operatorname{circle}(c)$ is the point $c+\tfrac d2(1+ish)$ with $d=e-c$, $h=\sqrt{4\rho/|d|^2-1}$, as before. Writing $d=d_x+i\sin36^\circ\,d_y$ with $d_x,d_y\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ and $g=h\sin36^\circ$, the scaled vector from $c$ to the endpoint is
$$X=\tfrac{d_x}2-\tfrac s2\,d_y\,g,\qquad Y=\tfrac{d_y}2+\tfrac{s}{2\sin^2 36^\circ}\,d_x\,g,\qquad g^2=\sin^2 36^\circ\Big(\frac{4\rho}{|d|^2}-1\Big)\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt5),$$
with $\sin^236^\circ=(5-\sqrt5)/8$. So each endpoint involves one square root of an element of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, exactly as for $n=8$, and the angular comparison of two endpoints is the sign of an element of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)(\sqrt{H_1},\sqrt{H_2})$, decided by two squarings.
- A self-test (`t4_10/cut/test_cut.py`) compares the exact real and imaginary parts, norms and rotations with floating point on random elements, and 20 464 exact angular comparisons of random endpoint symbols with the floating-point angles; all agree.
- *Tangency* $|c-p|^2=4\rho$ with $|c-p|^2\in\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ and $4\rho=(14-4\delta)-2\sqrt5$ is impossible when $8\delta\notin\mathbb Z$; it would abort the run in any case.

**Closure and verification** are as in `critical-radius-n8.md` §3.3:
1. start from the full circles $\partial D_1,\partial D_2$; repeatedly clip each new arc by $D_1$ and $D_2$, rotate the clipped parts by $A^{\pm1}$, respectively $B^{\pm1}$, and add the parts of the images not yet covered; stop when nothing new appears. During the closure only, angular comparisons whose float angles differ by more than $10^{-9}$ are decided by floats. To bound memory, the cache of exact endpoint data is evicted when it exceeds a fixed size and recomputed on demand, and the float angles are cached separately; neither affects any exact decision;
2. `verify.py` then re-checks the stored result with no float decision: it merges the pieces into maximal arcs by exact endpoint equality, checks (C1), recomputes every clipped image of every maximal arc and checks exactly that it lies inside a single maximal arc of the target circle (C2), checks (C3) through the tangency test, and checks (C4) by requiring the maximal arc itself to enter $D_p$ at each endpoint on $\partial D_p$. Floats only propose which target arc to test first.

**Independent consistency check (not part of the proof).** A separate floating-point closure (`t4_10/arcs_float10.py`, adapted from the $n=5$ program `t4/arcs_float.py`, which stores arcs as float angle intervals and merges numerically) gives the same number of circles at every $\delta$ where both were run: 318, 988, 5 986, 6 058, 11 722, 33 040, 33 364 at $\delta=1/2,1/3,1/5,1/7,1/10,1/14,1/16$. The exact closures at $\delta=1/2,\dots,1/10$ were run twice, with identical output; the runs at $1/14$ and $1/16$ used the version with the bounded cache, which reproduces the $\delta=1/5$ result exactly.

### 4.4 Why the computation does not reach $r_c$

- The size of $K(r)$ grows roughly like a power of $\delta^{-1}$: from $\delta=1/10$ to $1/14$ the piece count grows by a factor of $5.1$, and from $1/14$ to $1/16$ by $1.44$. Intermediate values such as $\delta=1/18$ were not tried. At $\delta=0.03$ the floating-point closure did not finish within its cap of 900 s (142 212 circles, $1.3\cdot10^6$ arcs at the cap).
- The internal extent $\max|\sigma(c)|$ grows roughly like $\delta^{-1}$ ($\delta\cdot\max|\sigma(c)|=6.4,\,7.8,\,10.2,\,7.3,\,8.0,\,8.4$ for $\delta=1/2,1/3,1/5,1/7,1/10,1/14$, and $7.3$ at $1/16$), matching the orbit data in `notes/engine.md`.

## 5. Relation to $n=5$

### 5.1 $GG_5$ inside $GG_{10}$

- $a^2$ is the rotation by $\zeta^{-2}=\zeta_5^{-1}$ about $-1$ on $D_1$, i.e. the $n=5$ generator. So $GG_5(r)=\langle a^2,b^2\rangle\subseteq GG_{10}(r)$ for every $r$.
- $a^5$ is the half-turn $s_1:z\mapsto-2-z$ on $D_1$ (identity outside), and $a=(a^2)^3(a^5)^{-1}$. So $GG_{10}(r)=\langle GG_5(r),s_1,s_2\rangle$ with $s_2=b^5$ the half-turn on $D_2$.
- **$GG_5(r)$ is finite for $r^2\le4-\varphi+\tfrac1{10}$**, in particular at $r=r_c(10)$. This follows from the $n=5$ note (Theorem B there covers $r\le2.1256$). It was also re-proved here independently: running the same closure with the generators $a^2,b^2$ (program `cut5.py`, identical except that the rotations are by $\zeta^{\mp2}$) at $r^2=4-\varphi+1/10$ terminates with 54 circles and 112 pieces, merged into 64 maximal arcs, and `verify.py` checks (C1)–(C4) exactly (164 image arcs, 60 boundary endpoints, 0 failures). At exactly $r^2=4-\varphi$ the $n=5$ closure meets a tangency, which is why a slightly larger radius was used; group monotonicity gives the radius $r_c(10)$.
- Consequently the infinite orbit of Theorem A is **not** produced by $GG_5$: at $r_c(10)$ every $GG_5$-orbit is finite. The three words $\mathtt{bbAB}=b^2a^{-1}b^{-1}$, $\mathtt{abAA}=a\,b\,a^{-2}$ and $\mathtt{aaaBBB}=a^3b^{-3}$ each contain odd powers. (A word with odd exponents can still represent an element of $GG_5(r)$ at a given radius; the argument that they do not all do so is the finiteness of $GG_5(r_c(10))$.)
- The geometry is also different: in the $n=5$ construction every intermediate segment lies in the lens, while here the segments leave the lens and use the full disks.

### 5.2 The Galois conjugacy $4-\varphi\leftrightarrow3+\varphi$

**What is proved.**
1. Both critical radii are lens-corner radii. The lens corners are $\pm i\sqrt{r^2-1}$, and they are the lattice points $\pm(\zeta_n-\zeta_n^{-1})=\pm2i\sin(2\pi/n)$ exactly when
$$r^2=\rho_n:=1+4\sin^2(2\pi/n)=3-2\cos(4\pi/n).$$
For $n=5$, $\rho_5=3+\varphi$; for $n=10$, $\rho_{10}=3-2\cos72^\circ=4-\varphi$. In both cases the proved mechanism (the $n=5$ note §3, and §3 here) has every word passing through a lens corner, and the mechanism switches on exactly at $\rho_n$.
2. For every odd $m$, $\rho_{2m}=3-2\cos(2\pi/m)$ and $\rho_m=3-2\cos(4\pi/m)$ are Galois conjugate, by the automorphism $\zeta_m\mapsto\zeta_m^2$ of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2m})$. For $m=5$ this automorphism is $\sqrt5\mapsto-\sqrt5$, and it maps $3+\varphi$ to $4-\varphi$.

So the conjugacy follows from the formula $r_c(n)^2=\rho_n$, which holds for $n=5$ and $n=10$ in the sense of Part A (and of the paper's conjecture for (B)).

**What is not explained.** Why $\rho_n$ is the critical radius for $n=5$ and $10$, and for no other $n$ in the paper's table. For $m=7$: $\rho_7=3-2\cos(4\pi/7)\approx3.445$ against $r_c(7)^2\approx1.6236^2\approx2.636$, and $\rho_{14}\approx1.753$ against $r_c(14)^2\approx1.1966^2\approx1.432$; for $n=8,9,12$ also $\rho_n>r_c(n)^2$. So in these cases $\rho_n$ exceeds the table value of $r_c(n)^2$, and the equality $r_c(n)^2=\rho_n$ is special to $n=5,10$.

**Search for a structural map (negative).**
1. *Galois transport.* Each of the three $n=5$ words (with its points $E',F',G',E$) was transported by every embedding $\zeta_5\mapsto\zeta_{10}^k$, $k=2,4,6,8$, with each letter replaced by its $m$-th power, $m=1,\dots,9$, and tested for validity in $GG_{10}(r_c)$. None is valid. This is expected: a Galois automorphism with $\sqrt5\mapsto-\sqrt5$ does not preserve the signs of the disk tests, since it exchanges the physical and internal places of the cut-and-project picture (`critical-radius-n5.md` §4.1).
2. *Self-conjugacy of the systems.* Under $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^3$ the ungated group generated by $A,B$ goes to the group generated by the rotations by $\zeta^{\mp3}$, and $\langle a^3\rangle=\langle a\rangle$ as gated maps, because $\gcd(3,10)=1$ and the gating disk is the same. Likewise for $n=5$ and $\zeta_5\mapsto\zeta_5^2$. So each family is mapped to itself, not to the other, and the conjugation relates the physical place of one system to the internal place of the *same* system. No isomorphism between the $n=10$ system at one place and the $n=5$ system at the other was found.
3. *Shape of the mechanisms.* The two constructions are of the same type: a chord through $0$, parallel to a side of the regular $n$-gon, with lattice endpoints on the boundary; three words; a rotation by $2\varphi^{-1}$ (rotation numbers $\varphi^{-1}$ and $1/(\varphi+2)$, both golden). But the chords, words and lengths are different, and neither construction maps to the other by a similarity or a Galois automorphism.

**Assessment.** The conjugacy $4-\varphi\leftrightarrow3+\varphi$ is a consequence of the lens-corner formula, which is elementary. That the lens-corner radius is the critical radius in both cases is, as far as this note can tell, a coincidence of two separately verified mechanisms of the same shape. A structural explanation would have to explain why the lens-corner chord construction closes up for $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ and not for other fields; none is known.

## 6. Is the closed form exactly the critical radius?

- **Proved:** $r_\ast\le\inf\{r:GG_{10}(r)\text{ infinite}\}\le r_c$, with $r_\ast$ from Theorem B. In the notation of `boundary-contact.md` (Corollary 3.6), $r_\ast\le r_c(GG_{10})\le r_c^{\rm orb}\le r^\ast(0)\le\sqrt{4-\varphi}$, and the orbit of $0$ is infinite at $r=\sqrt{4-\varphi}$ itself (the paper's footnote 4, for this $n$).
- **Proved:** $\sqrt{4-\varphi}$ is exactly the radius at which the Part A mechanism switches on.
- **Not proved:** that no point has an infinite orbit for $r\in(r_\ast,r_c)$. The closed form is confirmed as an upper bound, not as the infimum.
- **Evidence (exact orbit computations with the repository's engine `tdcsg-orbit`, run read-only; not proofs):**
  - the orbit of $0$ is finite with 821 points at $\delta=1/10$ and 182 159 points at $\delta=1/100$, and reaches the cap of $5\cdot10^6$ at $\delta=1/1000$;
  - the orbit of $P$ is finite with 102 436 points at $\delta=1/100$ and 1 552 440 points at $\delta=1/300$;
  - the cut-set closure terminates at every $\delta$ tried, exact or floating point (§4);
  - the engine's threshold sweep (`notes/engine.md` §(i)) shows thresholds accumulating only at $r_c$, with gap ratios $4.2097,4.2299\to\varphi^3$.

## 7. Renormalization data

1. **Scale unit.** $\varepsilon=\varphi^{-3}=2\varphi-3$ (so $\varepsilon^{-1}=\varphi^3=2+\sqrt5$), a unit of $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$, as for $n=5$. Measured: threshold-gap ratios $4.2097,4.2299$ and orbit-size multipliers $15.3,15.4$ per period (`notes/engine.md`).
2. **One-dimensional critical dynamics.** The rotation of §3.3 has rotation number $1/(\varphi+2)=[0;3,1,1,\dots]$. After one step of the Gauss map ($1/(\varphi+2)\mapsto\{\varphi+2\}=\varphi^{-1}$) it is the golden rotation, whose first-return maps reproduce themselves with scale $\varphi$. Three such steps give $\varphi^3$.
3. **Critical geometry.** At $r_c$ the boundary circles pass through the 20 lattice points per circle listed in §3.1, among them the chord ends $\pm W$, the decagon vertices $\pm P$ and the lens corners $\pm L$. Every word of §3.2 touches a boundary circle at such points.
4. **Conjecture R₁₀.** As in `critical-radius-n5.md` §6 (Conjecture R), with scale $\varphi^3$: the combinatorial type of $K(r)$ near the accumulation points, for $r^2\in[r_c^2-\delta_k,r_c^2-\delta_{k+1}]$, reproduces itself under a similarity of ratio $\varphi^{-3}$. Together with one level of Table 4.1 this would prove (B). The curvature obstruction of `critical-radius-n5.md` §6.4 applies verbatim.

**Gaps and caveats, from my own adversarial check.**
1. The cut-set programs are an adaptation of programs written by another session for $n=8,12$; the $n=10$ changes (the field module and the scaled coordinate) were written and run by the author of this note. The scaled-coordinate argument is elementary, and the self-test and the agreement of circle counts with the independent float program are reassuring, but neither is a proof. An independent re-run of the closure is needed to check Theorem B.
2. In `verify.py` pieces are merged into maximal arcs by exact endpoint equality; overlapping pieces would not be merged. That can only make (C2) and (C4) harder to pass, never easier.
3. Theorem A uses only the closed-disk convention at boundary lattice points. With open disks the construction fails at $r_c$ and holds for every $r>r_c$.
4. Part A proves an infinite orbit of one explicit point; it proves nothing about the structure of $GG_{10}(r)$ beyond infinitude.
5. The inspection of Figure 5b is visual; the identification of its dotted line with $[P',P]$ rests on the exact identity $|P-1|^2=4-\varphi$, not on the picture.
6. Nothing here shows that $r_c$ is the critical radius of the group; that requires (B) on $(r_\ast,r_c)$.

## 9. What a Lean formalization would need

1. **Arithmetic.** $\mathbb Z[\zeta_{10}]$ as $\mathbb Z^4$ with the reduced multiplication, conjugation and the map to $\mathbb C$; exact sign decision in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ (and, for Part B, in quadratic extensions $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)(\sqrt H)$). The identity $\operatorname{Re}\zeta=\varphi/2$ is the key real fact.
2. **Generators and words.** Generators as `Equiv.Perm ℂ` from closed disks; left-to-right word action; Lemma 1.1 from `Convex.segment_subset` and `convex_closedBall`.
3. **Part A** (the most formalizable part):
   - the six points $\pm\sqrt5u$, $\pm\varphi^{-1}u$, $\pm u$ (segment endpoints and their images) and the three words, with $2\times(4+4+6)=28$ disk tests, each an inequality $\alpha+\beta\varphi\ge0$ with small integers, by `norm_num` on $\sqrt5$;
   - the three translation identities in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$, by `decide` or `ring` on the integer data;
   - injectivity of $k\mapsto2k\varphi^{-1}\bmod2\sqrt5$, from `irrational_sqrt_five` (rational parts);
   - "infinite orbit $\Rightarrow$ infinite group".
   The statement is compact: for $r\ge\sqrt{4-\varphi}$, the orbit of $0$ under $GG_{10}(r)$ is infinite.
4. **Part B, initial range.** Theorem 4.1 (cell decomposition of a finite arc arrangement, the main cost), the certificate data of §4, a verified checker for (C1)–(C4) with exact two-radical sign tests, and group monotonicity from `boundary-contact.md`.
5. **Part B, full.** Conjecture R₁₀ first, then an induction on the level with explicit control of the curvature correction.
