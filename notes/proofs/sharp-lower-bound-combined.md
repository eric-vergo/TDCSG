# Finiteness of $GG_5(r)$ below $\sqrt{3+\varphi}$: a computed bulk bound at the critical radius, and the reduction to the critical windows

Authors: Eric Vergo, Claude

**Status.** Let $r_c=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$. Infiniteness of $GG_5(r)$ for $r\ge r_c$ is proved and Lean-checked (`critical-radius-n5.md`, Theorem A). **The converse, (B): $GG_5(r)$ finite for every $r<r_c$, is still open, so $r_{\rm crit}(5)=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$ is not proved by this note. Nor is the corresponding equality for $n=8,10,12$.** The note has been independently refereed: `referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-combined.md`. Its verdicts are recorded in §8. What the note adds:

1. **A bound on excursions, uniform in $r$ (proved, conditional on computations).**
   - Let $W$ be a union of small closed windows about the 38 lattice points of $\partial D_1\cup\partial D_2$ at $r_c$, each bounded by four arcs of radius $r_c$.
   - Fix one such $W$ for which a *restricted closure* (Definition 4.1) exists. Then there is $\beta_W$ such that, **for every $r<r_c$ at once**, every chain whose intermediate points avoid $W$ has at most $\beta_W$ endpoints (Theorem 4.2).
   - The closure is one computation at the critical radius itself. There $3+\varphi$ is a norm, so tangencies and triple points occur, and they are decided exactly.
   - Restricted closures were computed for nine window sizes, with circumradius from $0.292$ down to $0.0166$ (Table 5.1). The status differs by row:
     - the largest window, $w=(0,2,-1,0)$, is **certified** by the referee's independent interval-arithmetic checker;
     - the next two, $w=(5,-1,4,0)$ and $(-3,4,-4,0)$, are **computed** by two independent floating-point programs;
     - the six smaller ones are **computed** by one program only.
2. **(B) reduces to the windows (proved).**
   - For every $r<r_c$ and every $z$, $|O_r(z)|\le\beta_W\max(1,\sup_x|O_r(x)\cap W|)$. So, for the certified window, $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if the orbits meet $W$ in uniformly boundedly many points (Corollary 4.3).
   - The dynamics induced on $W$ is generated by one fixed finite set of first-return words, each with its itinerary, the same for all $r<r_c$.
   - Near $r_c$ its domains have constant combinatorics; they only move (Propositions 4.4, 4.5).
3. **A sufficient pair of statements (proved to imply (B), but stronger than (B)).** Proposition 6.3 shows that (B) follows from:
   - **(R-bulk)**, about the critical system alone: restricted orbits at $r_c$ are bounded for windows of every size, along a sequence of window sizes tending to $0$;
   - **(R-core)**: at each level $k$, orbits visit the level-scale windows $\bigcup_P B(P,C\varepsilon^k)$ boundedly often.

   Exact evidence for a *uniform* form of (R-core): in the orbits computed, the maximal number of core visits per orbit is $1578,1352,1546$ at levels $k=1,2,3$. Over the same levels the largest orbit grows from $7.2\cdot10^4$ to $1.5\cdot10^7$ points.
4. **Refuted, with an exact counterexample checked by three implementations:** the claim that the orbit partition of the normalized core points is level-independent, i.e. that level $k+1$ is exactly the $\varphi^{-3}$-rescaled copy of level $k$. It fails between levels 2 and 3 (§6.3). On the core points that satisfy the hypothesis of the Diophantine lune lemma, it does hold at the levels tested.

Terms introduced here: a *window* (§3), a *strict chain* (Definition 3.1), a *restricted orbit* (Definition 3.2), a *restricted closure* (Definition 4.1).

**Exactness.** Disk tests, tangencies and coincidences of points are decided exactly in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$. Orderings of distinct points along a circle are not exact:
- `rcut.c` uses double precision with a per-symbol bound of $5\cdot10^{-15}$ inherited from `kcut4.c`. The bound is plausible but not derived, and it trusts libm `atan2` (`referee-report-sharp-lower-bounds.md`, issue 1).
- `chk.py` uses 40-digit `mpmath` floating point with no error analysis (§5.1).
- The referee's checker uses `mpmath.iv` interval enclosures. Overlaps are resolved by an exact coincidence test.

## 1. Setting

As in `critical-radius-n5.md` §1 and `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` §1:
- $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$, $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$, $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and $\sigma:\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2$ is the internal embedding;
- $D_1,D_2$ are the closed disks of radius $r$ about $\mp1$;
- $A,B$ are the rotations by $\zeta^{-1}$ about $-1,+1$; $a=A$ on $D_1$ and $a=\mathrm{id}$ elsewhere, likewise $b$; $GG_5(r)=\langle a,b\rangle$;
- $\varepsilon=\varphi^{-3}$; the levels are $r_k^2=3+\varphi-\varepsilon^k/10$.

A *chain* at radius $r$ is a sequence $z_0,\dots,z_L$ with $z_i=s_i(z_{i-1})$, $s_i\in\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}\}$. $O_r(z)$ is the orbit.

Refereed facts used:
- orbit and group monotonicity in $r$ (`boundary-contact.md`, Theorems 3.1, 3.4);
- a finitely generated group of bijections with uniformly bounded orbits is finite (`theorem1-converse.md`, Proposition 4.2).

So (B) holds as soon as $GG_5(r_k)$ is finite for every $k\ge k_0$.

**Certified base.** $GG_5(r)$ is finite for $r^2\le3+\varphi-1/55$. This is the Lean-engine certificate checked by the float-free checker (`../certificates.md`), and it covers level one. The level-two closure of `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` is computed, not certified, and is not used here.

## 2. The critical lattice points (proved)

**Lemma 2.1.**
1. The solutions of $|w|^2=3+\varphi$ with $w\in\Lambda$ are exactly $20$ elements: $\pm\zeta^j\pi$ and $\pm\zeta^j\bar\pi$, with $\pi$ a prime of $\Lambda$ above $3+\varphi$.
2. The lattice points on $\partial D_1(r_c)\cup\partial D_2(r_c)$ are the $38$ points $p+w$ ($p=\pm1$). The two lens corners $\pm(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})$ lie on both circles; note $|\zeta-\zeta^{-1}|^2=2+\varphi=r_c^2-1$.
3. Under the symmetry group $\{z,-z,\bar z,-\bar z\}$ of the system they form $10$ classes: one of size 2 and nine of size 4 (Table 2.1). The tight points $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ of the critical words form **one** class, and the lens corners another.
4. A lattice-centred circle of radius $r_c$ is tangent to $\partial D_p(r_c)$ only if its centre is $p+2w$. The point of tangency is then the critical point $p+w$. Since every circle of a restricted closure has a lattice centre, this covers all of them.
5. A lattice point $y$ lies on a lattice-centred circle of radius $r_c$ about $c$ iff $y-c$ is one of the $20$ elements of item 1.

*Proof.*
1. $N_{\mathbb Q(\varphi)/\mathbb Q}(3+\varphi)=11$ is prime and $11\equiv1\pmod5$, so $(3+\varphi)$ splits in $\Lambda$ as $\mathfrak p\bar{\mathfrak p}$ with $\mathfrak p\ne\bar{\mathfrak p}$. $\Lambda$ is a principal ideal domain. If $w\bar w=3+\varphi$, then $(w)\in\{\mathfrak p,\bar{\mathfrak p}\}$, so $w=u\pi$ or $w=u\bar\pi$ with $u\bar u=1$. Every conjugate of $u$ then has modulus 1, so $u$ is a root of unity, $\pm\zeta^j$ (Kronecker). That gives exactly 20 elements. As a confirmation, a coefficient search finds exactly these 20 (`zz.py`; the referee searched $[-8,8]^4$).
2. Translate item 1. Exactly two points lie on both circles, the lens corners.
3. Exact enumeration (`zz.py`; re-done by the referee).
4. The tangency is external, with $|c-p|^2=4(3+\varphi)$, so $2\mid d\bar d$ for $d=c-p$. Since $2$ is prime in $\Lambda$ and $\bar d$ is the conjugate of $d$, this gives $2\mid d$. So $d=2w$ with $|w|^2=3+\varphi$.
5. Immediate from item 1. $\square$

## 3. Windows, strict chains and restricted orbits

**Windows.** Fix $w\in\Lambda$ with $|w|<r_c$, and put
$$W_0=\bigcap_{v\in\{w,-w,\bar w,-\bar w\}}D(v,r_c),\qquad W_P=P+W_0,\qquad W=\bigcup_{P\ \rm critical}W_P .$$
- $W_0$ is a compact convex neighbourhood of $0$, close to a square whose sides are arcs of radius $r_c$.
- Its inradius is $r_c-|w|$. Its circumradius $\rho_W$ is attained at the corners.
- $W$ is invariant under $z\mapsto-z$ and $z\mapsto\bar z$, so the system with $W$ removed keeps its symmetry.
- For every window used, $2\rho_W<\varphi^{-1}$, so the 38 windows are pairwise disjoint (Table 5.1, `wingeo.py`).

Theorem 4.2 does not use the radius of the window arcs. Only the programs do.

**Definition 3.1 (strict chains at $r_c$).** A *strict chain* is a sequence $z_0,\dots,z_L$ with $z_i=S_i(z_{i-1})$ for some $S_i\in\{A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}\}$ and $z_{i-1}$ in the **open** disk of $S_i$ of radius $r_c$.

**Definition 3.2 (restricted orbits).** For $W\subseteq\mathbb C$:
- $R^{(r)}_W(x)$ is the set of endpoints of chains at radius $r$ from $x$ whose intermediate points $z_1,\dots,z_{L-1}$ avoid $W$;
- $R^*_W(x)$ is the same set for strict chains;
- $\beta^{(r)}(W)=\sup_x|R^{(r)}_W(x)|$ and $\beta^*(W)=\sup_x|R^*_W(x)|$.

If $W\subseteq W'$, then $\beta^*(W')\le\beta^*(W)$: avoiding a larger set is a stronger condition.

**Lemma 3.3 (every chain below $r_c$ is a strict chain).** For $r<r_c$ and every $W$, $R^{(r)}_W(x)\subseteq R^*_W(x)$. Hence $\beta^{(r)}(W)\le\beta^*(W)$ for every $r<r_c$.

*Proof.* Delete the trivial steps of an $r$-chain. Each remaining step applies a rotation at a point of the closed disk of radius $r$, which lies in the open disk of radius $r_c$. Each remaining intermediate point lies strictly between two non-trivial steps, so it is one of the original intermediate points. $\square$

A point of the lune $D_p(r_c)\setminus D_p(r)$ is fixed by the generator at radius $r$. That is a deleted trivial step, so no comparison of the two systems' partitions is needed.

At $r_c$ itself the closed-disk dynamics has degenerate itineraries through the tight points; Theorem A rests on them. Strict chains avoid them. $\bigcup_{r<r_c}O_r(x)$ is exactly the strict orbit of $x$.

**Lemma 3.4 (generic points suffice).** Let $W$ be closed and let $K'$ be a finite union of circular arcs. Call $x$ *generic* if $h(x)\notin K'$ for every map $h:z\mapsto\zeta^jz+t$ with $t\in\Lambda$. Then $\beta^*(W)=\sup\{|R^*_W(x)|:x\text{ generic}\}$, and generic points are dense.

*Proof.* Being a strict chain with intermediate points outside $W$ is an open condition on the start point: the disks are open and the complement of $W$ is open. Take finitely many endpoints of strict chains from $x$. They are distinct images $g_i(x)$ under distinct isometries $g_i$. For $x'$ close to $x$ the same chains exist, and the $g_i(x')$ are still distinct. Generic points are dense by Baire's theorem: their complement is a countable union of nowhere dense closed sets $h^{-1}(K')$. $\square$

## 4. Restricted closures (proved)

**Definition 4.1.** Let $W$ be a finite union of pairwise disjoint compact convex sets $W_P$, each bounded by finitely many circular arcs. A *restricted closure* is a finite union $K'$ of closed arcs (and circles) of radius $r_c$ such that:
1. $\partial D_1\cup\partial D_2\cup\partial W\subseteq K'$, with the disks taken at radius $r_c$;
2. for every arc $\alpha$ of $K'$ and every $S\in\{A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}\}$, every non-degenerate component of $\alpha\cap D_S\setminus\operatorname{int}W$ is mapped by $S$ into $K'$. Here $D_S$ is the closed disk of radius $r_c$ on which $S$ acts.

Isolated points need not be checked; the proof below shows why.

**Theorem 4.2 (relative certificate).** Suppose a restricted closure $K'$ exists. Let $F$ be the number of bounded faces of $\mathbb C\setminus K'$ that are not contained in $W$. Then $\beta^*(W)\le1+100F$, and consequently $\beta^{(r)}(W)\le1+100F$ for every $r<r_c$.

No tangency or endpoint conditions like (C3) and (C4) of the certificate theorem are needed:
- strict chains act on open disks, and generic points never lie on $K'$ (Lemma 3.4);
- tangency points lie on $\partial D_S$, never in $\operatorname{int}D_S$. This is why a one-point intersection may be given no image.

*Proof.* Since $\partial W\subseteq K'$, every face (component of $\mathbb C\setminus K'$) lies in $\operatorname{int}W$ or in $\mathbb C\setminus W$. Since $\partial D_S\subseteq K'$, every face lies in $\operatorname{int}D_S$ or misses $D_S$.

*Step 1: faces outside $W$ map to faces outside $W$, or into $\operatorname{int}W$.* Let $X$ be a face in $\mathbb C\setminus W$ with $X\subseteq\operatorname{int}D_S$.
- Suppose some $y\in S(X)$ lies in $K'\setminus\operatorname{int}W$. Then $y\in\operatorname{int}D_S$.
- If $y$ lies in a non-degenerate component of $\alpha\cap D_S\setminus\operatorname{int}W$ for some arc $\alpha$ of $K'$, apply condition 2 with $S^{-1}$, which acts on the same disk. It gives $S^{-1}(y)\in K'$. But $S^{-1}(y)\in X$, which misses $K'$: a contradiction.
- If $y\notin W$, every arc of $K'$ through $y$ gives such a component.
- If $y\in\partial W$, then a boundary arc of the window $W_P$ containing $y$ gives one. It lies in $K'$ and avoids $\operatorname{int}W$, because the windows are disjoint and convex, and near $y$ it lies in $\operatorname{int}D_S$.
- Hence the connected open set $S(X)$ misses $K'\setminus\operatorname{int}W\supseteq\partial W$. So either $S(X)\subseteq\operatorname{int}W$, or $S(X)$ lies in a single face $Y\subseteq\mathbb C\setminus W$.
- In the second case, the same argument for $S^{-1}$ on $Y$ gives $S^{-1}(Y)\subseteq X$, so $S(X)=Y$.

*Step 2: counting.* By Lemma 3.4 it suffices to treat a generic $x$, all of whose chain points lie in faces.
- Let $z_0=x,z_1,\dots,z_L$ be a strict chain with $z_1,\dots,z_{L-1}\notin W$.
- The unbounded face misses $D_1\cup D_2$, so no strict step starts from it.
- By Step 1, $z_1,\dots,z_{L-1}$ lie in bounded faces $X_1,\dots,X_{L-1}$ outside $W$, and the composite isometry from $z_1$ to $z_i$ maps $X_1$ onto $X_i$.
- An isometry $x\mapsto\zeta^jx+t$ mapping a bounded face onto a given face is determined by $j$, because a non-trivial translation cannot map a bounded set onto itself. So at most 5 such isometries occur for each target face.
- Fix the first step. If $z_1\in\operatorname{int}W$, the chain stops, giving one endpoint. Otherwise $z_1$ lies in a face $X_1$. Then:
  - at most $5F$ endpoints lie in faces outside $W$, including $z_1$ itself;
  - at most $4\cdot5F$ further endpoints land in $\operatorname{int}W$ after one more step;
  - so each first step gives at most $25F$ endpoints.
- With at most 4 first steps and the start, $|R^*_W(x)|\le1+4\cdot25F=1+100F$.

The last statement of the theorem is Lemma 3.3. $\square$

The face count $F$ was **not computed** for any row of Table 5.1, so $\beta_W$ is finite but not explicit. So is the word-length bound of Proposition 4.4.

**Corollary 4.3 (reduction to the windows).** Suppose a restricted closure exists for $W$, and put $\beta_W:=1+100F$. Then for every $r<r_c$ and every $z\in\mathbb C$,
$$|O_r(z)|\le\beta_W\max\Bigl(1,\sup_{x}|O_r(x)\cap W|\Bigr).$$
Hence $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if $\sup_x|O_r(x)\cap W|<\infty$. And (B) holds if and only if this supremum is finite at every level $r_k$.

*Proof.*
- If $O_r(z)\cap W=\emptyset$, then $O_r(z)=R^{(r)}_W(z)$, which has at most $\beta_W$ points.
- Otherwise fix $y\in O_r(z)$. Orbits are equivalence classes, because the generating set is closed under inverses. So for any $w_0\in O_r(z)\cap W$ there is a chain from $w_0$ to $y$; the chain from $z$ itself need not visit $W$.
- Cut that chain at its last point in $W$. This gives a chain from a point of $O_r(z)\cap W$ to $y$ with intermediate points outside $W$ (`sharp-lower-bound-n8.md` Lemma 5.1).
- Hence $|O_r(z)|\le|O_r(z)\cap W|\cdot\beta^{(r)}(W)$, and Theorem 4.2 bounds $\beta^{(r)}(W)$.
- Bounded orbits give a finite group (`theorem1-converse.md` Proposition 4.2). Conversely, a finite group has orbits of size at most its order.
- Group monotonicity gives the last claim. $\square$

This is `sharp-lower-bound-n8.md` Theorem 5.5 with one window ($n=0$). The bulk factor is now bounded by the closure, and all remaining dependence on $r$ is in the core factor.

**Proposition 4.4 (finitely many first-return words).** Assume a restricted closure exists for $W$, and let $r<r_c$. Then every point of $R^{(r)}_W(x)$ is reached by an $r$-chain of length at most $\beta_W+1$ whose intermediate points lie outside $W$. Consequently:
- the pseudogroup induced on $W$ by $GG_5(r)$ is generated by one fixed finite set $\mathcal W$ of *itinerary words*, restricted to their $r$-domains. An itinerary word is a letter string of length $\le\beta_W+1$ together with the record of which letters act non-trivially. Without this record, one string can have different germs on different domains;
- the set $G_W$ of their germs $x\mapsto\zeta^jx+t$ is finite and independent of $r$;
- the translation parts satisfy $|\sigma(t)|\le T_W$ for one constant $T_W$.

*Proof.* Take a shortest such chain. Its intermediate points are distinct, since a repeated point could be cut out. Each of them is in $R^{(r)}_W(x)$, so there are at most $\beta_W$ of them. Itinerary words of bounded length form a finite set, and so do their germs. $\square$

**Proposition 4.5 (the induced partition is structurally stable near $r_c$).** Let $T$ be the finite set of test centres of the itinerary words in $\mathcal W$, that is, the points $h^{-1}(\pm1)$ for prefix maps $h$. Let $\mathcal A_r$ be the arrangement in $W$ of:
- the circles $\partial D(c,r)$, $c\in T$;
- $\partial W$;
- the curves $h^{-1}(\partial W)$ for all prefix maps $h$. These decide whether an intermediate point avoids $W$ and whether the endpoint lies in $W$. They are arcs of radius $r_c$ with fixed centres.

Then there is $\eta>0$ such that the combinatorial type of $\mathcal A_r$ is the same for all $r\in(r_c-\eta,r_c)$. On that interval the induced pseudogroup on $W$ is a fixed symbolic system: the same face labels, with the same admissible itinerary words and germs on each face. Only the positions of the faces change with $r$.

*Proof.* The type can change only at:
- a tangency of two curves;
- a triple point;
- a test circle through a vertex of the fixed curves. These vertices do not move, because the fixed curves have radius $r_c$.

With finitely many fixed centres and fixed curves, each such event is a non-trivial algebraic equation in $r$ with finitely many roots:
- a tangency of two test circles occurs at $r=|c-c'|/2$;
- a triple point occurs at $r=$ the circumradius of three non-collinear centres;
- a test circle passes through a fixed point $v$ at $r=|c-v|$;
- a tangency with a fixed circle of radius $r_c$ occurs at $r=|c-c_i|\pm r_c$;
- two test circles meet on a fixed curve: a non-trivial quadratic condition.

Between consecutive event values, the arrangement moves by an isotopy of the plane: the curves move continuously, and no tangency or triple point is created or destroyed. So its combinatorial type is constant there.

Whether an itinerary word applies at $x$ depends only on the face of $\mathcal A_r$ containing $x$. $\square$

So, near $r_c$, all the $r$-dependence of $GG_5(r)$ is the metric deformation of one finite symbolic system inside the windows. As $r\uparrow r_c$, some faces collapse:
- at every critical point $P$: the bounded faces cut out near $P$ by the circles $\partial D(c,r)$ with $|c-P|=r_c$. These circles pass at distance $\approx r_c-r$ from $P$;
- possibly at triple points of $\mathcal A_{r_c}$ inside $W$.

The constant $\eta$ is not computed.

## 5. The computation at $r_c$ (conditional on the computation)

### 5.1 Programs, algorithm and certificate format

The programs are **outside the repository**, which holds Lean code only. They are in the session scratch directory `sharp2/`, which a reboot or routine cleanup can clear. A port of the window clipping to the repository's Lean engine, with an independent checker, is in progress; it will be documented in `../relative-certificates.md`. The algorithm and format are described here fully enough to re-implement them.

**Algorithm (`rcut.c`).** It computes the smallest restricted closure, as a worklist closure.
1. Start with the full circles $\partial D_1$ and $\partial D_2$, and with the four boundary arcs of every window $W_P$.
2. Take a stored arc $\gamma$ on $\operatorname{circle}(c)$. For each $p=\pm1$ with $0<|c-p|<2r_c$:
   - clip $\gamma$ to $D_p$;
   - remove, for every window, the open arc $\gamma\cap\operatorname{int}W_P$. This is the interior of the intersection of the four arcs $\gamma\cap D(P+v,r_c)$. That intersection is connected, because each of the four arcs is shorter than $\pi$;
   - rotate each remaining non-degenerate piece by $\zeta^{\mp1}$ about $p$, and add the parts not yet covered to the store and to the worklist.
3. Tangency rule: if $|c-p|=2r_c$, the circle meets $D_p$ in one point of $\partial D_p\subseteq K'$, and no image is added. This matches Definition 4.1.
4. Stop when the worklist is empty.

Further details:
- Arcs of $\partial D_p$ are mapped into $\partial D_p$ by rotations about $p$, so they need no work.
- Only circles canonical under $\{\pm z,\pm\bar z\}$ are stored, as in `kcut4.c`. This is valid because $W$ is symmetric.
- Both prefilters (which windows a circle can meet) are conservative. Under-removal only adds arcs, which is safe.
- A re-check pass then verifies condition 2 for every stored arc. It uses the same angle code, so it is not independent.

**Endpoint symbols and exact decisions.** An endpoint on $\operatorname{circle}(c)$ is a symbol $(e,s)$, $e\in\Lambda$, $s=\pm1$. It names the point $c+\tfrac d2(1+ish)$, where $d=e-c$ and $h=\sqrt{4\rho/|d|^2-1}$. Then:
- disk and tangency tests are integer sign tests in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$, using `__int128`;
- at $r_c$, distinct symbols $(e_1,s_1)\ne(e_2,s_2)$ can name the same point, a triple point, because $3+\varphi$ is a norm. When two computed angles differ by less than $10^{-10}$, an exact test decides. With $u=e_1-c$, $v=e_2-c$ and $m=\operatorname{Im}(\bar uv)/\sin72^\circ\in\mathbb Z[\varphi]$:
  - the common point exists iff $|u|^2|v|^2|u-v|^2=(3+\varphi)(2+\varphi)m^2$;
  - the sides must satisfy $\operatorname{sign}(|v|^2-\operatorname{Re}\bar uv)\operatorname{sign}(m)=s_1$ and $\operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{Re}\bar uv-|u|^2)\operatorname{sign}(m)=s_2$;
  - if $e_1=e_2$, the two sides coincide iff the circles are tangent.
- Equal points are identified. A near pair that is not exactly equal aborts the run; this never happened.
- The coincidence test agreed with 42 604 numerical cases (`eqtest.py`). The only disagreements were 180 tangencies, where floating point misses an exact equality by about $10^{-8}$. The referee re-derived the test.
- At an exact tangency, $4\rho-|d|^2=0$ exactly, so the angle offset is exactly 0.

**Orderings.** These use double-precision angles $\arg(e-c)+s\operatorname{atan2}(\sqrt{4\rho-|d|^2},|d|)$, evaluated without cancellation. They rely on the hard-coded, underived per-symbol bound $5\cdot10^{-15}$ of `kcut4.c`.

**Validation.** With no windows ($w=0$) at $\delta=1/10$, the output is byte-identical to that of `kcut4.c`: 12 715 canonical circles and 49 838 arcs.

**Certificate format.** The file holds the canonical arcs, one line per maximal counterclockwise arc: `A c0 c1 c2 c3 e0 e1 e2 e3 s0 f0 f1 f2 f3 s1`. These are the centre and the start and end symbols, in the basis $1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3$; `F c0..c3` marks a full circle. The radius is $r_c$. The window is given by $w$. A checker must:
- expand the file by the symmetry group;
- verify (K1): $\partial D_1$ and $\partial D_2$ are full circles;
- verify (K2): every window boundary arc lies in $K'$;
- verify (K3): condition 2 of Definition 4.1, for every arc, $p=\pm1$ and both rotations;
- verify (K4): the windows are disjoint.

**`chk.py`.** It is a second implementation, independent of `rcut.c`'s decisions. It shares only the symbol semantics, the window definition and the lattice arithmetic of `zz.py`. It recomputes everything else: the symmetric expansion with no canonical reduction, its own merging, and (K1)–(K4).
- Its angles are 40-digit `mpmath` **floating point**, with no error analysis. Ties below $10^{-30}$ go to its own exact coincidence test.
- At an exact tangency it computes $\arccos(|d|/2r_c)$ from a floating-point ratio. A $10^{-40}$ error there becomes an error of order $10^{-20}$ in the angle, far above the tie threshold. So two names of one point are then ordered by noise.
- The referee checked that this is conservative for equal points: it can only produce false INVALID verdicts. But nothing bounds the separation of distinct points, so `chk.py` is **not a certificate**.
- Negative tests: deleting 53 arcs from the $w=(0,2,-1,0)$ certificate gives INVALID, with 464 uncovered images; checking it against a different window aborts.

**The referee's checker** (`refcomb/refchk.py`, in the referee's scratch directory) has a different design. There is no merging: coverage of each image is decided by a sweep over all expanded arcs. Every ordering uses `mpmath.iv` interval enclosures, refined from 30 to 70 to 150 digits. Overlaps are resolved by the referee's own exact coincidence test.

1. *This is the excursion bound* missing from `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` §8, item 3, in the strongest form: one bound for all $r<r_c$, for all points (not only lattice points), from one computation at $r_c$.
2. *Uniformity in $r$ comes for free.* The computation happens at $r_c$, and Lemma 3.3 transfers it to every smaller radius. No non-norm hypothesis is needed. Tangencies and triple points do occur at $r_c$: 2–4 canonical circles are tangent to $\partial D_p$, and there are up to 337 coincidences.

## 6. A sufficient strategy for (B): the core

### 6.1 The two statements

**(R-bulk)** *(critical system only).* There are windows $W^{(j)}$ with circumradius $\rho_{W^{(j)}}\to0$ such that $\beta^*(W^{(j)})<\infty$ for every $j$. Termination of a restricted closure is one way to prove each instance. By monotonicity of $\beta^*$, nested windows suffice, and no uniformity in $j$ is needed. Instances computed: the nine rows of Table 5.1, one of them certified.

**(R-core)** *(levels).* There are $C>0$ and $k_0$ such that for every $k\ge k_0$,
$$\sup_{x\in\mathbb C}\bigl|O_{r_k}(x)\cap B_k\bigr|<\infty,\qquad B_k=\bigcup_{P\ \rm critical}\overline B(P,C\varepsilon^k).$$
The *uniform* form asks for one bound $N$ for all $k\ge k_0$.

**Proposition 6.3 (proved).** (R-bulk) and (R-core) imply (B), and hence $r_{\rm crit}(5)=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$.

*Proof.* For $k\ge k_0$ choose $j$ with $\rho_{W^{(j)}}\le C\varepsilon^k$, so that $W^{(j)}\subseteq B_k$. By Corollary 4.3 (its proof uses only $\beta^{(r)}(W)\le\beta^*(W)<\infty$),
$$|O_{r_k}(z)|\le\beta^*(W^{(j)})\cdot\max\Bigl(1,\sup_x|O_{r_k}(x)\cap B_k|\Bigr)$$
for every $z$. So $GG_5(r_k)$ is finite for every $k\ge k_0$, and monotonicity gives (B). $\square$

**How this relates to (B).**
- The proof uses only finiteness at each level. Uniformity of the bound in $k$ is not used.
- By Corollary 4.3 with the certified window, (B) is *equivalent* to: $\sup_x|O_{r_k}(x)\cap W|<\infty$ for every $k$. So (R-bulk) together with (R-core) is a sufficient strategy, at least as strong as (B), and not a restatement of the gap.
- It is not known whether (R-bulk) follows from (B). (R-bulk) asks for bounds uniform over all $r<r_c$, and no argument that (B) forces this is known. Neither implication is claimed.
- The combined plan expected (S1) to be "a single finiteness statement". **It is not.** Theorem 5.2 settles it for each fixed window, uniformly in $r$. But the core factor must be taken at the level scale, so the windows must shrink with $k$. This requires either (R-bulk), or the nested annulus factors $\beta^{(r_c)}(W^{(j+1)};W^{(j)})$ for all $j$: an infinite family of statements about the single system at $r_c$ in either case. Monotonicity of $\beta^*$ runs the wrong way to reduce the family to finitely many instances. That needs a self-similarity argument (§7).

### 6.2 Why the core should be bounded (proved mechanism, limit statement)

**Lemma 6.1 (linear germs preserve the distance to the critical set).** Let $g$ be a germ $x\mapsto\zeta^jx+t$ with $g(P)=P'$ for critical points $P,P'$. Then $|g(x)-P'|=|x-P|$. So the orbit of a point under any family of such germs has at most $5\cdot38=190$ points.

*Proof.* $g(x)-P'=\zeta^j(x-P)$. $\square$

At level $k$, in coordinates $x=P+\varepsilon^kX$, the disk tests that are tight at $P$ become the half-planes $10\,\ell_P(P+X)+1\lessgtr0$. This holds for lattice points within the range of the Diophantine lune lemma (`sharp-lower-bound-n5.md`, Lemma 6.1, Proposition 6.2). All other tests near $P$ are decided by a margin.

So in the zoomed limit the core dynamics is generated by:
- linear germs, which preserve $|X|$;
- returns through the bulk, bounded by Theorem 5.2.

What is not controlled are the **non-linear returns**: germs with $g(P)\neq P'$ but $|g(P)-P'|=O(\varepsilon^k)$. Their lattice points $g(P)$ come from the infinite skeleton of the critical system, for example the points $t_jE$ of the critical chord that approach $E$. These returns are what makes core orbits merge and split between levels (§6.3).

The referee of `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` found that, at levels 0–2, the only lattice point in the tested window satisfying the lune hypothesis is $P$ itself. The lemma starts to apply from about level 4. So any induction built on it needs a base deeper than every certified level.

### 6.3 Exact data at levels 1–3

**Program.** `latbfs.c` takes the normalized core points $y=P+\varepsilon^k\eta$ with $\eta\in\Lambda$, $|\eta|\le0.3$ and $|\sigma\eta|\le8$: 21 per critical point, 798 in all. It then:
- computes their full orbits at $r_k$ exactly, by breadth-first search on $\Lambda$ with integer disk tests;
- records every visit to $B_k$ ($C=0.3$) in normalized coordinates $(P',\eta')$, where $\eta'=\varepsilon^{-k}(y-P')\in\Lambda$ is an exact lattice point.

So the same normalized point can be compared across levels.

**Table 6.1.** Levels 1 and 2 were reproduced exactly by the referee with independent code; level 3 was not.

| $k$ | orbits | points in all orbits | largest orbit | max visits to $B_k$ per orbit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 387 | 568 459 | 71 817 | 1 578 |
| 2 | 414 | 7 306 570 | 1 150 311 | 1 352 |

Orbit sizes grow by a factor of $13$–$16$ per level, while the number of core visits per orbit stays near $1\,500$. This is the behaviour the uniform form of (R-core) asserts. It is evidence only: these are the orbits of the chosen starting points, not a supremum over all $x$.

**Exact recursion is false at low levels (refutation, exact).** The plan's step (2), taken literally, asserts that the partition of normalized core points into orbits is the same at levels $k$ and $k+1$. Restricted to the normalized core points visited at both levels ($|\eta|\le0.3$, $|\sigma\eta|\le30$):
- $362$ of $387$ orbit blocks agree between levels 1 and 2 ($93.5\%$);
- $404$ of $414$ agree between levels 2 and 3 ($97.6\%$).

In the larger run ($|\eta|\le0.5$, $|\sigma\eta|\le30$, all 29 298 normalized core points), $11\,240$ of $11\,944$ blocks agree between levels 1 and 2. The differences include both splits and merges. These agreement figures were not reproduced by the referee.

An explicit counterexample:
- $A=(P,\eta)$ with $P=(0,1,2,2)$, $\eta=(-3,-3,0,-5)$, $|\eta|=0.146$, $|\sigma\eta|=6.85$;
- $B=(P',\eta')$ with $P'=(-2,-1,-2,0)$, $\eta'=(-8,-6,-1,-11)$, $|\eta'|=0.225$, $|\sigma\eta'|=14.73$.

At level 2 ($10r^2=17+18\varphi$), $y_A=(-55,-54,2,-87)$ and $y_B=(-146,-111,-23,-199)$ lie in one orbit of $102\,425$ points. At level 3 ($10r^2=85-24\varphi$), $y_A=(233,234,2,379)$ lies in an orbit of $258\,339$ points, and $y_B=(608,465,87,843)$ in a different orbit of $1\,315\,339$ points. Three implementations confirm this, all deciding exactly:
- `latbfs.c`;
- an independent Python breadth-first search (`verify_cex.py`);
- the referee's own code and the repository engine, run read-only with `tdcsg-orbit orbit -n 5 --r2 re:7/2,9/5` and `--r2 re:61/10,-12/5`.

**Where exactness does hold.** The hypothesis of the lune lemma requires the lune quantity $10|y-P|^2(20|\sigma y-\sigma P||\sigma P-p|+\varphi^{3k})$ to be $<1$.
- For the counterexample it fails: the values are $2.52$ (A) and $12.87$ (B) at level 2, and $0.595$ (A) and $3.04$ (B) at level 3. The referee recomputed these.
- Restricted to the 126 core points that satisfy it at level 2, the orbit partitions at levels 2 and 3 are **identical** (38 blocks). The referee did not reproduce this.
- This is consistent with exact recursion from some level $k_0(C)$ on. Level-4 orbits have about $10^9$ points, so the data cannot go further.

## 7. Referee's suggestions for closing (R-bulk) and (R-core)

The following are the referee's proposals (`referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-combined.md` §5), reproduced as proposals, not results. None of them has been carried out.

1. **Weakest form of (R-bulk).** Ask only for $\beta^*(W^{(j)})<\infty$ along one nested sequence $W^{(j)}\downarrow\{P\}$. Nested windows suffice by monotonicity, and Proposition 6.3 needs no uniformity in $j$. This is the form stated in §6.1.
2. **Windows bounded by arcs of any radius.** Theorem 4.2 never uses that $\partial W$ has radius $r_c$. Only the programs and the Lean port would need the generalization. It permits rescaled windows $P+u_P\varepsilon^j(W_0)$.
3. **A finite reduction of (R-bulk) through annuli.**
   - Factor $\beta^*(W^{(j+1)})\le\beta^*(W^{(j)})\cdot a_j$, where $a_j$ bounds the visits to the annulus $W^{(j)}\setminus\operatorname{int}W^{(j+1)}$.
   - If the face-labelled first-return systems on consecutive annuli have the same combinatorics for $j\ge j_0$, then $a_j=a_{j_0}$, and one more closure gives (R-bulk).
   - Exact conjugacy under $z\mapsto P+u\varepsilon(z-P)$ is unlikely, because circles of radius $r_c$ through $P$ do not rescale. In coordinates scaled by $\varepsilon^{-j}$ they converge to their tangent lines. So the statement to prove is structural stability of the tangent-cone system: lines through $P$, plus the skeleton returns $g(P)\to P$. That needs a finite non-degeneracy check of the limit, and an explicit $j_0$ from the curvature error $O(\varepsilon^j)$.
   - Test first whether the skeleton points approaching each of the 38 points are $\Psi_P$-invariant, as they are for the golden rotation on the critical chord.
4. **(R-core) through a limit polygon exchange.**
   - Fix the certified window $(0,2,-1,0)$. In coordinates $X=\varepsilon^{-k}(x-P)$ the collapsing faces converge to the half-plane system $10\,\ell_P(P+X)+1\lessgtr0$ with the finite germ set $G_W$. That is a piecewise isometry with straight cuts.
   - (i) Certify, by the closure method with lines, that this limit system has uniformly bounded orbits on $B(0,C)$.
   - (ii) Prove with the lune lemma that, from an explicit $k_0$ on, the level-$k$ induced system on $B_k$ coincides with the limit system face by face.
   - (iii) Cover the levels below $k_0$ by Lean-engine certificates, or by the hybrid certificate of §10.

## Appendix A. Reproduction

All in the session scratch directory `sharp2/`.
