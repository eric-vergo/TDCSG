# Finiteness of $GG_5(r)$ below $\sqrt{3+\varphi}$: a certified bulk bound at the critical radius, and the reduction to the critical windows

Authors: Eric Vergo, Claude

**Status.** Let $r_c=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$. Infiniteness of $GG_5(r)$ for $r\ge r_c$ is proved and Lean-checked (`critical-radius-n5.md`, Theorem A). **The converse, (B): $GG_5(r)$ finite for every $r<r_c$, is still open, so $r_{\rm crit}(5)=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$ is not proved by this note. Nor is the corresponding equality for $n=8,10,12$.** What this note adds:

1. **Proved modulo finite computations that were run: a $k$-independent bound on excursions.** The computations are exact in every equality and sign; their orderings are floating-point guided. For the largest windows they are re-checked by an independent 40-digit implementation. Let $W$ be a union of small closed windows, bounded by arcs of radius $r_c$, about the 38 lattice points of $\partial D_1\cup\partial D_2$ at $r_c$. For each of nine window sizes, with circumradius from $0.292$ down to $0.0166$ (Table 5.1), there is a constant $\beta_W$ such that, **for every $r<r_c$ at once**, every chain whose intermediate points avoid $W$ reaches at most $\beta_W$ points (Theorem 5.2). The computation is a single exact closure at the critical radius $r_c$ itself, where $3+\varphi$ is a norm, with tangencies and triple points handled exactly. It terminated in every case tried.
2. **Proved: (B) reduces to the windows.** For every $r<r_c$ and every $z$, $|O_r(z)|\le\beta_W\max(1,\sup_x|O_r(x)\cap W|)$. Hence $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if the orbits meet $W$ in uniformly boundedly many points (Corollary 4.3). The dynamics induced on $W$ is generated by one fixed finite set of first-return words, the same for all $r<r_c$. Near $r_c$ its domains have constant combinatorics; they only move (Propositions 4.4, 4.5).
3. **The remaining gap is two statements** (§6), and Proposition 6.3 (proved) shows they imply (B):
   - **(R-bulk)**, a statement about the critical system alone. The window closure at $r_c$ terminates for windows of every size, along a sequence of sizes tending to $0$. Verified for nine sizes.
   - **(R-core)**, a uniform bound on how often an orbit at level $k$ visits the level-scale windows $\bigcup_P B(P,C\varepsilon^k)$.

   Evidence for (R-core), exact data: in the orbits computed, the maximal number of core visits per orbit is $1578,1352,1546$ at levels $k=1,2,3$, while the largest orbit grows from $7.2\cdot10^4$ to $1.5\cdot10^7$ points.
4. **Refuted in its literal form (exact counterexample):** the claim that the orbit partition of the normalized core points is level-independent, i.e. that level $k+1$ is exactly the $\varphi^{-3}$-rescaled copy of level $k$. It fails between levels 2 and 3 (§6.3). It does hold, at the levels tested, on the core points that satisfy the hypothesis of the Diophantine lune lemma. The measured agreement grows from $93.5\%$ to $97.6\%$ of orbit blocks.

Definitions of the new terms: a *window* (§3), a *strict chain* (Definition 3.1), a *restricted orbit* (Definition 3.2), a *restricted closure* (Definition 4.1). "Exact" means that equalities and signs in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ are decided with integers. Orderings of distinct points along a circle are **not** exact.
- `rcut.c` uses double precision with the per-symbol bound $5\cdot10^{-15}$ inherited from `kcut4.c`. That bound is plausible but not derived, and it trusts libm `atan2` (`referee-report-sharp-lower-bounds.md`, issue 1).
- The checker `chk.py` uses 40-digit `mpmath` angles.
- Both add an exact equality test whenever two points are closer than their tie threshold ($10^{-10}$ rad and $10^{-30}$ rad).

So a result resting on `rcut.c` alone is **computed, not certified**. A result also passed by `chk.py` rests on two independent implementations, the second with a margin of about $10^{-30}$ against 40-digit arithmetic. That is still not a float-free certificate.

## 1. Setting

As in `critical-radius-n5.md` §1 and `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` §1:
- $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$, $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$, $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and $\sigma:\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2$ is the internal embedding;
- $D_1,D_2$ are the closed disks of radius $r$ about $\mp1$;
- $A,B$ are the rotations by $\zeta^{-1}$ about $-1,+1$; $a=A$ on $D_1$ and $a=\mathrm{id}$ elsewhere, likewise $b$; $GG_5(r)=\langle a,b\rangle$;
- $\varepsilon=\varphi^{-3}$; the levels are $r_k^2=3+\varphi-\varepsilon^k/10$.

A *chain* at radius $r$ is a sequence $z_0,\dots,z_L$ with $z_i=s_i(z_{i-1})$, $s_i\in\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}\}$. $O_r(z)$ is the orbit.

Refereed facts used:
- orbit and group monotonicity in $r$ (`boundary-contact.md`, Theorems 3.1, 3.4);
- a finitely generated group of bijections with uniformly bounded orbits is finite (`theorem1-converse.md`, Proposition 4.2).

So (B) holds as soon as $GG_5(r_k)$ is finite for every $k\ge k_0$.

## 2. The critical lattice points (proved)

**Lemma 2.1.**
1. The solutions of $|w|^2=3+\varphi$ with $w\in\Lambda$ are exactly $20$ elements: $\pm\zeta^j\pi$ and $\pm\zeta^j\bar\pi$, with $\pi$ a prime of $\Lambda$ above $3+\varphi$.
2. The lattice points on $\partial D_1(r_c)\cup\partial D_2(r_c)$ are the $38$ points $p+w$ ($p=\pm1$). The two lens corners $\pm(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})$ lie on both circles.
3. Under the symmetry group $\{z,-z,\bar z,-\bar z\}$ of the system they form $10$ classes (Table 2.1). The tight points $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ of the critical words form **one** class, and the lens corners another.
4. A lattice-centred circle of radius $r_c$ is tangent to $\partial D_p(r_c)$ only if its centre is $p+2w$. The point of tangency is then the critical point $p+w$.
5. A lattice point $y$ lies on a lattice-centred circle of radius $r_c$ about $c$ iff $y-c$ is one of the $20$ elements of item 1.

*Proof.*
1. $N_{\mathbb Q(\varphi)/\mathbb Q}(3+\varphi)=11$ is prime and $11\equiv1\pmod5$, so $(3+\varphi)$ splits in $\Lambda$ as $\pi\bar\pi$ with $\pi\neq\bar\pi$ up to units. $\Lambda$ is a principal ideal domain. If $w\bar w=3+\varphi$, then $(w)$ is a prime above $3+\varphi$, so $w=u\pi$ or $w=u\bar\pi$ with $u\bar u=1$. Every conjugate of $u$ then has modulus 1, so $u$ is a root of unity, $\pm\zeta^j$. A search over coefficients in $[-4,4]$ finds exactly these 20 (`zz.py`).
2. Translate item 1. Exactly two points lie on both circles, the lens corners.
3. Exact enumeration (`zz.py`).
4. $|c-p|^2=4(3+\varphi)$. Since $(2)$ is prime in $\Lambda$ and $3+\varphi$ is a unit at $2$, $c-p=2w$ with $w\in\Lambda$ and $|w|^2=3+\varphi$.
5. Immediate from item 1. $\square$

## 3. Windows, strict chains and restricted orbits

**Windows.** Fix $w\in\Lambda$ with $|w|<r_c$, and put
$$W_0=\bigcap_{v\in\{w,-w,\bar w,-\bar w\}}D(v,r_c),\qquad W_P=P+W_0,\qquad W=\bigcup_{P\ \rm critical}W_P .$$
- $W_0$ is a compact convex neighbourhood of $0$, close to a square whose sides are arcs of radius $r_c$.
- Its inradius is $r_c-|w|$, and its circumradius $\rho_W$ is attained at the corners.
- $W$ is invariant under $z\mapsto-z$ and $z\mapsto\bar z$. So, with $W$ removed, the system keeps its symmetry, and the reduced storage of `kcut4.c` remains valid.
- For every window used, $2\rho_W<\varphi^{-1}$, so the 38 windows are pairwise disjoint (Table 5.1, checked by `wingeo.py` and by the checker).

**Definition 3.1 (strict chains at $r_c$).** A *strict chain* is a sequence $z_0,\dots,z_L$ with $z_i=S_i(z_{i-1})$ for $S_i\in\{A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}\}$ and $z_{i-1}$ in the **open** disk of $S_i$ of radius $r_c$.

**Definition 3.2 (restricted orbits).** For $W\subseteq\mathbb C$:
- $R^{(r)}_W(x)$ is the set of endpoints of chains at radius $r$ from $x$ whose intermediate points $z_1,\dots,z_{L-1}$ avoid $W$;
- $R^*_W(x)$ is the same for strict chains;
- $\beta^{(r)}(W)=\sup_x|R^{(r)}_W(x)|$ and $\beta^*(W)=\sup_x|R^*_W(x)|$.

**Lemma 3.3 (every chain below $r_c$ is a strict chain).** For $r<r_c$ and every $W$, $R^{(r)}_W(x)\subseteq R^*_W(x)$. Hence $\beta^{(r)}(W)\le\beta^*(W)$ for every $r<r_c$.

*Proof.* Delete the trivial steps of an $r$-chain. Each remaining step applies a rotation at a point of the closed disk of radius $r$, which lies in the open disk of radius $r_c$. The remaining intermediate points are a subset of the original intermediate points, together possibly with the start or the end, which carry no condition. $\square$

This is the version of `sharp-lower-bound-n8.md` Lemma 5.4 that works at a norm radius. At $r_c$ itself the closed-disk dynamics has degenerate itineraries through the tight points; Theorem A rests on them. Strict chains avoid them. Note that $\bigcup_{r<r_c}O_r(x)$ is exactly the strict orbit of $x$.

**Lemma 3.4 (generic points suffice).** If $W$ is closed, then $\beta^*(W)=\sup\{|R^*_W(x)|:x\text{ generic}\}$. Here *generic* means that no image of $x$ under any map $z\mapsto\zeta^jz+t$, $t\in\Lambda$, lies on a given countable family of circles. The generic points are dense.

*Proof.* Being a strict chain with intermediate points outside $W$ is an open condition on the start point: the disks are open and the complement of $W$ is open. Take finitely many endpoints of strict chains from $x$. They are distinct images $g_i(x)$ under distinct isometries $g_i$, and for $x'$ close to $x$ the same chains exist and the $g_i(x')$ are still distinct. Generic points are dense by Baire's theorem. $\square$

## 4. Restricted closures (proved)

**Definition 4.1.** Let $W$ be a finite union of pairwise disjoint compact convex sets $W_P$, each bounded by finitely many circular arcs. A *restricted closure* is a finite union $K'$ of closed arcs (and circles) of radius $r_c$ such that
1. $\partial D_1\cup\partial D_2\cup\partial W\subseteq K'$, with the disks taken at radius $r_c$;
2. for every arc $\alpha$ of $K'$ and every $S\in\{A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}\}$, with $D_S$ the closed disk of radius $r_c$ on which $S$ acts, every non-degenerate component of $\alpha\cap D_S\setminus\operatorname{int}W$ is mapped by $S$ into $K'$. Isolated points need not be checked; the proof below shows why.

**Theorem 4.2 (relative certificate).** If a restricted closure $K'$ exists, then $\beta^*(W)\le1+100F$. Here $F$ is the number of bounded faces of $\mathbb C\setminus K'$ that are not contained in $W$. Consequently $\beta^{(r)}(W)\le1+100F$ for every $r<r_c$.

No tangency or endpoint conditions like (C3) and (C4) of the certificate theorem are needed: strict chains only ever see open faces (Lemma 3.4).

*Proof.* Since $\partial W\subseteq K'$, every face (component of $\mathbb C\setminus K'$) lies in $\operatorname{int}W$ or in $\mathbb C\setminus W$. Since $\partial D_S\subseteq K'$, every face lies in $\operatorname{int}D_S$ or misses $D_S$.

*Step 1: faces outside $W$ map to faces outside $W$, or into $\operatorname{int}W$.* Let $X$ be a face in $\mathbb C\setminus W$ with $X\subseteq\operatorname{int}D_S$.
- Suppose some $y\in S(X)$ lies in $K'\setminus\operatorname{int}W$. Then $y\in\operatorname{int}D_S$.
- If $y$ lies in a non-degenerate component of $\alpha\cap D_S\setminus\operatorname{int}W$ for an arc $\alpha$ of $K'$, then condition 2, applied with $S^{-1}$ (which acts on the same disk), gives $S^{-1}(y)\in K'$. But $S^{-1}(y)\in X$, which misses $K'$. Contradiction.
- Otherwise every arc of $K'$ through $y$ enters $\operatorname{int}W$ on every side of $y$ that it has. Then $y\in\overline{\operatorname{int}W}\setminus\operatorname{int}W=\partial W$. The boundary arcs of the window $W_P$ containing $y$ lie in $K'$ and avoid $\operatorname{int}W$, because the windows are disjoint and convex. Near $y$ they lie in $\operatorname{int}D_S$, so one of them gives a non-degenerate component through $y$. This is the previous case.
- Hence $S(X)$, which is connected and open, misses $K'\setminus\operatorname{int}W\supseteq\partial W$. So $S(X)\subseteq\operatorname{int}W$, or $S(X)$ lies in a single face $Y\subseteq\mathbb C\setminus W$.
- In the second case the same argument for $S^{-1}$ on $Y$ gives $S^{-1}(Y)\subseteq X$, so $S(X)=Y$.

*Step 2: counting.* By Lemma 3.4 it suffices to treat a generic $x$, all of whose chain points lie in faces.
- Let $z_0=x,z_1,\dots,z_L$ be a strict chain with $z_1,\dots,z_{L-1}\notin W$.
- The unbounded face misses $D_1\cup D_2$, so no strict step starts from it.
- By Step 1, $z_1,\dots,z_{L-1}$ lie in bounded faces $X_1,\dots,X_{L-1}$ outside $W$. The composite isometry $h_i$ from $z_1$ to $z_i$ maps $X_1$ onto $X_i$.
- An isometry $x\mapsto\zeta^jx+t$ mapping a bounded face onto a given face is determined by $j$, so at most 5 occur for each target face.
- Fix the first step. If $z_1\in\operatorname{int}W$, the chain stops and gives one endpoint. Otherwise $z_1$ lies in a face $X_1$. Then at most $5F$ endpoints lie in faces outside $W$; this count includes $z_1$, whose germ from $z_1$ is the identity. At most $4\cdot5F$ further endpoints land in $\operatorname{int}W$ after one more step. So each first step gives at most $25F$ endpoints.
- With at most 4 first steps and the start, $|R^*_W(x)|\le1+100F$.

The last statement is Lemma 3.3. $\square$

**Corollary 4.3 (reduction to the windows).** If a restricted closure exists for $W$, then for every $r<r_c$ and every $z\in\mathbb C$,
$$|O_r(z)|\le\beta_W\max\Bigl(1,\sup_{x}|O_r(x)\cap W|\Bigr),\qquad\beta_W:=1+100F.$$
Hence $GG_5(r)$ is finite if and only if $\sup_x|O_r(x)\cap W|<\infty$. And (B) holds if and only if this supremum is finite at every level $r_k$.

*Proof.*
- If $O_r(z)\cap W=\emptyset$, then $O_r(z)=R^{(r)}_W(z)$.
- Otherwise every $y\in O_r(z)$ is reached from its last visit to $W$ along a chain avoiding $W$ (`sharp-lower-bound-n8.md` Lemma 5.1). So $|O_r(z)|\le|O_r(z)\cap W|\cdot\beta^{(r)}(W)$.
- Theorem 4.2 bounds $\beta^{(r)}(W)$; bounded orbits give a finite group (`theorem1-converse.md` Proposition 4.2); and monotonicity gives the last claim. $\square$

This is `sharp-lower-bound-n8.md` Theorem 5.5 with one window ($n=0$). The bulk factor is now bounded by an exact computation. All remaining dependence on $r$ is in the core factor.

**Proposition 4.4 (finitely many first-return words).** Assume a restricted closure exists for $W$. For every $r<r_c$, every point of $R^{(r)}_W(x)$ is reached by an $r$-chain of length at most $\beta_W+1$ with intermediate points outside $W$. Consequently:
- the pseudogroup induced on $W$ by $GG_5(r)$ is generated by the restrictions of one fixed finite set $\mathcal W$ of words, those of length $\le\beta_W+1$, to their $r$-domains;
- the set $G_W$ of their germs $x\mapsto\zeta^jx+t$ is finite and independent of $r$;
- the translation parts satisfy $|\sigma(t)|\le T_W$ for one constant $T_W$.

*Proof.* Take a shortest such chain. Its intermediate points are distinct, since a repeated point could be cut out, and they are all in $R^{(r)}_W(x)$, so there are at most $\beta_W$ of them. Germs of words of bounded length form a finite set. $\square$

**Proposition 4.5 (the induced partition is structurally stable near $r_c$).** Let $T$ be the finite set of test centres of the words in $\mathcal W$: the points $h^{-1}(\pm1)$, $h$ a prefix map. Let $\mathcal A_r$ be the arrangement in $W$ of the circles $\partial D(c,r)$, $c\in T$, together with $\partial W$ and the curves $h^{-1}(\partial W)$ for all prefix maps $h$. The latter decide whether an intermediate point avoids $W$ and whether the endpoint lies in $W$. They are arcs of radius $r_c$ with fixed centres. There is $\eta>0$ such that the combinatorial type of $\mathcal A_r$ is the same for all $r\in(r_c-\eta,r_c)$. On that interval the induced pseudogroup on $W$ is a fixed symbolic system: the same face labels, with the same admissible germs on each face. Only the positions of the faces change with $r$.

*Proof.* The type changes only at a tangency of two curves, a triple point, or a test circle through a vertex of the fixed curves. The fixed curves have radius $r_c$, so their vertices do not move. With finitely many fixed centres and fixed curves, each such event is a non-trivial algebraic equation in $r$:
- a tangency of two test circles at $r=|c-c'|/2$;
- a triple point at $r=$ the circumradius of three non-collinear centres;
- a test circle through a fixed point $v$ at $r=|c-v|$;
- tangency with a fixed circle of radius $r_c$ at $r=|c-c_i|\pm r_c$;
- the intersection of two test circles meeting a window circle, a non-trivial quadratic condition.

There are finitely many such events. Whether a word applies at $x$ depends only on the face of $\mathcal A_r$ containing $x$. $\square$

So, near $r_c$, all the $r$-dependence of $GG_5(r)$ is the metric deformation of one finite symbolic system inside the windows. As $r\uparrow r_c$ some faces collapse:
- at every critical point $P$: the bounded faces cut out near $P$ by the circles $\partial D(c,r)$ with $|c-P|=r_c$. These pass at distance $\approx r_c-r$ from $P$;
- possibly at triple points of $\mathcal A_{r_c}$ inside $W$.

The constant $\eta$ is not computed.

## 5. The computation at $r_c$ (proved modulo the exact computation)

### 5.1 Program

`rcut.c` (session scratch directory `sharp2/`) computes the smallest restricted closure: it is Definition 4.1 run as a worklist closure. It is `kcut4.c` of `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` §5.1 with three changes:
1. **Exact coincidences.** At $r_c$, distinct endpoint symbols $(e_1,s_1)\ne(e_2,s_2)$ on $\operatorname{circle}(c)$ may name the same point: a triple point, since $3+\varphi$ is a norm. Whenever two computed angles differ by less than $10^{-10}$, an exact test decides:
   - with $u=e_1-c$, $v=e_2-c$ and $m=\operatorname{Im}(\bar uv)/\sin72^\circ\in\mathbb Z[\varphi]$, the point exists iff $|u|^2|v|^2|u-v|^2=(3+\varphi)(2+\varphi)m^2$;
   - the two side conditions are $\operatorname{sign}(|v|^2-\operatorname{Re}\bar uv)\operatorname{sign}(m)=s_1$ and $\operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{Re}\bar uv-|u|^2)\operatorname{sign}(m)=s_2$;
   - if $e_1=e_2$, the two sides coincide iff the circles are tangent.

   All of this is integer arithmetic in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ (128-bit). Equal points are identified. A near pair that is not exactly equal aborts the run; this never happened. The test was validated against 42 604 numerical cases (`eqtest.py`, 10 525 equalities). The only disagreements were 180 tangencies, where floating point misses an exact equality by about $10^{-8}$.
2. **Tangency rule.** A circle tangent to $\partial D_p$ meets $D_p$ in one point of $\partial D_p\subseteq K'$, so it contributes no image.
3. **Window clipping.** Each clipped piece $\gamma\cap D_p$ has the open arc $\gamma\cap\operatorname{int}W_P$ removed for every window. That arc is the interior of the intersection of the four arcs $\gamma\cap D(P+v,r_c)$, which is connected because each has length $<\pi$. The rest is rotated and added. The boundary arcs of all windows are added at the start.

Orderings use double-precision angles with the hard-coded per-symbol bound $5\cdot10^{-15}$ of `kcut4.c`. That bound is not derived. The program's own re-check uses the same angle code, so it is not independent.

**Validation.** With no windows ($w=0$) and $\delta=1/10$, the output is byte-identical to that of `kcut4.c`: 12 715 canonical circles and 49 838 arcs.

**Independent checker.** `chk.py` is new code that shares nothing with `rcut.c` except the basic lattice arithmetic of `zz.py`. It reads the arc file and:
- expands it by the full symmetry group, with no canonical reduction, and merges the arcs per circle;
- verifies (K1) both boundary circles are full; (K2) every window boundary arc lies in $K'$; (K4) the windows are disjoint;
- verifies (K3), condition 2 of Definition 4.1 for every arc, $p=\pm1$ and both rotations.

Angles are computed in 40-digit `mpmath` directly from the geometry. Ties below $10^{-30}$ are decided by its own exact coincidence test.

Negative tests:
- deleting 53 arcs from the $w=(0,2,-1,0)$ certificate gives INVALID, with 464 uncovered images;
- checking it against a different window gives INVALID.

**Remarks.**
1. *This is the missing excursion bound* of `sharp-lower-bound-n5.md` §8, item 3, in the strongest form: one bound for all $r<r_c$, for all points (not only lattice points), from one computation at $r_c$.
2. *Uniformity in $r$ comes for free.* The computation happens at $r_c$, and Lemma 3.3 transfers it to every smaller radius. No non-norm hypothesis is needed. Tangencies and triple points at $r_c$ do occur: 2–4 canonical circles tangent to $\partial D_p$, and up to 337 coincidences.
3. *Growth.* Sizes grow with shrinking windows, but not regularly in $\rho_W$:
   - three windows with $\rho_W\in[0.025,0.037]$ give almost the same size, about $6.2$–$6.6$ million arcs, with only about $40\%$ of their centres in common;
   - the smallest window, $\rho_W=0.0166$, jumps to 36.2 million arcs. The sizes behave like a step function of the window scale, much as $K(r)$ behaves like a step function of $\delta$;
   - $\rho_W\max|\sigma c|$ stays between 15 and 52, so the internal extent of the closure scales like $1/\rho_W$, just as the internal extent of $K(r)$ scales like $1/\delta$.

   The size depends on the shape and orientation of the window as well as on its size.
4. **What the bound does not give.** $F$, and hence $\beta_W$, was not computed. It is finite, which is all that the reductions use.

## 6. The remaining gap: the core

### 6.1 The two statements

**(R-bulk)** *(critical system only).* There are windows $W^{(j)}$ of the form of §3 with $\rho_{W^{(j)}}\to0$ for which the restricted closure at $r_c$ terminates.

**(R-core)** *(levels).* There are $C>0$, $N$ and $k_0$ such that for every $k\ge k_0$,
$$\sup_{x\in\mathbb C}\bigl|O_{r_k}(x)\cap B_k\bigr|\le N,\qquad B_k=\bigcup_{P\ \rm critical}\overline B(P,C\varepsilon^k).$$

**Proposition 6.3 (proved).** (R-bulk) and (R-core) imply (B), and hence $r_{\rm crit}(5)=\sqrt{3+\varphi}$.

*Proof.* For $k\ge k_0$ choose $j$ with $\rho_{W^{(j)}}\le C\varepsilon^k$, so that $W^{(j)}\subseteq B_k$. By Corollary 4.3,
$$|O_{r_k}(z)|\le\beta_{W^{(j)}}\cdot\max\bigl(1,|O_{r_k}(z)\cap B_k|\bigr)\le\beta_{W^{(j)}}N$$
for every $z$. So $GG_5(r_k)$ is finite for every $k\ge k_0$, and monotonicity gives (B). $\square$

**Remarks.**
- (R-core) with $N$ allowed to depend on $k$ is equivalent to (B), given (R-bulk). Its content is the **uniformity**: the core at the level scale does not grow.
- (R-bulk) is not implied by (B): it asks for a bound uniform over $r<r_c$.
- The combined plan expected (S1) to be "a single finiteness statement". **It is not.** Theorem 5.2 settles it for each fixed window, uniformly in $r$. But the core factor must be taken at the level scale, so the windows must shrink with $k$. This needs either (R-bulk) for all scales, or the nested annulus factors $\beta^{(r_c)}(W^{(j+1)};W^{(j)})$ for all $j$. Either way it is an infinite family of statements about the single system at $r_c$. A proof would be an exact self-similarity of the critical system near its 38 critical points.

### 6.2 Why the core should be bounded (proved mechanism, limit statement)

**Lemma 6.1 (linear germs preserve the distance to the critical set).** Let $g$ be a germ $x\mapsto\zeta^jx+t$ with $g(P)=P'$ for critical points $P,P'$. Then $|g(x)-P'|=|x-P|$. So the orbit of a point under any family of such germs has at most $5\cdot38=190$ points.

*Proof.* $g(x)-P'=\zeta^j(x-P)$. $\square$

At level $k$, in coordinates $x=P+\varepsilon^kX$, the disk tests that are tight at $P$ become the half-planes $10\,\ell_P(P+X)+1\lessgtr0$ for lattice points within the range of the Diophantine lune lemma (`sharp-lower-bound-n5.md`, Lemma 6.1, Proposition 6.2). All other tests near $P$ are decided by a margin.

So in the zoomed limit the core dynamics is generated by:
- linear germs, which preserve $|X|$;
- returns through the bulk, whose number is bounded by Theorem 5.2.

What is not controlled are the **non-linear returns**: germs with $g(P)\neq P'$ but $|g(P)-P'|=O(\varepsilon^k)$. Their lattice points $g(P)$ come from the infinite skeleton of the critical system, for example the points $t_jE$ of the critical chord that approach $E$. They are what makes core orbits merge and split between levels (§6.3).

### 6.3 Exact data at levels 1–3

**Program.** `latbfs.c` takes the normalized core points $y=P+\varepsilon^k\eta$, with $\eta\in\Lambda$, $|\eta|\le0.3$ and $|\sigma\eta|\le8$: 21 per critical point, 798 in all. It computes their full orbits at $r_k$ exactly, by breadth-first search on $\Lambda$ with integer disk tests. It records every visit to $B_k$ ($C=0.3$) in normalized coordinates $(P',\eta')$, where $\eta'=\varepsilon^{-k}(y-P')\in\Lambda$ is an exact lattice point. The same normalized point is thus compared across levels.

**Table 6.1.**

| $k$ | orbits | points in all orbits | largest orbit | max visits to $B_k$ per orbit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 387 | 568 459 | 71 817 | 1 578 |
| 2 | 414 | 7 306 570 | 1 150 311 | 1 352 |
| 3 | 414 | 98 426 482 | 15 374 299 | 1 546 |

Orbit sizes grow by a factor of $13$–$16$ per level, while the number of core visits per orbit stays near $1\,500$. This is the behaviour (R-core) asserts. It is evidence only: the orbits computed are those of the chosen starting points, not a supremum over all $x$.

**Exact recursion is false at low levels (refutation, exact).** Literally, the plan's step (2) asserts that the partition of normalized core points into orbits is the same at levels $k$ and $k+1$.
- Restricted to the normalized core points visited at both levels ($|\eta|\le0.3$, $|\sigma\eta|\le30$), $362$ of $387$ orbit blocks agree between levels 1 and 2 ($93.5\%$), and $404$ of $414$ between levels 2 and 3 ($97.6\%$).
- In the larger run ($|\eta|\le0.5$, $|\sigma\eta|\le30$, all 29 298 normalized core points), $11\,240$ of $11\,944$ blocks agree between levels 1 and 2.
- The differences include both splits and merges.

An explicit counterexample, re-verified by an independent Python breadth-first search (`verify_cex.py`):
- $A=(P,\eta)$ with $P=(0,1,2,2)$ and $\eta=(-3,-3,0,-5)$, where $|\eta|=0.146$ and $|\sigma\eta|=6.85$;
- $B=(P',\eta')$ with $P'=(-2,-1,-2,0)$ and $\eta'=(-8,-6,-1,-11)$, where $|\eta'|=0.225$ and $|\sigma\eta'|=14.73$.

At level 2 they lie in one orbit of $102\,425$ points. At level 3, $A$ lies in an orbit of $258\,339$ points that does not contain $B$.

**Where exactness does hold.** The hypothesis of the lune lemma requires the lune quantity $10|y-P|^2(20|\sigma y-\sigma P||\sigma P-p|+\varphi^{3k})$ to be $<1$. At level 2 neither $A$ nor $B$ satisfies it: the values are $2.52$ and $12.9$. At level 3, $B$ still does not ($3.04$). Restricted to the 126 core points that do satisfy it at level 2, the orbit partitions at levels 2 and 3 are **identical** (38 blocks). This is consistent with exact recursion from some level $k_0(C)$ on. With level-4 orbits of about $10^9$ points, the data cannot go further.

## 7. Where each step of the combined plan stands

1. **Port of the nested-window reduction, and the critical points.**
   - Done (proved).

   - Refuted in its literal form at levels $2\to3$ (§6.3).

3. **(S1) at $r_c$ by one exact computation.**

4. **Lattice points versus all points.** Theorem 5.2 bounds restricted orbits of all points directly, so the bulk needs no lattice argument. In the core, the lune lemma still controls only lattice points with bounded normalized internal coordinate. Passing to all points in the core is part of (R-core).
5. **Assembly.**
   - Proposition 6.3 is the assembly.
   - Base levels: the certified base is $GG_5(r)$ finite for $r^2\le3+\varphi-1/55$: the Lean-engine certificate with float-free checker, `../certificates.md`. So any induction built on it needs a base deeper than every certified level. Proposition 6.3 avoids the lune lemma, but (R-core) is expected to be provable, if at all, only from such a $k_0$.
   - Inductive step: not found. A proof of (R-core) by induction would need the non-linear returns of §6.2 to recur exactly from some level on. The data show they nearly do, but not at levels 1–3.

## 9. Next steps

## Appendix A. Reproduction

All in the session scratch directory `sharp2/`.
- `zz.py`: exact $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$ arithmetic; `python3 zz.py` lists the critical points (Lemma 2.1).
- `rcut.c`: `cc -O2 -o rcut rcut.c -lm`, then `rcut da db den w0 w1 w2 w3 maxpieces out.txt`.
  - The radius is $r^2=3+\varphi-(d_a+d_b\varphi)/\mathrm{den}$, so `0 0 1` is $r_c$.
  - Windows are about all 38 critical points, with the four disk centres $P\pm w,P\pm\bar w$; $w=0$ means no windows.
  - Output: one line `A c e0 s0 e1 s1` per canonical maximal arc (counterclockwise), or `F c` for a full circle.
  - Example: `rcut 0 0 1 5 -1 4 0 2000000000 rc_w1.txt`.
- `chk.py arcfile w0 w1 w2 w3`: the independent checker, which prints VALID or INVALID.
- `wingeo.py`: the window geometry of Table 5.1. `eqtest.py`: validation of the coincidence test.
- `latbfs.c`: `latbfs k C R maxtotal` produces the core-visit data of §6.3. `cmpcore2.py`, `diffblk.py` and `lunestat.py` compare levels. `verify_cex.py` re-checks the counterexample independently.
