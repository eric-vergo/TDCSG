# The critical radius of two-disk compound symmetry groups for n = 5, 8, 10, 12: an open problem

Authors: Eric Vergo, Claude

**Problem and status.** Prove that the two-disk group $GG_n(r)$ is finite for every radius $r$ below the closed forms $\sqrt{3+\varphi}$, $\sqrt{10-5\sqrt2}$, $\sqrt{4-\varphi}$, $\sqrt{40-22\sqrt3}$ ($n=5,8,10,12$). Infinitude at and above these radii is machine-checked in Lean. Finiteness is proved, modulo certified exact computations, up to radii between $0.002$ and $0.007$ below them. The remaining windows are open.

Status labels used throughout: **machine-checked** (Lean 4 / Mathlib, no `sorry`, standard axioms only); **refereed proof** (a written proof with an independent referee report); **proved modulo a certified computation**; **computed, not certified**; **conjecture**. Claims attributed to a referee's own computation or analysis, not yet independently checked, are marked as such.

---

## 1. Setting

Let $n\ge2$, $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}$, and let $D_L=D_L(r)$, $D_R=D_R(r)$ be the **closed** disks of radius $r$ centred at $-1$ and $+1$. Let $A(z)=-1+\zeta^{-1}(z+1)$ and $B(z)=1+\zeta^{-1}(z-1)$ be the clockwise rotations by $2\pi/n$ about the two centres. Define $a=A$ on $D_L$ and $a=\mathrm{id}$ off $D_L$, and likewise $b=B$ on $D_R$ and $b=\mathrm{id}$ off $D_R$. Since $A(D_L)=D_L$, $a$ and $b$ are bijections of $\mathbb C$. Put $GG_n(r)=\langle a,b\rangle\le\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb C)$. It is a group of piecewise isometries, and its pieces are cut out by circles of radius $r$.

Orbits are non-decreasing in $r$ *as sets*. Finiteness of the group passes to smaller radii: if $GG_n(r)$ is finite, so is $GG_n(r')$ for every $r'\le r$ (refereed proof; machine-checked as `orbit_mono` and `finite_GG_of_le`). Hence
$$r_{\rm crit}(n)=\inf\{r:\ GG_n(r)\text{ infinite}\}=\sup\{r:\ GG_n(r)\text{ finite}\}$$
is well defined (`TDCSG.criticalRadius`, an `sInf`). For $r<1$ the group is finite. For $n\notin\{2,3,4,6\}$ it is infinite for large $r$ (paper, Theorem 1). For $\operatorname{lcm}\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ it is finite for all radii (refereed proof; machine-checked as `finite_GG`).

## 2. Arithmetic structure

Along any word, the composite of the rotations actually applied to a point is a map $z\mapsto\zeta^jz+t$ with $t\in(1-\zeta)\mathbb Z[\zeta]$. Write $\mathcal T(r)$ for the set of these *word maps*. For $\varphi(n)=4$, $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ is a CM quartic field over the real quadratic field $K^+=\mathbb Q(\zeta)^+$. With $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^k$, $k\ne\pm1$, $x\mapsto(x,\sigma x)$ embeds $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ as a lattice in $\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$, the product of physical and internal space. The translation parts satisfy $|t|\le2+2r$. So $\mathcal T(r)$ is finite iff $\sup|\sigma(t)|<\infty$, and finiteness of $\mathcal T(r)$ implies finiteness of the group (refereed proof). Thus an orbit is finite iff its Galois-conjugate image is bounded: orbits are cut-and-project sets of $\Lambda$, but with a window that depends on $r$ and is not fixed.

Computed evidence: for $n=5$ the internal hull of orbits at $r^2=r_c^2-\delta$ is a round disk of radius $\approx6.49/\delta$, filled only in a fractal part. The full-plane group $\Gamma=\langle A,B\rangle\subset\mu_n\ltimes\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ is non-discrete exactly when $n\notin\{2,3,4,6\}$, and it then contains arbitrarily small translations (refereed proof). $GG_n(r)$ is its cut-down to two disks: $GG_n(r)z\subseteq\Gamma z\cap(D_L\cup D_R)$. The units $\varphi^{3}$ ($n=5,10$), $1+\sqrt2$, $2+\sqrt3$ contract one coordinate and expand the other, and govern the observed self-similarity.

## 3. The problem

Write $r_c(n)$ for the closed form:

| $n$ | $r_c^2$ | $r_c$ | minimal polynomial of $r_c^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | $3+\varphi$ | 2.148961 | $x^2-7x+11$ |
| 8 | $10-5\sqrt2$ | 1.711412 | $x^2-20x+50$ |
| 10 | $4-\varphi$ | 1.543362 | $x^2-7x+11$ |
| 12 | $40-22\sqrt3$ | 1.376547 | $x^2-80x+148$ |

**Problem.** Prove $r_{\rm crit}(n)=r_c(n)$, i.e. that $GG_n(r)$ is finite for every $r<r_c(n)$. The Lean propositions are `CriticalRadiusEqFive`, `…Eight`, `…Ten` and `…Twelve` (open).

### 3.1 Proved: infinitude for $r\ge r_c$ (machine-checked)

`infinite_GG_five`, `infinite_GG_eight`, `infinite_GG_ten` and `infinite_GG_twelve` give $1\le r_{\rm crit}(n)\le r_c(n)$ for all four $n$. Each also has a refereed written proof; validity at $r_c$ persists for $r>r_c$ because the disks only grow.

- **$n=5$ (also $n=10$).** Let $E=\zeta-\zeta^2$, with $|E+1|^2=3+\varphi$. The chord $[-E,E]$ through $0$ is split at $\pm\varphi^{-1}E$ and $\pm\varphi^{-3}E$. Three explicit words act on three closed sub-segments as translations. Together they induce the rotation $t\mapsto t+2\varphi^{-1}\pmod 2$ of the chord, so the orbit of $0$ is infinite. $r_c$ is also where the lens corners become the lattice points $\pm(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})$. For $n=10$ three other words act on the chord $\{s\zeta^4:|s|\le\sqrt5\}$, with rotation number $1/(\varphi+2)$.
- **$n=8$ (also $n=12$).** The point $p^\ast=-1+(2+i)(1-\zeta)$ lies on $\partial D_L$ exactly at $r_c$, since $|p^\ast+1|^2=5|1-\zeta|^2$. A finite family $\mathcal F$ of partial isometries of a chord through $p^\ast$ is realized by explicit words: 31 words of length $\le12$. $\mathcal F$ is closed under conjugation by the rescaling $t\mapsto\varepsilon t$ about $p^\ast$, $\varepsilon=\sqrt2-1$. Hence the orbit of $p^\ast$ contains $p^\ast+4i\varepsilon^{k+1}$ for all $k\ge0$. For $n=12$, $p^\ast=(2-\sqrt3)(1-2i)$, the family has 30 words of length $\le28$, and $\varepsilon=2-\sqrt3$. The closure data are checked by kernel reduction (`decide +kernel`).

### 3.2 Proved: finiteness up to explicit radii (proved modulo a certified computation)

**Certificate theorem (refereed proof; Lean has it only as the hypothesis `CutSetCertificate n`).** Fix $r$. Let $K$ be a finite union of closed arcs of circles of radius $r$ such that:
- (C1) $\partial D_L\cup\partial D_R\subseteq K$;
- (C2) $A^{\pm1}(K\cap D_L)\subseteq K$ and $B^{\pm1}(K\cap D_R)\subseteq K$;
- (C3) no carrying circle other than $\partial D_p$ is tangent to $\partial D_p$;
- (C4) every endpoint of a maximal arc that lies on $\partial D_p$ is the endpoint of an arc of $K$ entering $\operatorname{int}D_p$ there.

Then $GG_n(r)$ is finite, and by monotonicity so is $GG_n(r')$ for every $r'\le r$.

The natural candidate is the cut set $K(r)$, the closure of the two boundary circles under the generators.

**How the certificates are produced and checked.** A generator (`tdcsg-orbit certify`, an executable written in Lean) computes $K(r)$. Centres lie in $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$; endpoints are symbols for intersections of two lattice-centred circles; every disk test, tangency, coincidence and cyclic order is decided exactly by signs in quadratic towers over $K^+$ (a float filter with rigorous error bounds is used only when it decides). A separate float-free checker (`check-certificate`), sharing only the exact-arithmetic layer, verifies (C1)–(C4) from the file alone. At the refereed radii independent re-implementations agree (identical arc sets for $n=5$, identical counts for $n=10$, matching invariants for $n=8,12$); the four deepest certificates were regenerated byte for byte and re-checked independently; all are deterministic and recorded with SHA-256 checksums. The checker is a tested program, not a formally verified one. Its correspondence with the Lean predicate `TDCSG.ExistsCertificate` is by inspection of definitions.

Rows 5, 10, 12 are ordinary certificates (`notes/certificates.md`). The $n=8$ row (`notes/relative-certificates.md` §7) is a window-free closure stored one circle per orbit of $\pm z,\pm\bar z$, with (C1)–(C4) checked by the separate relative checker; it unfolds to an ordinary certificate by symmetry (argument in that note, refereed correct in `notes/proofs/referee-report-relative-certificates.md`). For $n=10,12$ an exact *interval of validity* (§8 there; argument refereed correct) shows the same symbolic arcs remain a certificate for every $r^2<\rho_1$, and the arcs moved just below $\rho_1$ were accepted by the ordinary checker. A closure at $\delta=1/250$ for $n=12$ exists, but its check is unfinished (computed, not certified). No single computation can close the windows: the cut set grows like a power of $\delta^{-1}$ (for $n=5$, about $\times20$ in circles per factor $\varphi^3$ in $\delta$). Its internal extent satisfies $\delta\cdot\max|\sigma(c)|\approx19$.

## 4. The reduction obtained for $n=5$, and what transfers

Throughout, $\varepsilon=\varphi^{-3}=2\varphi-3$. It is a unit of norm $-1$ with $\sigma(\varepsilon)=-\varphi^3$. The levels are $r_k^2=3+\varphi-\varepsilon^k/10$.

**4.1 Non-norm radii (refereed proof).** Call $r$ a *non-norm radius* if $r^2\in K^+$ but $r^2\ne|w|^2$ for every $w\in\mathbb Q(\zeta)$. This holds, for example, when $\sigma(r^2)<0$, or when $v_2(r^2)$ is odd. Every level $r_k$ is a non-norm radius. At such radii:
- no point of $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ lies on a lattice-centred circle of radius $r$;
- no two such circles are tangent;
- no three meet in a point.

Consequently (C3) and (C4) hold automatically, and $GG_5(r)$ is finite iff the closure $K(r)$ terminates. Equivalently, word maps realized at lattice points are bounded in internal space. Since finiteness is monotone, (B) is *equivalent* to termination along $r_k\uparrow r_c$. This is a reformulation, not a reduction to something easier. This transfers to $n=8,10,12$ at non-norm radii; for $n=12$ some levels of the natural sequence are norms (§4.7).

**4.2 Critical points (refereed proof).** $3+\varphi$ has norm 11, a split prime, so $|w|^2=3+\varphi$ has exactly 20 solutions in $\Lambda$. The lattice points on $\partial D_L\cup\partial D_R$ at $r_c$ are 38 points (the two lens corners lie on both circles). They fall into 10 classes under $\{\pm z,\pm\bar z\}$. The tight points $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ of the critical words form one class, and the lens corners another. A lattice-centred circle of radius $r_c$ is tangent to $\partial D_p$ only at a critical point. For $n=8$ there are 32 critical points in 8 classes (refereed). For $n=12$ see §4.7.

**4.3 Relative certificates (refereed proof).** Let $W$ be a union of disjoint small compact convex windows about the critical points, each bounded by four arcs of radius $r_c$. A *restricted closure* is a finite union of arcs of radius $r_c$ that:
- contains $\partial D_L$, $\partial D_R$ and $\partial W$;
- is closed under the four rotations applied to the parts of arcs inside the respective disk and outside $\operatorname{int}W$.

It is computed **at $r_c$**, where tangencies and triple points occur and are decided exactly. Theorem: if a restricted closure exists, with $F$ bounded faces outside $W$, then for **every $r<r_c$ simultaneously**, every chain whose intermediate points avoid $W$ has at most $1+100F$ endpoints. The key step is that every non-trivial move at radius $r<r_c$ is a move inside an open disk of radius $r_c$. Corollary:
$$|O_r(z)|\le\beta_W\max\bigl(1,\sup_x|O_r(x)\cap W|\bigr).$$
So $GG_5(r)$ is finite iff orbits visit $W$ boundedly. The induced pseudogroup on $W$ is generated by one finite set of itinerary words, the same for all $r<r_c$. Of nine window sizes computed at $r_c$ (circumradius $0.292$ down to $0.0166$), windows 1–8 are **certified** float-free by the repository's relative checker (`notes/relative-certificates.md` §5, counts equal to the note's table; the largest also by the referee's interval-arithmetic checker); window 9 is computed, not certified. For general $n$ the bound is $1+20nF$ (re-derived there, refereed correct); exact coincidences of three circles occur at lattice points in the bulk as well as in the windows, and the theorem needs no hypothesis on them. $F$, hence $\beta_W$, was not computed. With $W=\varnothing$ the same proof gives **Theorem H** (`notes/relative-certificates.md` §9; proved there, refereed correct after minor fixes): an arc set containing $\partial D_L\cup\partial D_R$ and closed under the rotations on non-degenerate components, with no (C3)/(C4), gives finiteness for every $r'<r$; a hybrid (windowed) certificate built on it saved at most about $2.5\times$ in memory, too little for $n=5$ level 2.

**4.4 From (B) to (R-bulk) and (R-core) (refereed proof).** Two statements together imply (B):
- **(R-bulk)**, about the critical system alone: restricted orbits at $r_c$ avoiding windows $W^{(j)}$ are bounded, along some sequence of window sizes $\to0$;
- **(R-core)**: for some $C$ and $k_0$, $\sup_x|O_{r_k}(x)\cap\bigcup_P\overline B(P,C\varepsilon^k)|<\infty$ for every $k\ge k_0$.

The pair is at least as strong as (B). Whether (B) implies (R-bulk) is not known. The $n=8$ analogue is Conjecture S (refereed implication S $\Rightarrow$ finiteness for $n=8$): (S1) restricted orbits at $r_c$ avoiding small windows about the 32 critical points are finite, and (S2) the innermost induced pseudogroup renormalizes exactly along one geometric sequence of radii. Its base needs levels 4 and 5: the certificate at $\delta=0.02188$ (§3.2) covers both for phases $\theta_*\ge0.80014$ (for the note's $\theta_*=4/5$, $r_5$ sits at $\delta\approx0.0218762$, just beyond it). Nothing about (S1) or (S2) follows.

**4.5 Further reduction to the four tight points (refereed note `sharp-lower-bound-limit-system.md`, with its labels).**
- *Proved*: the fixed-word limit is trivial (§5.3).
- *Proved modulo an exact finite computation* (skeleton computation at $r_c$, recomputed exactly by the referee). For the nine non-tight classes, with $C=3/10$ and $k\ge4$, every first return to the core is a *linear* germ $X\mapsto\zeta^jX$ in zoomed coordinates. (for all points). Hence (R-core) is equivalent, up to a factor 190, to (R-core-t), its restriction to the tight cores at $\pm E,\pm\bar E$: the problem lives at the four tight points.
- *Certified* (two rows) / *computed, not certified* (seven rows): restricted closures with windows **only** at the four tight points exist for nine window sizes. The two largest are VALID under a four-window copy of the interval-arithmetic checker (re-run by the referee, with negative tests); the referee confirmed the theorem needs no extra argument when tangencies lie outside the windows. Consequently, for every $r<r_c$, $GG_5(r)$ is finite iff its orbits meet four small windows at $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ in uniformly boundedly many points.
- *Proved*: (R-bulk-t) and (R-core-t), the tight-class versions, imply (B).

**4.6 Diophantine lune lemma (refereed proof).** Let $P$ be a tight point with $|P-p|^2=3+\varphi$, and let $\ell_P(y)=2\operatorname{Re}((y-P)\overline{(P-p)})$. Then exactly
$$10(|y-p|^2-r_k^2)=\bigl(10\ell_P(y)+\varepsilon^k\bigr)+10|y-P|^2 .$$
The bracket is $\equiv\varepsilon^k\pmod{10}$, so its norm is $\equiv\pm1\pmod{10}$ and it is bounded below by $1/|\sigma(\cdot)|$. Hence, for lattice points with
$$10|y-P|^2\bigl(20|\sigma y-\sigma P||\sigma P-p|+\varphi^{3k}\bigr)<1,$$
the disk test equals the tangent half-plane test: no lattice point lies in the thin lune. In zoomed coordinates $y=P+\varepsilon^k\eta$, the substitution $\Psi_P(y)=P+\varepsilon(y-P)$ carries membership at level $k$ to level $k+1$ exactly. The hypothesis is met, for $|\eta|,|\sigma\eta|\le1$, only from level $k_0=4$, and for $\le2$ from $k_0=6$. Any induction built on it needs a base deeper than every certified level: the certified base $\delta=1/55$ is level $k\approx1.18$ ($\varepsilon^k=2/11$). The limit-system note extends the lemma to all 38 critical points with one constant ($20|\sigma w|=30.87$).

**4.7 The same reduction for $n=12$** (refereed note `sharp-lower-bound-n12.md`, with its labels). *Proved:* there are 24 lattice vectors of norm $r_c^2$, hence 48 critical points in 12 classes. The level radii $r_m^2=r_c^2-\varepsilon^m/10$ ($\varepsilon=2-\sqrt3$) never put a lattice point on a lattice-centred circle, but at $m=1,2,3$ they **are** norms from $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})$ (explicit witnesses, e.g. $r_1^2=|u/(3+i)|^2$ with $u=13-18\zeta-3\zeta^2+13\zeta^3$), so the non-norm lemma does not apply there; it applies at $m\in\{0,4,5\}$ and all $m\ge6$. *Proved modulo an exact computation:* 40 of the critical points are rigid from level 0 on, for all points; the other 8 ($\pm p^\ast,\pm\bar p^\ast$ and four more) have skeleta exceeding $2\cdot10^6$ points and are treated as tight (not proved infinite; nothing depends on it). *Certified* by the repository's separate float-free checker (two rows re-run VALID by the referee): relative certificates at $r_c$ with windows about all 48 critical points, for circumradii $0.0915$, $0.0358$, $0.0197$, and further shapes $0.0718$, $0.0405$, $0.0124$ (`notes/relative-certificates.md` §6; one window for $n=8$ and two for $n=10$ are certified there too). Hence for every $r<r_c$, $GG_{12}(r)$ is finite iff orbits meet the 48 windows boundedly, and (B) for $n=12$ follows from tight-class statements analogous to (R-bulk-t), (R-core-t). The certified base $\delta=1/180$ covers levels $0$–$2$; level 3 ($\delta\le1/520$) is not certified, and the closure at $\delta=1/250$ (unchecked) would not reach it.

## 5. What fails, and what is observed (exact data)

**5.1 The cut set has no stable limit** (evidence, $n=5$ and $n=8$). Rescaled about a critical point, $K(r)$ refines everywhere. Since a similarity cannot map radius-$r$ arcs to radius-$r$ arcs, any 2-D renormalization is at best combinatorial and asymptotic; the renormalizable objects are orbits and first-return germs.

**5.2 Literal $\varphi^{-3}$ self-similarity of core orbits is false** (refuted; exact; refereed and confirmed by independent exact implementations, including the referee's code and the repository engine). Take $P=(0,1,2,2)$, $\eta=(-3,-3,0,-5)$ and $P'=(-2,-1,-2,0)$, $\eta'=(-8,-6,-1,-11)$, in the basis $1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3$, and put $y=P+\varepsilon^k\eta$.
- At level 2 ($10r^2=17+18\varphi$), $y_A=(-55,-54,2,-87)$ and $y_B=(-146,-111,-23,-199)$ lie in **one** orbit of 102 425 points.
- At level 3 ($10r^2=85-24\varphi$), $y_A=(233,234,2,379)$ lies in an orbit of 258 339 points and $y_B=(608,465,87,843)$ in a **different** orbit of 1 315 339 points.

Both points violate the lune hypothesis. At the tight points, $\varphi^{-3}$ matching of first-return sets is *refuted* (exact; reproduced by the referee) at every tested consecutive pair up to $4\to5$. On the fixed sample $S_E$ of 811 tight core points, agreement between consecutive levels is 0, 14 and 14 at $1\to2$, $2\to3$, $3\to4$; on the 84 tight points of a second sample it is 0, 0, 0. Neither the four symmetries nor the unit $-\varepsilon$ repairs this (the latter gives 34, 64, 80 of 811). Near $E$ the infinite orbit of $E$ at $r_c$ is not invariant under $y\mapsto E+\varphi^{-3}(y-E)$ (*proved*: about 22% of images are exact non-members, since their own orbits are finite and miss $E$). Example: $(E,\eta=(-3,-1,-1,-3))$ has excursions of 21 066, 16 128 171 and 3 473 926 points at levels 3, 4, 5. Its first-return sets have 6, 46 and 6 elements, with levels 3 and 5 equal and level 4 different. Levels 1–3 were re-checked by an independent program.

**5.3 A fixed finite word set gives a trivial limit** (refereed, *proved*). In zoomed coordinates the induced system of the fixed word set of a certified window converges to a pseudogroup of rotations on half-planes, whose orbits have at most $5\cdot38=190$ points. Every *non-linear* return to the level-$k$ core is a concatenation of at least $\lceil(\varphi^{3k}/2C-6)/T_W\rceil$ first returns to the window, by a norm argument, i.e. on the order of $\varepsilon^{-k}$ window visits. A useful limit object must therefore be a limit of first-return maps with words of unbounded length.

**5.4 The period of the renormalization** (exact computations unless stated).
- *$n=5$, period two with ratio $\varphi^{-6}$: conjecture* (refereed; data reproduced exactly by the referee). Evidence is on tight points only, on fixed samples. On $S_E$ (811 points), agreement two levels apart is 185 ($1\to3$) and 518 ($2\to4$), against at most 14 one level apart. On tight points whose level-$k$ excursion is lune-free within zoomed distance 3, the sets at $k$ and $k+2$ agree in all 273 cases tested ($k=2,3$; none is lune-free at $k=1$), while 0 of 32 such points agree one level apart at $3\to4$. Caveat, as the note states it: level 5 could be computed only for a selected family of 129 points (those whose excursions fit under the cap, all of about $3.47\cdot10^6$ points), which already agreed 116/117 at $1\to3$. Its 100% agreement at $3\to5$ is a selection effect, not a trend; 129 of the 273 lune-free cases come from this family. The orbit of $E$ at $r_c$ is mapped into itself by $y\mapsto E+\varphi^{-6}(y-E)$ on all 3 661 tested points within distance 1 of $E$ (exact memberships); the invariance fails between distance 1 and 1.2, and whether it holds exactly within distance 1 is open.
- *$n=12$, exact period one: refuted for every starting level $m_0\le3$; open beyond* (refereed note; reproduced count for count by a second, independent referee engine). On the first referee's sample (265 points at $p^\ast$, zoomed internal radius $\le100$), first-return sets agree exactly between consecutive levels from level 2 on ($265/265$ at $2\to3$ and $3\to4$; 184/184 uncapped at $4\to5$), with non-linear returns present (147 starts), and excursions growing about $9\times$ per level. On a larger sample (internal radius $\le300$; 2 413 points per tight class) exact period one **fails**: first-return agreement is 1 181, 2 195, 2 364, 2 391 of 2 413 at $0\to1,\dots,3\to4$, and the orbit partition of the core changes by 28 merges at $2\to3$ and 4 merges at $3\to4$ (complete run). Onset example: $\eta=-45+11\zeta+71\zeta^2-67\zeta^3$, internal radius $124.5$; its full orbit has 27 456 points at level 2 and 284 536 at level 3, and its core orbit set grows from 8 to 38 points. The smallest internal radius of a change grows with the level ($13.9$, $26.9$, $124.5$, $142.7$), so exactness holds out to an internal radius that grows with the level. Whether exact period one holds from some deeper level on is **open**; the data fit both answers. *Observed structure (evidence, not proved):* from level 1 on every change is a growth (first-return sets only gain points, 290/290; orbit blocks only merge, 32/32); every change is preceded by a lune point (a lattice point where the circle test and its tangent-line test disagree) in the earlier excursion; no lune-free excursion changes at the next level (4 248 cases); core visits per full orbit do not grow with the level (at most 280 at levels 0–4). Near $p^\ast$ the infinite orbit at $r_c$ appears invariant (evidence) under the period-one homothety within distance $0.14$ (4 149/4 149; exact non-memberships from $0.1415$ on).
- *$n=8$*: floating-point sampling (evidence) shows the same parity as $n=5$. Sampled germ sets at $p^\ast$ agree under $\tau\mapsto\varepsilon^2\tau$ for levels $2\to4$ (27 of 27) and $3\to5$ (20 of 23), but only 10 match under $\tau\mapsto\varepsilon\tau$. $n=8$ and $n=10$ have not been tested with the exact method.

**5.5 Bounded window visits** (computed, exact; levels 1–2 reproduced by the referee). For $n=5$, the full orbits of 798 normalized core points were computed. The maximal number of visits to the level-$k$ core per orbit is 1 578, 1 352 and 1 546 at levels 1, 2 and 3, while the largest orbit grows from $7.2\cdot10^4$ to $1.54\cdot10^7$ points (×13–16 per level). Evidence only: not a supremum over all points.

**5.6 Computation sizes grow in steps** (computed): certificate sizes are nearly constant over ranges of $\delta$ and jump roughly once per factor of the unit in $\delta$; orbit-of-0 thresholds accumulate at $r_c$ with gap ratios tending to the unit.

**5.7 The mechanism of the period** (referee's analysis, attributed; not yet independently checked). All disk tests are signs of real numbers in $\mathbb Z[\varphi]$, whose internal space is a *line*. On that line $\sigma(\varepsilon)=-\varphi^3$ reverses orientation. Two facts make the sign matter: Hermitian norms are totally positive, $\sigma(|u|^2)=|\sigma u|^2\ge0$; and the level defect alternates, $\sigma(r_c^2-r_k^2)=(-\varphi^3)^k/10$. For a test at a non-critical point, with $u=h(Q)-p$, the zoomed cut offset $\tilde\delta=\varepsilon^{-k}\cdot10(|u|^2-r_k^2)\in1+10\mathbb Z[\varphi]$ satisfies exactly
$$\sigma\tilde\delta-1=(-\varphi^{3})^{-k}\bigl(10|\sigma u|^2-10(4-\varphi)\bigr),\qquad \operatorname{sign}(\sigma\tilde\delta-1)=(-1)^k\operatorname{sign}\bigl(|\sigma u|-|\sigma w|\bigr),$$
where $w$ is a critical vector. So for large $k$ the cut offsets of non-critical near misses lie on opposite sides with the parity of the level, and no non-critical offset occurs exactly at two consecutive levels; the only common offset is the critical tangent half-plane. The condition is invariant under the totally positive unit $\varepsilon^2$. This is a necessary-condition argument about exact cut offsets; it proves neither the failure of period-one matching of return sets nor period two. **Proposed rule:** period one exactly when the level unit is totally positive; hence period two for $n=5$, $8$, $10$ (units of norm $-1$) and period one for $n=12$ ($2-\sqrt3$, totally positive), where the alternating factor is replaced by $\varepsilon^m>0$ (proved identity, no parity obstruction). In the data the rule distinguishes the cases: for $n=5$ consecutive levels disagree already at small internal radius, while for $n=12$ there is no parity alternation, agreement is high, and failures occur only through lune points at an internal radius that grows with the level. It does **not** hold as exact period one in the strong sense (equality of return relations on all core lattice points from a fixed level): for $n=12$ that is refuted for $m_0\le3$ (§5.4). $n=8$ agrees in floating-point evidence only; $n=10$ is untested.

## 6. Questions for specialists

1. **Existing renormalization theorems.** Is there a renormalization theorem for planar piecewise rotations or isometries with quadratic-irrational parameters (5-, 8-, 10- and 12-fold) that applies to the first-return map on a neighbourhood of a tight point at $r=r_c$, or just below it? Candidates: references 2–8, 11, 12. *An answer would give* a limit system with a proved renormalization, or the reason this setting falls outside these frameworks.
2. **Off-critical renormalization with curved cuts.** Here a one-parameter family of piecewise isometries has discontinuity curves that are circles of fixed radius $r$. As $r\uparrow r_c$ it converges to a system with an exact 1-D renormalization on a chord (golden rotation for $n=5,10$; a self-similar pseudogroup for $n=8,12$). Is there a framework for asymptotic self-similarity under rescaling by a unit, with curvature corrections of relative size $O(\varepsilon^k)$ controlled uniformly? *An answer would give* the matching step with an explicit starting level.
3. **Uniform bounds on returns.** Finiteness below $r_c$ is equivalent to bounded returns to four small windows at $\pm E,\pm\bar E$ (refereed reduction). Is there a known mechanism that yields return-count bounds uniform across scales? (A height function in internal space, an odometer structure on first-return words, bounded distortion?) *An answer would give* (R-core-t) uniformly; the data (§5.5) show visit counts near 1 500 while orbits grow ×14 per level.
4. **Period and total positivity.** Is it known, for renormalization over a real quadratic field (round-off rotations, quadratic PETs, model sets with one-sided windows), that the renormalization period is 1 when the scaling unit is totally positive and 2 otherwise, through the sign of the unit on the one-dimensional internal line, and in what sense: exact from some level, or only asymptotically with defects at growing internal radius as observed for $n=12$? *An answer would give* the right form of the inductive statement for each $n$.
5. **$n=12$ as the model case.** The open window is the narrowest ($0.002$), there is no parity obstruction, and the return relation is exactly level-independent up to an internal radius that grows with the level. An exact one-step induction (first-return map to the rescaled core conjugate to the previous level) is refuted from every starting level $\le3$; the attempt in the repository did not close the problem and leaves (R-core-t) and (R-bulk) for $n=12$ open. Is there a standard scheme that tolerates defects of this kind, for instance an induction on internal radius as well as on level, or a renormalization of the eventual relation $\sim_\infty$ ("same orbit at all large levels")? *An answer would give* the first complete case.
6. **Monotone growth and lunes.** For $n=12$, first-return sets and core orbit classes are observed only to grow with the level, and every change is preceded by a lune point. Is there a known mechanism making induced relations monotone in the renormalization level for such families? The natural argument, that the rescaled disks contain the previous ones, fails because the rescaled generators rotate about different centres, so orbit monotonicity in $r$ does not apply. Can the lune lattice points be controlled by a Diophantine argument uniformly in the level, beyond bounded internal radius where the lune lemma already applies? *An answer would give* the monotone limit, reducing (R-core-t) to a bound on the classes of one limit object.
7. **Type of the transition.** Orbits are model sets of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$ with a radius-dependent window that is not fixed. Infinitude switches on when lattice points ($\pm E$, $\pm\bar E$, the lens corners, or $p^\ast$) reach a boundary circle. Is this a known type of bifurcation? Compare digital filters with overflow (Chua–Lin), outer billiards on kites (Schwartz), renormalizable PET families (Hooper, Schwartz). *An answer would give* a model in which "finite below, infinite at" has already been proved.
8. **Exact self-similarity at $r_c$.** Is there a technique for proving local invariance of the infinite orbit at $r_c$ under the observed homothety ($\varphi^{-6}$ about $E$ for $n=5$, ratio $2-\sqrt3$ about $p^\ast$ for $n=12$) by a finite set of conjugating words, a 2-D analogue of golden induction? *An answer would give* a route to the single-system statement (R-bulk-t), which needs no limit in $r$.
9. **A Lagrange-type finiteness theorem.** Is there a planar or pseudogroup analogue of Boshernitzan–Carroll (finitely many descendants for interval exchanges over a quadratic field) for cuts that are circular arcs of radius $r$, $r^2\in K^+$? *An answer would give* finiteness of the family of renormalized first-return systems.
10. **Generic points.** Is there a lower bound, polynomial in the window size, on the inradius of faces of an arrangement of radius-$r$ circles centred in a bounded internal window of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$? *An answer would give* the passage from lattice points of bounded internal coordinate to all points.
