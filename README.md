# Two-Disk Compound Symmetry Groups

**The fivefold critical radius is exactly R = √(3 + φ), where φ = (1 + √5)/2 is the golden ratio.**
Place two closed disks of radius r at −1 and 1. Each move turns one disk clockwise through 72° and fixes everything outside it. The group of plane permutations generated by these moves is finite exactly when r < R. At r = R the orbit of the origin is already infinite.

```lean
theorem TDCSG.CriticalRadius.criticalRadius_five : criticalRadius 5 = √(3 + φ)
```

[Read the illustrated proof](https://eric-vergo.github.io/TDCSG/) · [Read the challenge statement](Comparator/FiveDisk/Challenge.lean) · [Inspect the Lean proof](TDCSG/CriticalRadius/Five.lean) · [Check the published revision](https://eric-vergo.github.io/TDCSG/verification.json)

Below the threshold, track which disk centers become active as a point moves. Each switch changes one integer coordinate. The disk geometry puts barriers on these coordinates, giving a uniform bound on the resulting orbits and hence a finite group. At the threshold a diagonal chord supports a sequence of distinct orbit points. The formal proof establishes this qualitative classification. The written proof also bounds the growth of the conjugate translations as the threshold approaches. [Written proof and quantitative bound](notes/proofs/sharp-lower-bound-n5-pentagrid.md)

R. A. Hearn, W. Kretschmer, T. Rokicki, B. Streeter and E. Vergo introduced these groups in *Two-Disk Compound Symmetry Groups* and proposed closed forms for orders 5, 8, 10 and 12. The latter three equalities remain open. The general construction permits different radii and rotation orders on the two disks. This repository includes the Lean proof library, an exact orbit engine, certificates and referee reports. [Original paper](2302.12950v1.pdf) · [Research results](#results)

## Reproduce the independent check

The challenge states one theorem using Mathlib alone: it defines the two generators, the group they generate and the radical $\sqrt{3+(1+\sqrt5)/2}$, and asserts

```lean
theorem FiveDisk.finite_iff (r : ℝ) : Finite (group r) ↔ r < radius
```

with an intentional proof hole: the group is finite exactly for radii below the threshold, so it is infinite at the threshold and above. It corresponds to `finite_GG_five_iff` in the library. The solution proves the same statement from the project library. Lake Comparator checks that the two statements match and replays the solution with Lean’s kernel, allowing only `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`. [Challenge](Comparator/FiveDisk/Challenge.lean) · [Solution](Comparator/FiveDisk/Solution.lean) · [Configuration](Comparator/FiveDisk/comparator.json)

From a checkout with [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) and Linux bubblewrap with user namespaces available, run

```sh
bash scripts/get-mathlib-cache.sh
lake build
lake comparator --config Comparator/FiveDisk/comparator.json
bash scripts/check-comparator-negative.sh
```

The final command makes two changes to the challenge, each in its own disposable copy: it weakens `r < radius` to `r ≤ radius`, and it changes the definition of `radius` to `√(4 + (1 + √5) / 2)`. It succeeds only when Comparator rejects both, the first as a mismatched statement and the second as a mismatched definition. Each published blueprint links its commit to the Linux run that checked this statement. [Release workflow](.github/workflows/blueprint.yml) · [Local blueprint build](blueprint/README.md)

## Building

The project uses Lean `v4.36.0` (see `lean-toolchain`) and Mathlib `v4.36.0` (see `lakefile.toml`). With
[elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed:

```sh
bash scripts/get-mathlib-cache.sh   # fetch the imported Mathlib modules and their dependencies
lake build
lake test            # self-test of the orbit engine, pins of the figures' words
.lake/build/bin/tdcsg-orbit orbit -n 5 --r2 closed-1/100   # an exact orbit computation
```

`lake build` builds the proof library and the engine; `lake test` runs the engine's self-test (known
finite cases, rows of the certificate tables, tangency and cap cases, tampered certificates) and
kernel-checks the pins of `TDCSGTests` (words for the figures of chapter 4). The commands that
generate and check certificates, and the caps the large runs need, are in
[`notes/certificates.md`](notes/certificates.md) and
[`notes/relative-certificates.md`](notes/relative-certificates.md).

## Results

Every claim below has one of these statuses: **proved in Lean**; **refereed written proof** (a
note in `notes/proofs/` with an independent referee report in the repository; "refereed" here
does not mean journal peer review); **proved modulo a certified computation**; **computed, not
certified**; **conjecture**; **open**.

In Lean there is no `sorry`, no `native_decide` and no extra axiom: every theorem of `TDCSG`
depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

### The fivefold theorem

**Proved in Lean**, in `TDCSG/CriticalRadius/Five.lean`, namespace `TDCSG.CriticalRadius`:

- `criticalRadius_five`: the critical radius `criticalRadius 5`, the infimum of the radii with an
  infinite group, is √(3+φ);
- `finite_GG_five_iff`: GG_5(r) is finite if and only if r < √(3+φ);
- `sSup_setOf_finite_GG_five`: the supremum of the radii with a finite group is √(3+φ);
- `infinite_GG_five`: for every r ≥ √(3+φ) the group GG_5(r) is infinite;
- `infinite_orbit_zero_five`: for every r ≥ √(3+φ) the orbit of 0 is infinite.

Below the threshold, integer labels for the active disk centers meet coordinate levels whose
crossing edges end at leaves; these levels confine every walk to a box and bound every orbit
uniformly. Lean obtains the bound from density and compactness, without an explicit value.

**Refereed written proof**: [`sharp-lower-bound-n5-pentagrid.md`](notes/proofs/sharp-lower-bound-n5-pentagrid.md).
Sections 1–3 and the confinement step are refereed in
[`referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-n5-pentagrid.md`](notes/proofs/referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-n5-pentagrid.md);
the conclusion of §4 and §5 (what Lean proves) are refereed, correct after minor fixes, in
[`referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-n5-pentagrid-conclusion.md`](notes/proofs/referee-report-sharp-lower-bound-n5-pentagrid-conclusion.md).
Only the written proof gives the explicit bound N ≤ 16√5/δ, δ = R² − r², and the growth
O(1/(3+φ−r²)) of the Galois conjugates of the translations as r approaches the threshold.

The private companion repository TDCSG-archive holds notes on other approaches to the fivefold
bound, which the proof above does not use.

### Other results proved in Lean

Theorem names are in the namespaces `TDCSG.Statements` (file `TDCSG/Statements.lean`) and
`TDCSG.CriticalRadius` (file `TDCSG/CriticalRadius/Statements.lean`), unless a file is named.

- **Definitions.** `TDCSG.ballPerm` extends an isometry of a closed ball to a permutation that is
  the identity outside the ball, and `TDCSG.compoundSymmetryGroup c r H` is the group generated by
  all these extensions (`TDCSG/Compound/Basic.lean`). `TDCSG.GG n₁ n₂ r₁ r₂` is the instance for
  the two disks about ∓1, each with the cyclic group of its rotation; `TDCSG.GG_eq_closure` shows
  it is generated by `permA n₁ r₁` and `permB n₂ r₂` (`TDCSG/Group.lean`).
  `TDCSG.criticalRadius n` is the infimum of the radii with infinite group
  (`TDCSG/CriticalRadius/Basic.lean`).
- **General compound symmetry groups** (`TDCSG/Compound/Basic.lean`). The extension is an
  injective homomorphism (`ballPerm_injective`); the group is monotone in the `H i`
  (`compoundSymmetryGroup_mono`), fixes every point outside the union of the balls and maps the
  union onto itself (`apply_eq_of_notMem_ballUnion`, `image_ballUnion`), so orbits lie in the
  union together with the base point (`orbit_subset_insert_ballUnion`); finitely many balls with
  finitely generated `H i` give a finitely generated group, which is finite as soon as its orbits
  are uniformly bounded (`finite_compoundSymmetryGroup_of_encard_orbit_le`); orbits are monotone
  in the balls (`orbit_subset_orbit_of_eqOn`, `finite_of_forall_orbit_subset`); for finitely
  many pairwise disjoint balls the group is the product of the `H i` (`mulEquivPi`,
  `natCard_compoundSymmetryGroup`).
- **Monotonicity.** Orbits grow with the radii (`orbit_mono`); finiteness of the group passes to
  smaller radii (`finite_GG_of_le`); in `TDCSG/CriticalRadius/Basic.lean`, infinitude passes to
  larger radii (`TDCSG.infinite_GG_of_le`) and the critical radius separates finite from
  infinite groups (`TDCSG.finite_GG_of_lt_criticalRadius`,
  `TDCSG.infinite_GG_of_criticalRadius_lt`).
- **The omitted half of Theorem 1.** If lcm(n₁, n₂) ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}, the orbits are uniformly
  bounded (`exists_encard_orbit_le`) and the group is finite for all radii, equal or unequal
  (`finite_GG`). The bounds are stated as existing; the written proof gives them explicitly.
- **Bounded orbits.** A finitely generated group acting faithfully with uniformly bounded orbits is
  finite (`finite_of_encard_orbit_le`).
- **Boundary contact.** A finite orbit is unchanged for slightly larger radii
  (`exists_orbit_eq_of_finite`); the radii at which a point has an infinite orbit are empty or a
  closed half-line [r₀, ∞) with r₀ > 1 (`infinite_orbit_radii`); at r₀ the orbit touches a boundary
  circle or accumulates on one from inside (`boundary_depth`,
  `touchesBoundary_or_accumulatesInside`, `of_not_touchesBoundary`).
- **Small radii.** For r₁, r₂ ≤ 1 every orbit is finite (`finite_orbit_of_le_one`); for equal
  radii r < 1 the group is finite (`TDCSG.finite_GG_of_lt_one`, `TDCSG/CriticalRadius/Basic.lean`).
- **Disjoint disks.** For r₁ + r₂ < 2 the group is isomorphic to C_{n₁} × C_{n₂} and has order
  n₁n₂ (`nonempty_mulEquiv_of_add_lt_two`, `card_GG_of_add_lt_two`).
- **Infinitude at the closed forms for n = 8, 10, 12.** GG_n(r) is infinite for every r at or
  above √(10−5√2) (n = 8, `infinite_GG_eight`), √(4−φ) (n = 10, `infinite_GG_ten`) and
  √(40−22√3) (n = 12, `infinite_GG_twelve`), through explicit infinite orbits: of 0 for n = 10
  (`infinite_orbit_zero_ten`), and of the lattice point p* for n = 8, 12
  (`mem_orbit_pStar_eight_pow`, `mem_orbit_pStar_twelve_pow`, whose closure data are checked by
  kernel reduction, `decide +kernel`). Hence 1 ≤ r_crit(n) ≤ the closed form
  (`criticalRadius_eight_le`, `criticalRadius_ten_le`, `criticalRadius_twelve_le`,
  `one_le_criticalRadius_eight`, `one_le_criticalRadius_ten`, `one_le_criticalRadius_twelve`).

### Proved modulo a certified computation

GG_n(r) is finite for every r ≤ r_*, r_*² = r_c² − δ, by the certificate theorem (refereed, not in
Lean) applied to a certificate at r_* and group monotonicity. The ordinary cut-set certificates
were generated by `tdcsg-orbit certify` and accepted by `tdcsg-orbit check-certificate`, a separate
float-free checker sharing only the exact arithmetic layer; those at δ = 1/36, 1/47, 1/180 were
regenerated byte for byte and re-checked in an independent verification. The checkers are tested
programs, not kernel-checked proofs. Values are from [`notes/certificates.md`](notes/certificates.md)
and [`notes/relative-certificates.md`](notes/relative-certificates.md) §10, which record every run
and checksum.

| n | r_c (proved in Lean to be an upper bound) | refereed r_* | best certified r_* (δ) | kind of certificate | distance r_c − r_* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | √(10−5√2) ≈ 1.711412 | 1.669154 (δ = 1/7) | 1.705008 (δ = 547/25000 = 0.02188) | symmetry-reduced window-free closure, (C1)–(C4) checked by `check-relative-certificate` (ordinary certificate at δ = 1/36, r_* = 1.703278) | 0.006404 |
| 10 | √(4−φ) ≈ 1.543362 | 1.522979 (δ = 1/16) | 1.536454 (δ = 1/47); 1.536455 (r² < ρ₁, δ ≈ 0.0212735) by the interval of validity | ordinary cut-set certificate, extended by the interval of validity | 0.006908 |
| 12 | √(40−22√3) ≈ 1.376547 | 1.358264 (δ = 1/20) | 1.374528 (δ = 1/180); r² < ρ₁, δ ≈ 0.0055543, by the interval of validity | ordinary cut-set certificate, extended by the interval of validity | 0.002019 |

For n = 5 the value is exact and proved in Lean (above). "Refereed r_*" is the range whose
computation was reproduced during refereeing by an independent implementation. Finiteness
throughout (r_*, r_c) is open. Since r_* > r_A(n), r_A < r_crit holds modulo the same
computations. The best entries rest on arguments proved in `relative-certificates.md` and
refereed correct in
[`referee-report-relative-certificates.md`](notes/proofs/referee-report-relative-certificates.md):

- **n = 8.** The closure stores one circle per orbit of z ↦ ±z, ±z̄; the union of the listed arcs
  and their images is an ordinary certificate because the system is invariant under these
  symmetries (§§2, 7 there). It lies past the level-5 jump δ₅ ≈ 0.0272 of
  `sharp-lower-bound-n8.md`, and gives the base (levels 4 and 5) of its Conjecture S for phases
  θ* ≥ 0.80014 (the note's θ* = 4/5 puts r_5 at δ ≈ 0.0218762, just beyond it). During refereeing
  the file was regenerated byte for byte and accepted again by the relative checker, which is the
  only checker that has read it.
- **n = 10, 12.** `tdcsg-orbit validity-interval` computes the exact radius ρ₁ at which the symbolic
  arcs of the δ = 1/47 and δ = 1/180 certificates stop being valid, so GG_n(r) is finite for every
  r² < ρ₁ (§8 there). The same arcs moved to a radius just below ρ₁ were accepted by
  `check-certificate`. The gain in δ is about 2·10⁻⁴ relative.

**Relative certificates at r_c** (`tdcsg-orbit certify-relative` and
`check-relative-certificate`; `relative-certificates.md` §§5–6). Restricted (window-clipped)
closures at r_c itself, checked float-free from the file alone, including the critical points and
the exact disjointness of the windows: for n = 8 one window shape, for n = 10 two and for n = 12
four. Through the refereed Theorem 4.2 of the combined note (with the general-n bound 1 + 20nF of
`relative-certificates.md` §2, refereed correct) each gives a uniform bound on window-avoiding
chains for all r < r_c, not finiteness of any GG_n(r).

### Open problems and conjectures

- **Prove r_crit(n) equals the closed form for n = 8, 10, 12.** For n = 8 equality follows from
  Conjecture S. For n = 12 it follows from statements about the eight tight points; exact period
  one from levels m₀ ≤ 3 is refuted, and eventual period one through monotone growth is a
  conjecture. n = 10 has no note of its own; its relative certificates at r_c give the window
  reduction through the general-n form of Theorem 4.2.
  [`open-problem-critical-radius.md`](notes/open-problem-critical-radius.md) states the problem
  for specialists, with questions.
- **The paper's footnote 4**: whether the group is infinite at the group-level critical radius. The
  per-point version is proved (above). The group version holds for n = 5 and is open in general.
- **The rank conjecture**: the self-similarity is governed by the unit group of ℤ[ζ_n]⁺.
- **The large-n entries** of the paper's critical-radius table: true critical radii, or the onset
  of very large finite orbits.
- Further conjectures (r_c(n)² ∈ ℚ(ζ_n)⁺ for all n, complex dimensions of the critical fractal) are
  in [`overview.md`](notes/overview.md); open directions are collected in
  [`ideas.md`](notes/ideas.md).

### Findings about the paper

- **Theorem 1, "if" direction.** It needs n₁, n₂ ≥ 2: for n₁ = 1, n₂ = 5 the group is ⟨b⟩ ≅ C_5 for
  every radius although lcm = 5 ∉ {2, 3, 4, 6}. The finiteness direction holds with lcm ∈
  {1, 2, 3, 4, 6} ([`theorem1-converse.md`](notes/proofs/theorem1-converse.md), Remark 8.2).
- **Monotonicity.** The paper's justification ("all group elements that affect it are still
  available") is imprecise: the same word acts differently at different radii, and there is no
  generator-preserving homomorphism between the groups. The conclusion is correct and is proved,
  in writing and in Lean ([`boundary-contact.md`](notes/proofs/boundary-contact.md), Example 3.2,
  Theorems 3.1, 3.4).
- **The closed forms for n = 8 and 12** were derived from the assumption that a limit point lies on
  a disk boundary. Infinitude at those radii is proved without it (in writing and in Lean):
  the limit point is a lattice point p* that lies on ∂D_L exactly at r_c. That these radii are the
  critical radii is open, as for n = 10. The paper's description of segments that "traverse
  angles of π/8" (n = 8) was not reproduced; the approach to p* found here is along a straight line
  ([`critical-radius-n8.md`](notes/proofs/critical-radius-n8.md) §2.6).
- **n = 10.** The paper's estimate 1.543357 lies just below the closed form √(4−φ) ≈ 1.543362,
  inside the open window, consistent with the stated accuracy of the table.
